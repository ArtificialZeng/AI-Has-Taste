## Independent reconstruction

Fix an arbitrary field \(k\). In
\(A=k\times k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\), put

\[
e=(1,0),\qquad f=(0,1),\qquad t=(0,\varepsilon).
\]

Then \(1_A=e+f\), and direct-product multiplication gives

\[
e^2=e,\quad f^2=f,\quad ef=fe=0,\quad
et=te=0,\quad ft=tf=t,\quad t^2=0.
\]

These relations independently verify all decisive points as follows.

1. With \(A_0=ke\oplus kf\), \(A_1=kt\), and \(A_i=0\) for
   \(i\geq2\), one has the direct sum \(A=A_0\oplus A_1\) and
   \(A_iA_j\subseteq A_{i+j}\) in every degree. The unit lies in \(A_0\).
2. The whole algebra is already the vector-space sum of \(A_0\) and \(A_1\),
   so the subalgebra generated by them is \(A\). Thus the grading is standard
   in the frozen sense.
3. The homogeneous dimensions are \(2,1,0,0,\ldots\), proving local
   finiteness.
4. The degree-zero algebra is \(A_0=ke\oplus kf\cong k\times k\), a product
   of fields and hence a semisimple \(k\)-algebra. Thus the grading is
   semiconnected.
5. For \(J=ke\) and \(x=ae+bf+ct\), the displayed relations give
   \(x(de)=(ad)e=(de)x\). Hence \(J\) is a two-sided ideal.
6. In \(A/J\), the classes of \(f,t\) form a basis and satisfy
   \(\bar f^2=\bar f\), \(\bar f\bar t=\bar t\bar f=\bar t\), and
   \(\bar t^2=0\). Therefore
   \(A/J\cong k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\) and
   \(\dim_k(A/J)=2=\dim_k A_0\).
7. The natural map on degree zero is
   \(ae+bf\mapsto b\bar f\). Its kernel is the nonzero subspace
   \(ke=J\cap A_0\). It is consequently not an isomorphism.

The verification is characteristic-free. Zero higher graded pieces cause no
empty-domain or multiplication issue and are permitted by the frozen
hypotheses. The quotient is unital because \(J\) is proper, and the natural map
sends \(e+f\) to the quotient unit \(\bar f\). Thus no convention about unital
maps changes the conclusion.
