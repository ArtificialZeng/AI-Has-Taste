## Positive cases

If (u,d>0), every successive starting level is strictly larger than the
previous one on every input word.  Hence both tie orders give the identity for
((1,1),(1,2),(2,1)).

For (A\in\{U,D\}), let

\[
 I_A(x,y)\iff \exists z\,(x\leq z<y\land A(z)),\qquad x<y,
\]

and define

\[
x\prec_A y\iff
(x<y\land I_A(x,y))\ \lor\ (y<x\land\neg I_A(y,x)).
\]

Positions having the same number of preceding (A)'s form consecutive
plateaux.  The formula orders distinct plateaux from left to right and each
plateau from right to left, so it is a strict total order.  With all positions
selected and labels copied, this is an explicit one-copy first-order (hence
MSO and regular) string transduction (M_A).

For ((1,0)), left ties preserve input order and right ties give (M_U); for
((0,1)), they give respectively the identity and (M_D).  These statements
hold on all words, hence on all Dyck words.

Finally, direct comparison of sorting keys gives, for every word,

\[
 \Phi_{-u,-d}^{t}=\operatorname{rev}\circ\Phi_{u,d}^{\bar t}.
\]

Indeed, negation reverses the order of level blocks, and output reversal also
reverses the order inside each block.  Output reversal and the displayed MSO
maps are regular, and polyregular maps are closed under composition.  This
transports the ten positive pairs above to the negative/nonpositive
representatives and accounts for exactly 20 positive labeled triples.

## Lower-bound mechanism

Each negative argument uses a regular parameter language (a^+\#b^+) and a
deterministic two-way transducer producing a Dyck word (W_{m,n}) of length
(O(m+n)).  If a partial polyregular realization of the level sort existed,
precomposition and restriction to this parameter language would yield a
linear-growth polyregular function.  The linear-growth collapse makes that a
regular/MSO string function, whose inverse image of a regular output language
is regular.  Thus it suffices to obtain either

\[
 \{a^m\#b^n:n\geq m+c\}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \{a^m\#b^n:n\leq m\}.
\]

Both are nonregular by pumping at (a^p\#b^{p+c}) or
(a^p\#b^p), respectively.  Fixed positive lower bounds on the exponents do
not affect the proof.  The three slices below are all generated by finitely
many two-way passes: output the two runs for a mountain on two passes and
output (UD) per letter for a zigzag.

### Weight ((1,-1))

For (P_{m,n}=U^mD^mU^nD^n), level zero contributes (UU).  At every level
where all four runs occur, right ties contribute (DUDU) and left ties
contribute (UDUD).  Checking the boundary level where one pyramid ends gives
exactly

\[
\Phi_{1,-1}^{R}(P_{m,n})=
\begin{cases}
 UU(DU)^{2n-2}DD(UD)^{m-n},&n\leq m,\\
 UU(DU)^{2m-1}DD(UD)^{n-m-1},&n>m,
\end{cases}
\]

and

\[
\Phi_{1,-1}^{L}(P_{m,n})=
\begin{cases}
 UU(UD)^{2m-2}D(UD)^{n-m}D,&m\leq n,\\
 UU(UD)^{2n-2}UDD(UD)^{m-n-1}D,&m>n.
\end{cases}
\]

In the right-tie output, the parity before the first (DD) is even exactly
when (n\leq m), as detected by the regular language
(UU(DU)^{2q}DD(UD)^s).  For left ties and (m,n\geq2), the analogous first
(DD) factorization with (UU(UD)^{2q+1}UDD(UD)^sD) holds exactly when
(m\leq n).  The first (DD) makes both parses unambiguous.  The two pumping
languages above therefore prove non-polyregularity for both ties.

### Weight ((1,-2))

For (Z_{m,n}=(UD)^mU^nD^n), put (r=n-m).  The four starting-level sets are

\[
1-j,\quad 2-j,\quad k-m-1,\quad r-2k+2,
\]

for the zigzag (U,D) and mountain (U,D), respectively.  Reading the top
level blocks from these formulas gives

\[
\Phi_{1,-2}^{L}(Z_{m,n})\text{ ends in }UD\iff r\geq2,
\]

because for (r\geq2) the unique top step is a mountain (D) at level (r)
and the preceding block ends in the last mountain (U); for (r\leq1) the
top zigzag (D), together with the preceding block when needed, forces a
terminal (DD).

For right ties, the top three relevant blocks similarly give

\[
\Phi_{1,-2}^{R}(Z_{m,n})\text{ ends in }UUD\iff r\geq4.
\]

At (r=3) the level-one block ends in the old zigzag (D); at (r=2,1)
the output ends in (DD); and for (r\leq0), the right-tie level-zero block
ends in (DU) before the top zigzag (D).  The regular suffix probes therefore
pull back to (n\geq m+2) and (n\geq m+4).

### Weight ((2,-1))

For (Y_{m,n}=U^mD^m(UD)^n), again put (r=n-m).  The four starting-level
sets are

\[
2(i-1),\quad 2m-i+1,\quad m+j-1,\quad m+j+1.
\]

Their top blocks give

\[
\Phi_{2,-1}^{L}(Y_{m,n})\text{ ends in }DUDD\iff r\geq1.
\]

For (r\geq1), the two top singleton zigzag (D)'s are preceded by a block
ending (DU); at the boundary (r=1), the mountain top supplies the needed
(D).  For (r\leq0), the top mountain levels/ties force incompatible
terminal (D)-blocks, with ((m,n)=(1,1)) checked separately.

For right ties, inspection of five top level blocks gives

\[
\Phi_{2,-1}^{R}(Y_{m,n})\text{ ends in }DUDUDDD\iff r\geq3.
\]

When (r\geq3), the blocks end successively in
(D,UD,UD,D,D).  At (r=2) the boundary block is (UDD); at (r=1) it is
again (UDD); at (r=0), level (2m-1) ends in the earlier mountain (D)
under right ties, so the final three top (D)'s are actually preceded by
another (D).  At (r=-1), the top tie and next level supply at least four
terminal (D)'s except for the explicitly checked smallest case.  For
(r=-2,-3), and (r\leq-4), the decisive top-block endings are respectively
(DDU,DD,D), (DDU,D,D), and (DU,D,D), again incompatible with the target
suffix.  Thus the suffix probe pulls back exactly to (n\geq m+3); the left
probe pulls back to (n\geq m+1).

The negation identity transports these six representative lower bounds (three
weights, two ties) to the other six mixed-sign labeled triples.  Hence all 12
mixed-sign cases are negative.
