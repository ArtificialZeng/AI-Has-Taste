## Reconstruction of the algebra

Put `x=e1`, `y=e2`, and `t=xy-q^2 yx`.  Since `q^3=1` and `q!=1`, one has
`q^{-2}=q` and `1+q+q^2=0`.  The two source relations therefore become

```
x^2 y+x y x+y x^2=0,              y^3 x=x y^3.
```

I checked the proposed iterated Ore presentation from its defining data, not
by specializing the cited center theorem.  Start with `k[z,t]`, adjoin `x`
with `xt=qtx`, and then adjoin `y` using

```
yt=q^2ty+z,       yx=qxy-qt,       yz=zy.
```

The scaling map for the last extension preserves `xt=qtx`.  For the associated
skew derivation, `delta(t)=z` and `delta(x)=-qt`; applying it to either side of
`xt=qtx` gives `qxz-qt^2` (using centrality of `z`).  The relations with `z`
are immediate.  Thus this is a valid iterated Ore extension, with PBW basis
`z^d t^c x^a y^b`, and it is a domain.

I also checked both directions of the identification with the source
presentation.  The first specialized Serre relation is exactly `xt=qtx` after
using the definition of `t`.  In the Ore extension, two applications of the
last two rewrite rules give

```
y^2x=q^2xy^2+qty-qz,
y^3x=xy^3,
```

so the second relation holds.  Conversely, define `z=yt-q^2ty` in the source
algebra.  The first relation gives `xt=qtx`, and direct reduction gives

```
xz=zx,
yz-zy=q^2(xy^3-y^3x)=0.
```

It follows as well that `zt=tz`.  Hence all Ore relations hold in the source
algebra, and the two maps fix `x,y` and recover `t,z`; they are inverse.  This
establishes the PBW/domain input specifically at order three.

## Reconstruction of the full center

The displayed cubic relations give central `x^3` and `y^3`.  The rules
`xt=qtx` and

```
yt^n=q^(2n)t^n y+(1+q^2+...+q^(2(n-1)))zt^(n-1)
```

give central `t^3`; `z` is already central.  The two denominators in the
claimed fifth generator are nonzero because `q` has order three.  Writing

```
s=xyt+xz/(q^2-1)+t^2/(q^4-1)
```

and normal-ordering it as `txy+gamma*zx+beta*t^2`, where
`beta=1/(q-1)` and `gamma=-beta`, I checked its commutators with `t,x,y`.
They vanish using only

```
1/(q^2-1)=-1+q^2/(q^2-1),
beta=-1+q*beta,
gamma=1+q*gamma,
gamma+beta=0.
```

Thus all five asserted generators are central.

For completeness, I checked the exclusion of further central elements.  Give
each PBW generator `z,t,x,y` filtration degree one.  The lower terms `z` in
`yt` and `t` in `yx` disappear in the associated graded algebra.  For a PBW
monomial `t^c x^a y^b z^d`, commutation with `t,x,y` gives exactly

```
a+2b=0,       b-c=0,       a-c=0             (mod 3).
```

These are equivalent to `a=b=c (mod 3)`; `d` is unrestricted.  Distinct PBW
monomials cannot cancel in these commutator equations.  The resulting
nonnegative semigroup is generated by the three coordinate cubes, the free
`z` coordinate, and `(1,1,1)`.  The residue-two case is the square of the last
generator, up to a nonzero quantum scalar.  Hence

```
Z(gr A)=k[x^3,y^3,t^3,z,txy].
```

The leading symbol of the central lift `s` is `txy`.  If `C` is central in
`A`, its leading symbol is central in `gr A`; subtracting a homogeneous
polynomial in `x^3,y^3,t^3,z,s` with that leading symbol strictly lowers the
filtration degree and preserves centrality.  Induction terminates at scalars.
This proves, as a generated subalgebra (without asserting algebraic
independence),

```
Z(A)=k[x^3,y^3,t^3,z,s].
```

This is exactly the center equality in the frozen claim, with `t=e3` and
`s=z1`.

## Reconstruction of the ozone group

Let `phi` fix the center pointwise.  In the original connected grading, the
PBW domain property implies that top degrees add for products.  From
`phi(x)^3=x^3`, it follows that `deg(phi(x))=1`; its scalar term is zero, so
`phi(x)=ax+by`.  The two-sided quotient by `(x)` is exactly `k[y]`, and the
cube equality there forces `b=0` and then `a^3=1`.  Applying the same argument
to `phi(y)^3=y^3` and the quotient by `(y)`, which is `k[x]`, gives

```
phi(x)=ax,       phi(y)=by,       a^3=b^3=1.
```

The definitions then give `phi(t)=ab*t` and `phi(z)=ab^2*z`.  Since the
nonzero central PBW element `z` is fixed, `ab^2=1`.  Every term of `s` scales
by `a^2b^2`, and its leading term is nonzero, so fixation of `s` gives
`a^2b^2=1`.  Dividing these equations yields `a=1`; then `b^2=b^3=1`, hence
`b=1`.  Therefore every center-fixing automorphism is the identity.  The
identity fixes the full center, so `Aut_{Z(A)-alg}(A)={id_A}`.

The quotient, degree, nonzero-element, and equality-case steps above do not
use algebraic closure beyond the frozen hypotheses, and no limiting,
localization, or division-by-a-possibly-zero step occurs.
