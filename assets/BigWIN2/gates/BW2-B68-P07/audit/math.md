## Reconstruction of the decisive argument

For subgroups \(H,L\leq S_n\), the diagonal action on
\(S_n/H\times S_n/L\) has orbits indexed by \(H\backslash S_n/L\). The orbit
represented by \((H,rL)\) has stabilizer
\(H\cap rLr^{-1}\). Hence
\[
 (X^n/H)\times(X^n/L)
 \cong\coprod_{r\in H\backslash S_n/L}
 X^n/(H\cap rLr^{-1}),
\]
and \(|HrL|=|H||L|/|H\cap rLr^{-1}|\). This follows directly from coset
equalities, so there is no unverified appeal to a converse involving cycle
indices.

For the target pair,
\[
H=\{e,(12),(34),(12)(34)\},\qquad
L=\{e,(1234),(13)(24),(1432)\}.
\]
Thus \(H\cap L=\{e\}\). With \(\pi=(23)\), conjugating the generator gives
\((1324)\), whose square is \((12)(34)\); its two elements of order four do
not lie in \(H\). Therefore
\[
H\cap\pi L\pi^{-1}=\{e,(12)(34)\}.
\]
The corresponding double cosets have sizes 16 and 8. They are distinct and
their sizes sum to 24, so they exhaust \(S_4\). Consequently
\[
\mathbf K_{(2,2)}\times\mathbf K_{(4)}
\cong \mathbf K_{(1,1,1,1)}\;\coprod\;
X^4/\langle(12)(34)\rangle.
\]
The second stabilizer is not conjugate to an independent-row row group: the
orders of \(G_{(4)},G_{(3,1)},G_{(2,2)},G_{(2,1,1)},G_{(1^4)}\) are
respectively \(4,3,4,2,1\), and the sole order-two row group is generated by
a transposition, not a double transposition.

Direct averaging over the five row groups gives, in power-sum notation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\mathbf K_4}&=(p_{1^4}+p_{22}+2p_4)/4,\\
Z_{\mathbf K_{31}}&=(p_{1^4}+2p_{31})/3,\\
Z_{\mathbf K_{22}}&=(p_{1^4}+2p_{211}+p_{22})/4,\\
Z_{\mathbf K_{211}}&=(p_{1^4}+p_{211})/2,\\
Z_{\mathbf K_{1^4}}&=p_{1^4}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The coefficient matrix displayed in the evidence has determinant \(1/24\),
which I independently obtain by its triangular pivots
\(1/2,2/3,1/4,1/2,1\). Thus the five cycle indices are linearly independent.
Using \(p_\lambda\star p_\mu=\delta_{\lambda\mu}z_\lambda p_\lambda\),
the only common types are \(1^4\) and \(22\), and
\[
Z_{\mathbf K_{22}}\star Z_{\mathbf K_4}
=\tfrac32p_{1^4}+\tfrac12p_{22}
=2Z_{\mathbf K_{22}}-2Z_{\mathbf K_{211}}+2Z_{\mathbf K_{1^4}}.
\]
Linear independence makes these coefficients unique. Any species coproduct
with nonnegative multiplicities would induce a nonnegative cycle-index
expansion, contradicting the forced coefficient \(-2\). This proves the
species-level nonclosure claimed.

For the minimal-degree assertion I independently checked the complete
unordered product tables. In degree 1 the only product is \(K_1\). In degree
2 the products are \(K_2\), \(K_{11}\), and \(2K_{11}\) for
\((2,2),(2,11),(11,11)\), respectively. In degree 3, writing
\(A=G_3\), \(B=G_{21}\), and \(T=1\), the six products are
\[
2K_3,\ K_{111},\ 2K_{111},\ K_{21}+K_{111},\
3K_{111},\ 6K_{111}
\]
for \((A,A),(A,B),(A,T),(B,B),(B,T),(T,T)\). These follow from the same
double-coset lemma: \(A=A_3\) is normal of index two; every conjugate of
\(B\) meets \(A\) trivially; and \(B\backslash S_3/B\) has one orbit with
stabilizer \(B\) and one with trivial stabilizer. This exhausts every
partition pair in degrees below four and gives actual species decompositions,
not merely symmetric-function identities.
