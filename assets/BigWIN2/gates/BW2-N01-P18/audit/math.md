## Reconstructed decisive argument

### 1. Constant terms of nonnegative monomials

Put a_j=(p^j-1)/(p-1), j>=1, and Y(z)=sum_j z^(a_j). The identity
a_(j+1)=p a_j+1 and Frobenius imply Y=z(1+Y^p), with leading term z.
Thus Y is the compositional inverse of f(x)=x/(1+x^p).

Formal residue substitution gives, for r,n>=0,

    [z^n]Y^r
      = Res_x x^r f(x)^(-n-1) f'(x) dx
      = [x^(n-r)](1+x^p)^n
      = [x^0]P^n x^r.

In characteristic p, f'(x)=(1+x^p)^(-1); this is the precise factor needed
in the middle equality. In particular Phi(x^r)=Y^r, where
Phi(Q)=sum_(n>=0) [x^0]P^n Q z^n, and Phi(1)=1.

The characteristic-p residue justification is valid. For a substitution with
linear coefficient 1 and a Laurent series bounded below, each residue depends
on finitely many coefficients. Lift those coefficients to integers. Over Q,
the residue of f^m f' is zero for m!=-1 by differentiation, and the residue
of f'/f is one. Both residues themselves are integral expressions, so their
equality reduces modulo p. The proof never divides by m+1, n, or r inside
F_p. Compatibility of differentiation and substitution with reduction is
coefficientwise. No analytic convergence or exchange of infinite limits is
being assumed.

### 2. Laurent reduction and the entire zero kernel

In R=K[t,X]/(X^p-tX+1), X has inverse t-X^(p-1). Sending X to x and t to P
therefore gives an isomorphism R -> K[x,x^(-1)], with the inverse stated
in the frozen proof. Both compositions fix the ring generators. Division
by the monic polynomial in X proves freeness over K[t], so every Q has a
unique expression sum_(r=0)^(p-1) f_r(P)x^r. This argument includes every
negative Laurent exponent, not just those covered by the computations.

Index shifting gives Phi(PQ)=(Phi(Q)-[x^0]Q)/z. On polynomial images this
is the operator

    L(H)=(H(y)-H(0))(y^(-1)+y^(p-1)).

It is polynomial because the first factor is divisible by y. It kills
constants, and sends a nonconstant degree-d polynomial to one with the same
leading coefficient and degree d+p-1. Consequently Phi maps every Laurent
polynomial to a polynomial in Y. The free K-basis P^k x^r has image zero
for r=0,k>=1. Its other images are 1 and L^k(y^r), 1<=r<=p-1,k>=0,
which are monic of pairwise distinct degrees 0 and r+k(p-1). Those degrees
are exactly all the nonnegative integers, proving independence and spanning.

Substitution of Y in K[y] is injective: the least nonzero degree of H is
also the least nonzero degree of H(Y), with the same coefficient. Thus the
kernel is exactly the K-span of P,P^2,..., which equals P K[P]. There is
no assertion that Phi is multiplicative. This proves the full algebraic
classification rather than merely a sufficient condition for a zero word.

### 3. A support constraint that survives cancellations

If H is nonzero of degree D and [z^n]H(Y) is nonzero, at least one monomial
Y^r, 0<=r<=D, contributes at n. For r>0, some ordered r-tuple of exponents
a_j sums to n, so (p-1)n+r is a sum of r powers of p. Carrying yields
s_p((p-1)n+r)<=r<=D. For r=0, only n=0 occurs, and the same bound applies.
Each coefficient involves finitely many tuples. Cancellation can remove
indices from this union of supports, but cannot introduce new indices.

It follows that the support is contained in

    S_(p,D)={n>=0: s_p((p-1)n+r)<=D for some integer 0<=r<=D}.

The offset r and the multiplier p-1 are both essential and have been
retained throughout the argument.

### 4. What ordinary recurrence forces

Suppose a recurrent word has v_a=c!=0. Repeatedly return to the prefix ending
at a+T_m, where T_m is the sum of the previously chosen return positions.
Recurrence allows the new starting position t_(m+1) to be positive and
larger than T_m. Copying that whole prefix preserves the value c at every
old position a+s and copies it to a+s+t_(m+1). Induction gives

    v_(a+s)=c for all s in FS_0(t_1,t_2,...).

Here FS_0 consists of the finite subset sums and includes zero. This uses
only arbitrarily late prefix occurrences. It introduces no bound on return
gaps and makes no assumption of uniform recurrence. Every finite subset
appears at a finite stage of the induction.

### 5. Exclusion of every fixed translate of finite subset sums

For any positive integer sequence (t_i), every unused tail contains a
positive consecutive block sum divisible by p^K: two among p^K+1 partial
sums coincide modulo p^K. Choose K_1 with p^K_1>(p-1)a+D, and choose such
a block b_1. After b_i, choose K_(i+1) with p^K_(i+1)>(p-1)b_i and select
b_(i+1) in a tail after the previously used indices. Continue through b_(D+1).

These are disjoint finite sets of generators. Divisibility puts the digits
of (p-1)b_i at positions at least K_i, while the next strict inequality
puts them below K_(i+1). Positivity also ensures K_(i+1)>K_i, even for
p=2. For every offset 0<=r<=D, the digits of (p-1)a+r lie below K_1.
Thus there are no carries between any of these summands. The same index
n=a+sum_i b_i satisfies, simultaneously for all the allowed offsets,

    s_p((p-1)n+r)
      = s_p((p-1)a+r) + sum_i s_p((p-1)b_i)
      >= D+1.

Every positive block contributes at least one digit. Hence this n lies
in a+FS_0(t_i) but outside S_(p,D). This contradiction with Steps 3 and 4
excludes recurrence of every nonzero H(Y). The zero word is recurrent,
and Step 2 identifies all Q producing it. No part of this implication
depends on a finite search succeeding.
