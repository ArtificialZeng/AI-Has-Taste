# Exact counterexample certificate

## Statement

For every field \(k\), the unrestricted assertion in `source.md` is false. Let

\[
R=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),\qquad A=k\times R.
\]

Write an element of \(A\) uniquely as a triple \((a,b,c)\), meaning
\((a,b+c\varepsilon)\). Multiplication is

\[
(a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(aa',\,bb',\,bc'+cb'). \tag{1}
\]

Define

\[
A_0=\{(a,b,0):a,b\in k\},\qquad
A_1=\{(0,0,c):c\in k\},\qquad A_i=0\quad(i\ge2),
\]

and define the two-sided ideal

\[
J=\{(a,0,0):a\in k\}=k\times0.
\]

## Verification of every hypothesis

1. **Algebra and grading.** The direct-product algebra \(A=k\times R\) is an
   associative unital \(k\)-algebra, with unit \((1,1,0)\in A_0\). Every triple
   decomposes uniquely as \((a,b,0)+(0,0,c)\), so
   \(A=A_0\oplus A_1\). Formula (1) gives
   \(A_0A_0\subseteq A_0\), \(A_0A_1,A_1A_0\subseteq A_1\), and
   \(A_1A_1=0\subseteq A_2=0\). Products involving an \(A_i\) with \(i\ge2\)
   vanish. Hence this is a nonnegative grading.

2. **Standardness.** Since \(A=A_0\oplus A_1\) as a vector space, the
   \(k\)-subalgebra generated by \(A_0\cup A_1\) contains every element of
   \(A\). Thus the grading is standard.

3. **Local finiteness.** The homogeneous dimensions are
   \(\dim_k A_0=2\), \(\dim_k A_1=1\), and \(\dim_k A_i=0\) for \(i\ge2\).

4. **Semiconnectedness.** The grading is nonnegative, and
   \(A_0\cong k\times k\) is a finite product of fields, hence a semisimple
   \(k\)-algebra. No property of \(k\) beyond being a field is used.

5. **Two-sided ideal closure.** For \(x=(a,b,c)\in A\) and
   \(j=(d,0,0)\in J\), formula (1) gives
   \(xj=(ad,0,0)\in J\) and \(jx=(da,0,0)\in J\). Additive and scalar closure
   are immediate, so \(J\lhd A\).

6. **Required dimension equality.** The map
   \[
   \pi:A\longrightarrow R,\qquad (a,b,c)\longmapsto b+c\varepsilon
   \]
   is a surjective unital \(k\)-algebra homomorphism with kernel \(J\).
   Therefore
   \[
   A/J\cong R,\qquad \dim_k(A/J)=2=\dim_k(A_0).
   \]

7. **Failure of splitness.** Under the preceding quotient identification, the
   natural composite \(\theta_J:A_0\to A/J\) is
   \[
   \theta_J(a,b,0)=b\in R.
   \]
   Consequently
   \[
   \ker\theta_J=\{(a,0,0):a\in k\}=J\cap A_0\ne0;
   \]
   in particular \((1,0,0)\) lies in its kernel. Thus \(\theta_J\) is not
   injective and hence is not an isomorphism (indeed, its image is the
   one-dimensional constant subalgebra of the two-dimensional algebra \(R\)).

This checks precisely the hypotheses frozen in `problem.md` and disproves the
universal assertion in `source.md`. It makes no minimality claim and does not
add indecomposability, faithfulness, or tangent-dimension assumptions.
