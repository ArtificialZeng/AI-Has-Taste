# Exact invariants for the cube-of-broom closed-neighborhood ideal

All polynomial rings below have the standard grading, and the field `K` is
arbitrary.  Put (X=x_1cdots x_n) and
(Y[a,b]=y_acdots y_b).

## 1. Minimal generators

Distances in the defining broom give


\[
q_{x_i}=X Y[1,\min(m,3)]
\]

for every leaf, and

\[
q_{y_j}=\begin{cases}
X Y[1,\min(m,j+3)],&1\leq j\leq3,\\
Y[j-3,\min(m,j+3)],&4\leq j\leq m.
\end{cases}
\]

If (m=2,3), the graph cube is complete, so (I_{n,m}) is the
principal ideal generated by (XY[1,m]).  If (m\geq4), deleting duplicates
and divisible monomials gives

\[
I_{n,m}=\bigl(XY[1,3],\;Y[a,a+6]\ (1\leq a\leq m-7),\;Y[m-3,m]\bigr), \tag{1}
\]

where the middle list is empty for (m\leq7).  Indeed, (XY[1,3])
divides the three generators centered at (y_1,y_2,y_3), and the last
four-variable monomial divides all the other right-truncated balls as well as
the seven-variable ball ending at (y_m).  The remaining displayed supports
do not contain one another.  Thus (1) is the inclusion-minimal generating set.

## 2. A minimal mapping-cone lemma

We use the following elementary graded lemma three times.

**Lemma.**  Let (R=K[u,z_1,\ldots,z_s]), let (A) be a monomial ideal
not involving (u), and let (f=u v), where (v) is a monomial in the
(z)'s.  Then the standard exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow R/(A:v)(-\deg f)
 \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\cdot f} R/A
 \longrightarrow R/(A+(f))\longrightarrow0 \tag{2}
\]

has a minimal mapping-cone resolution.  Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{pd}R/(A+(f))
 &=\max\{\operatorname{pd}R/A,\operatorname{pd}R/(A:v)+1\},\\
\operatorname{reg}R/(A+(f))
 &=\max\{\operatorname{reg}R/A,\operatorname{reg}R/(A:v)+\deg f-1\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]

The same statement holds if (u) is replaced by a product of new variables.

**Proof.**  Extend minimal multigraded resolutions of (A) and (A:v)
from (K[z_1,\ldots,z_s]) to (R).  In every homological degree, every
entry of a homogeneous comparison map lifting multiplication by (f) has
positive (u)-degree, hence lies in the homogeneous maximal ideal.  The
other two differentials are already minimal.  The cone is therefore minimal,
and its free module in degree (i) is the direct sum of the degree-(i)
module for (R/A) and the shifted degree-((i-1)) module for (R/(A:v)).
This proves (3).  The multidegree argument is unchanged for several new
variables.  (square)

## 3. Two interval ideals

For (t\geq0), define an ideal (L_t\subseteq K[z_1,\ldots,z_t]) by

\[
L_t=\begin{cases}
0,&0\leq t\leq3,\\
(z_{t-3}z_{t-2}z_{t-1}z_t),&4\leq t\leq7,\\
(z_a\cdots z_{a+6}:1\leq a\leq t-7)
 +(z_{t-3}z_{t-2}z_{t-1}z_t),&t\geq8.
\end{cases} \tag{4}
\]

Write (p_t=\operatorname{pd}(K[z_1,\ldots,z_t]/L_t)) and
(r_t=\operatorname{reg}(K[z_1,\ldots,z_t]/L_t)).  For (t\geq8), regard
(L_{t-1}) as an ideal in (K[z_2,\ldots,z_t]).  Direct monomial division
gives

\[
L_t=(z_1\cdots z_7)+L_{t-1},\qquad
(L_{t-1}:z_1\cdots z_7)=(z_8)+L_{t-8}, \tag{5}
\]

where (L_{t-8}) uses (z_9,\ldots,z_t).  To see the colon identity, the
first surviving seven-window divided by (z_2\cdots z_7) gives (z_8);
all windows through the one beginning at (z_8) then become multiples of
(z_8), while the windows beginning at (z_9) and the terminal four-window
are exactly (L_{t-8}).  The same statement covers (8\leq t\leq11), when
the terminal quotient is a multiple of (z_8) and (L_{t-8}=0).

The quotient by the right side of the colon in (5) has projective dimension
(p_{t-8}+1) and regularity (r_{t-8}).  The lemma, with the new variable
(z_1), therefore yields

\[
p_t=\max(p_{t-1},p_{t-8}+2),\qquad
r_t=\max(r_{t-1},r_{t-8}+6). \tag{6}
\]

The base values are ((p_t,r_t)=(0,0)) for (0\leq t\leq3) and
((p_t,r_t)=(1,3)) for (4\leq t\leq7).  Induction in (6) now gives

\[
p_t=\left\lfloor\frac t4\right\rfloor,
\qquad r_t=3\left\lfloor\frac t4\right\rfloor \quad(t\geq0). \tag{7}
\]

Indeed, the second entry in each maximum in (6) is respectively
(lfloor t/4\rfloor) and (3\lfloor t/4\rfloor), while the first is no
larger.

Next let (Q_t\subseteq K[z_1,\ldots,z_t]), for (t\geq1), be the ideal
whose minimal supports are the closed radius-three balls in a path of length
(t).  Explicitly, (Q_t=(z_1\cdots z_t)) for (1\leq t\leq4), and for
(t\geq5),

\[
Q_t=(z_1z_2z_3z_4,
 z_a\cdots z_{a+6}\ (2\leq a\leq t-7),
 z_{t-3}z_{t-2}z_{t-1}z_t), \tag{8}
\]

with the middle list empty when (t\leq8).  For (t\geq5), again shifting
the variables of (L_{t-1}),

\[
Q_t=(z_1z_2z_3z_4)+L_{t-1},\qquad
(L_{t-1}:z_1z_2z_3z_4)=Q_{t-4}, \tag{9}
\]

where (Q_{t-4}) uses (z_5,\ldots,z_t).  For (5\leq t\leq8) this is
an immediate division of the terminal four-window.  In general, division of
the first seven-window gives the left four-window (z_5z_6z_7z_8); the next
three quotients are its multiples; the remaining seven-windows and terminal
four-window are precisely those in (8) on (z_5,\ldots,z_t).

Put (a_t=\operatorname{pd}(K[z_1,\ldots,z_t]/Q_t)) and
(b_t=\operatorname{reg}(K[z_1,\ldots,z_t]/Q_t)).  Equations (3), (7), and
(9) give, for (t\geq5),

\[
a_t=\max(p_{t-1},a_{t-4}+1),\qquad
b_t=\max(r_{t-1},b_{t-4}+3). \tag{10}
\]

The principal base cases (1\leq t\leq4) have (a_t=1) and (b_t=t-1).
Induction in (10) gives

\[
a_t=\left\lceil\frac t4\right\rceil,
\qquad b_t=t-\left\lceil\frac t4\right\rceil \quad(t\geq1). \tag{11}
\]

For the first formula, the second term in (10) is
(lceil t/4\rceil) and the first is one smaller.  For the second formula,
the second term is (t-lceil t/4\rceil), while
(3\lfloor(t-1)/4\rfloor) is no larger (with equality only in one residue
class).

## 4. Projective dimension and regularity of the broom ideal

For (m\geq4), equation (1) says

\[
I_{n,m}=(XY[1,3])+L_m, \tag{12}
\]

where (L_m\subset K[y_1,\ldots,y_m]).  Dividing its monomial generators
by (Y[1,3]) and minimizing gives the third key identity

\[
(L_m:Y[1,3])=Q_{m-3} \tag{13}
\]

on (y_4,\ldots,y_m).  For (4\leq m\leq7), this says simply that the
terminal four-window divides to the full product on (m-3) variables.  For
(m\geq8), the first seven-window divides to the left four-window
(Y[4,7]); the next three quotients are its multiples; the windows beginning
at (y_5), together with the terminal four-window, give the rest of
(Q_{m-3}).

Apply the lemma to (12), using any (x_i) as the new variable.  Since
(deg(XY[1,3])=n+3), equations (7), (11), and (13) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{pd} S/I_{n,m}
 &=\max\left\{\left\lfloor\frac m4\right\rfloor,
 \left\lceil\frac{m-3}{4}\right\rceil+1\right\}
 =\left\lfloor\frac m4\right\rfloor+1,\\
\operatorname{reg} S/I_{n,m}
 &=\max\left\{3\left\lfloor\frac m4\right\rfloor,
 n+2+(m-3)-\left\lceil\frac{m-3}{4}\right\rceil\right\}\\
 &=n+m-1-\left\lfloor\frac m4\right\rfloor .
\end{aligned} \tag{14}
\]

Here (lceil(m-3)/4\rceil=\lfloor m/4\rfloor).  If
(m=4c+s), (0\leq s\leq3), the second regularity term exceeds the first
by (n+s-1>0).  For (m=2,3), the principal generator has degree (n+m),
so (14) remains valid.  Thus there are no exceptional small-(m) values in
the two closed formulas.

## 5. Height

Because (1) is squarefree, the height is the minimum size of a vertex cover
of its displayed supports.  We prove

\[
\operatorname{ht}(I_{n,m})=\left\lfloor\frac m7\right\rfloor+1. \tag{15}
\]

The assertion is clear for (m=2,3).  For (4\leq m\leq6), the single
variable (y_{m-3}) meets both generators in (1), and the nonzero ideal has
height at least one.

Now let (m\geq7), and write (q=\lfloor m/7\rfloor\).  The following
(q+1) generator supports are pairwise disjoint:

\[
\operatorname{supp}(XY[1,3]),\quad
\operatorname{supp}(Y[4+7i,10+7i])\ (0\leq i\leq q-2),\quad
\operatorname{supp}(Y[m-3,m]). \tag{16}
\]

The middle list is empty for (q=1).  Its last interval ends at (7q-4),
whereas the terminal interval begins at (7q+(m-7q)-3\geq7q-3); all the
listed seven-windows occur in (1).  Hence every vertex cover has at least
(q+1) elements.

Conversely, the (q+1) vertices

\[
\{y_{3+7i}:0\leq i\leq q-1\}\ \cup\ \{y_{m-3}\} \tag{17}
\]

meet every generator.  The first vertex meets the leaf-block generator, the
last meets the terminal generator, and consecutive selected indices differ
by seven except for the last gap, which is (m-7q+1\leq7).  Therefore no
interval of seven consecutive (y)-vertices can avoid (17).  This proves
(15), including every residue class and endpoint.

## 6. Cohen--Macaulay classification

Auslander--Buchsbaum gives Cohen--Macaulayness exactly when
(operatorname{pd}S/I_{n,m}=\operatorname{ht}(I_{n,m})).  By (14)--(15)
this is

\[
\left\lfloor\frac m4\right\rfloor
=\left\lfloor\frac m7\right\rfloor. \tag{18}
\]

For common floor value zero and (m\geq2), (18) gives (m=2,3).  For
common value one, the intervals ([4,7]) and ([7,13]) meet only at (m=7).
For a common value (k\geq2), the intervals ([4k,4k+3]) and
([7k,7k+6]) are disjoint because (7k>4k+3).  Hence

\[
S/I_{n,m}\text{ is Cohen--Macaulay exactly for }m\in\{2,3,7\},
\quad n\geq2. \tag{19}
\]

Every argument above is monomial and characteristic-free.

## 7. Exact finite verification (not used as proof)

`evidence/compute_exact_table.py` independently reconstructs every closed
distance-three ball by breadth-first search, prunes supports by divisibility,
and invokes Singular 4.4.1 `mres(I,0)` over characteristic zero and
characteristic two.  It also checks height by exhaustive hitting-set search.
The generated `evidence/exact_table.json` covers (n=2,3) and
(2\leq m\leq24), contains all minimal Betti tables, and records no generator,
height, characteristic, (n)-dependence, or formula discrepancy.  These are
exact finite checks of the derivation and boundary bookkeeping, not a proof of
the quantified theorem.
