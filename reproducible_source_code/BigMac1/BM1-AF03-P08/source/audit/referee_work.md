# Adversarial referee reconstruction and verifier specification

Date: 2026-08-29 (Asia/Shanghai)  
Role: adversarial referee, independent of the breaker route  
Scope: the source archive
`literature/original/arxiv-1312.2170v1/q-Fibonacci-s.tex`, the literal user
statement, and the mathematical definitions below. I did not read or use any
breaker output.

## 1. Referee verdict at this stage

The proposed computational route can certify the \(n=10\) instance, but only
after two essential repairs.

1. The phrase "divide out the maximum power of \(q\)" must be made
   unambiguous. The conjectural exponent \(g\) is the *degree* of the
   \(\widetilde R\)-polynomial, whereas the greatest common monomial divisor
   is determined by its *valuation*. They are generally different.
2. Factoring over \(\mathbb Z[x]\) and requiring every irreducible factor to
   be a \(q\)-Fibonacci polynomial is not an equivalent membership test. A
   counterexample claim requires a complete, exact test for membership in the
   multiplicative monoid generated by all \(F_h\), not a failed greedy or
   irreducible-factor test.

These are fatal for any counterexample produced by the route as presently
worded. They are repairable by the finite exact monoid test in Section 6.

At the snapshot inspected, `problem/formal_statement.md` contains headings
only. It therefore does not yet meet the mandatory formal-statement gate.

## 2. What the original paper actually states

The source defines right descents and the subword formulation of Bruhat order
at source lines 155--173. Its ordinary \(R\)-recurrence, including the
non-comparable and equality cases, is at lines 180--194. The displayed
\(\widetilde R\)-recurrence is at lines 206--216, and the exact change of
variables between \(R\) and \(\widetilde R\) is at lines 218--223.

The paper defines

\[
 F_0(x)=F_1(x)=1,\qquad
 F_h(x)=F_{h-1}(x)+xF_{h-2}(x)\quad(h\ge2)
\]

at lines 288--290. Its proved endpoint case is

\[
 \widetilde R_{e,v_n}(q)=q^{2n-4}F_{n-2}(q^{-2})
\]

at lines 291--294. The conjecture is printed at lines 780--792 and says that,
for \(n\ge2\) and \(e\le \sigma_1\le\sigma_2\le\Omega_n\), the polynomial is
a power of \(q\) times a product of \(F_h(q^{-2})\). The same paragraph reports
verification for \(n\le9\).

There is a harmless but real type issue in the printed conjecture:
\(\Omega_n=s_2s_1s_3s_2\cdots s_{n-1}s_{n-2}\) is a reduced *word*, while
Bruhat order compares group elements. The paper earlier proves that this word
represents \(v_n=34\cdots n12\). Thus the intended endpoint is
\(\sigma_2\le v_n\), not literally a comparison with a word.

The source says that the \(h_i\) are integers, but \(F_h\) was defined only
for \(h\ge0\). A well-formed repair must explicitly impose \(h_i\in
\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). The source gives no additional restriction such as
\(h_i\le n\). A checker that silently imposes \(h_i\le10\) is not testing the
printed conjecture.

## 3. Formal \(n=10\) endpoint reconstructed from definitions

Use one-line notation for \(S_{10}\), function composition on the right, and
let \(s_i=(i,i+1)\); consequently, right multiplication by \(s_i\) swaps
positions \(i\) and \(i+1\). Put

\[
 v_{10}=(3,4,5,6,7,8,9,10,1,2).
\]

The precise finite endpoint is:

> For every \(u,v\in S_{10}\) with \(u\le v\le v_{10}\) in (strong) Bruhat
> order, there exist \(k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\),
> \(g\in\mathbb Z\), and \(h_1,\ldots,h_k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) such that the
> following equality holds in \(\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]\):
> \[
> \widetilde R_{u,v}(q)=q^g\prod_{i=1}^kF_{h_i}(q^{-2}).
> \]
> The left side in fact belongs to \(\mathbb Z[q]\), so the right side must
> have no negative exponent.

Here the empty product is \(1\). Allowing \(k=0\) is equivalent to requiring a
positive number of factors, because \(F_0=F_1=1\). For a canonical certificate
all unit factors \(F_0,F_1\) should be deleted and the remaining indices sorted.
This loses no solutions and avoids infinitely many encodings of the same one.

The equality endpoint \(u=v\) must be included:
\(\widetilde R_{u,u}=1\), represented canonically by \(g=0,k=0\). The case
\(u\nleq v\) is not in the quantified conjecture, but the value \(0\) is an
essential recurrence base case because recursive subcalls can be
non-comparable.

## 4. Exact Bruhat and recurrence conventions

For \(w\in S_n\), set

\[
 \ell(w)=\#\{(i,j):1\le i<j\le n,\ w(i)>w(j)\}
\]

and

\[
 r_w(p,a)=\#\{i\le p:w(i)\le a\}.
\]

The rank-matrix test to use is

\[
 u\le v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 r_u(p,a)\ge r_v(p,a)
 \quad(1\le p,a\le n).
\]

This direction of the inequality must have unit tests, for example
\(123\le213\). A reversed inequality silently enumerates the opposite order.
The verifier must also check permutations, rather than assuming that arrays
with the right length are permutations.

Define (D_R(w)=\{s_i:w(i)>w(i+1)\}\). The complete deterministic recurrence
for a polynomial-coefficient dictionary is

\[
 \widetilde R_{u,v}=0\ (u\nleq v),\qquad
 \widetilde R_{u,u}=1,
\]

and, for \(u<v\), choose a specified \(s\in D_R(v)\) (for reproducibility,
say the least index) and use

\[
 \widetilde R_{u,v}=
 \begin{cases}
 \widetilde R_{us,vs},&s\in D_R(u),\\
 \widetilde R_{us,vs}+q\widetilde R_{u,vs},&s\notin D_R(u).
 \end{cases}
\]

In the second branch, \(us\nleq vs\) can occur; that term is then exactly
zero. Omitting the zero base case produces wrong values even in \(S_2\): for
\(u=e,v=s_1\), the correct answer is \(q\).

Memoization keys must contain the full ordered pair \((u,v)\). Selecting a
different right descent should give the same result; small-rank tests should
compute all available choices as a consistency check rather than building
choice-independence into the trusted input.

## 5. Degree, parity, and the two different normalizations

Let \(P(q)=\widetilde R_{u,v}(q)\) and
\(d=\ell(v)-\ell(u)\). Induction in the recurrence gives:

* \(P\) is monic of degree \(d\);
* every exponent in its support is congruent to \(d\pmod2\);
* \(P\ne0\) for \(u\le v\).

If the conjectural formula holds, every \(F_h(x)\) has constant term \(1\), so
the largest \(q\)-exponent on the right is \(g\), with coefficient \(1\).
Consequently

\[
 \boxed{g=d=\deg P}.
\]

Thus \(g\) is not an independent search parameter.

There are two valid but different normalizations.

**Top-degree/Laurent normalization.** Define

\[
 H_{u,v}(x)=\sum_{j\ge0}[q^{d-2j}]P(q)\,x^j,
\]

stopping at the lowest nonzero exponent. Then

\[
 P(q)=q^dH_{u,v}(q^{-2}),
\]

and the conjecture is exactly

\[
 H_{u,v}(x)\in\langle F_h(x):h\ge2\rangle_{\rm mult}.
\]

Writing \(q^{-d}P(q)\) and then factoring it as though it were an ordinary
polynomial in \(q\) is invalid: it is a Laurent polynomial, and the intended
ordinary variable is \(x=q^{-2}\).

**Common-monomial/valuation normalization.** Let
\(m=\min\{r:[q^r]P\ne0\}\), \(D=(d-m)/2\), and \(t=q^2\). Then

\[
 q^{-m}P(q)=B_{u,v}(t),\qquad
 B_{u,v}(t)=\prod_i \widehat F_{h_i}(t),
\]

where

\[
 \widehat F_h(t)=t^{\lfloor h/2\rfloor}F_h(t^{-1})
\]

is the reciprocal \(q\)-Fibonacci polynomial. Therefore, if "divide out the
maximum power of \(q\)" means divide by the greatest common monomial \(q^m\),
the factors to recognize are the reciprocal family \(\widehat F_h\), not
the original \(F_h\).

## 6. Why irreducible-factor recognition is not sufficient

The first nontrivial obstruction already occurs in the proved range:

\[
 F_5(x)=1+4x+3x^2=(1+x)(1+3x).
\]

The irreducible factor \(1+3x\) is not any \(F_h(x)\). Nevertheless \(F_5\)
is, tautologically, an allowed single factor. In particular the paper's proved
formula at \(n=7\) gives

\[
 \widetilde R_{e,v_7}(q)
 =q^{10}F_5(q^{-2})
 =q^{10}+4q^8+3q^6.
\]

Dividing by the valuation gives
\(q^4+4q^2+3=(q^2+1)(q^2+3)\). A test demanding that each irreducible factor
it sees be a (reciprocal) Fibonacci polynomial would falsely call this known
case a counterexample.

Exact factorization is useful as an accelerator, but the correct decision
problem is multiplicative-monoid membership. A complete verifier can solve it
without trusting a CAS factorization:

1. Construct \(H(x)\) exactly from \(P(q)\). Reject immediately if \(P\) is
   not monic of degree \(d\) or has a wrong-parity exponent.
2. Put \(D=\deg H\). Unit factors \(F_0,F_1\) are irrelevant. In any
   nonunit representation,
   \(\deg F_h=\lfloor h/2\rfloor\le D\); hence it is enough to try the finite
   set \(2\le h\le2D+1\). For \(n=10\), \(d\le16\), so \(D\le8\) and
   \(h\le17\). This is a proof-derived bound, not an extra conjectural
   restriction.
3. Run memoized backtracking on the exact integer polynomial \(Q\), initially
   \(H\). Try indices in nondecreasing order. A branch exists only when exact
   division \(Q/F_h\) has zero remainder in \(\mathbb Z[x]\); recurse on the
   quotient. Accept exactly at \(Q=1\).
4. Do not use greedy division. Shared or nested factors can make a locally
   chosen allowed divisor lead to a dead end even when another choice works.

Termination follows because every tried \(F_h\), \(h\ge2\), has positive
degree and strictly lowers degree. Completeness follows because any putative
product contains a first factor from the finite candidate set, which exact
division will try. Thus a complete failed search, together with independently
validated \(P\), is an exact nonmembership certificate. Merely reporting that
`factor()` returned an unfamiliar factor is not one.

For a positive pair certificate it is simpler to serialize the sorted list of
indices and multiply the \(F_h\)'s from their recurrence. For a negative pair
certificate the verifier itself must run the complete membership decision (or
check a separately proved equivalent exponent-vector certificate).

## 7. Independent second evaluator

The verifier should not import the discovery program's
\(\widetilde R\)-recurrence output. A compact independent evaluator is
available entirely from the two formulas printed in the original paper.

1. Recompute the *ordinary* \(R_{u,v}(Q)\in\mathbb Z[Q]\) using lines
   180--194:
   \[
   R_{u,v}=0\ (u\nleq v),\quad R_{u,u}=1,
   \]
   and, for \(s\in D_R(v)\),
   \[
   R_{u,v}=\begin{cases}
   R_{us,vs},&s\in D_R(u),\\
   Q R_{us,vs}+(Q-1)R_{u,vs},&s\notin D_R(u).
   \end{cases}
   \]
2. Parse the claimed exact \(P(q)\), but do not trust it. In a Laurent
   polynomial dictionary in a fresh variable (z), check the identity
   \[
   \boxed{R_{u,v}(z^2)=z^dP(z-z^{-1})}.
   \]
   This is the paper's change of variables (lines 218--223) with (Q=z^2),
   and uses integer arithmetic only.
3. The substitution check uniquely determines \(P\): if \(A\ne0\), the
   highest (z)-exponent in (z^dA(z-z^{-1})) has the nonzero leading
   coefficient of \(A\). Thus no distinct claimed polynomial can pass.

This evaluator differs from discovery in both the recurrence coefficients and
the final representation, and it does not read cached discovery polynomials.
As an optional third cross-check, Dyer's increasing-Bruhat-path formula can be
implemented with the reflection order

\[
 (1,2)\prec(1,3)\prec(2,3)\prec(1,4)\prec(2,4)\prec(3,4)\prec\cdots
\]

(ordered first by the second endpoint, then by the first). It counts strictly
increasing directed Bruhat paths by their number of edges. Before treating
that third evaluator as a release gate, the exact theorem location in Dyer or
Brenti must be citation-verified; the local paper mentions Dyer's definition
but does not print the path formula.

## 8. Complete finite-enumeration requirement

For \(n=10\), the fixed reduced word

\[
 \Omega_{10}=s_2s_1s_3s_2\cdots s_9s_8
\]

has length \(16\). Enumerating all \(2^{16}\) subexpressions, multiplying them
with the stated right-action convention, and deduplicating gives the principal
ideal below \(v_{10}\); every resulting element must also pass the independent
rank test. Compare every ordered pair from that deduplicated set with the rank
test \(u\le v\).

A claim that *all* pairs passed requires the verifier to regenerate this set
and pair list. It must not trust a discovery-supplied pair count or a list that
could omit a hard pair. A finite positive result is correctly classified as
`CERTIFIED_FINITE_RESULT` for \(n=10\), not as a proof of the all-\(n\)
conjecture. A single pair is a disproof only after the second evaluator verifies
its exact polynomial and the complete monoid test proves nonmembership.

The reported \(n\le9\) statement is an author report in the original paper,
not a replacement for the user's required baseline reproduction. At minimum,
the release tests should reproduce \(n=7\), including the reducible \(F_5\)
case above, and then run the entire \(n=9\) boundary with the same certification
pipeline intended for \(n=10\).

## 9. Fail-closed certificate schema and rejection tests

A single-pair JSON certificate should contain only a fixed versioned schema,
for example: `schema_version`, `n`, `u`, `v`, `g`, `coefficients`, and either
`factor_indices` (positive certificate) or `claim: nonmember` (counterexample
certificate). Coefficients are an exact dense list from \(q^0\) upward with no
trailing zero. Code and input hashes are computed and printed by the verifier,
never accepted as evidence merely because the input contains them.

The parser and verifier must reject, with nonzero exit status, at least the
following deliberately damaged inputs:

* duplicate JSON keys, unknown keys, missing keys, wrong schema version, a
  truncated file, or concatenated extra JSON;
* floats, scientific notation, strings, `null`, or booleans in integer fields
  (in languages where `bool` subclasses `int`, check this explicitly);
* \(n\ne10\) for an \(n=10\) endpoint certificate;
* arrays of the wrong length, repeated/missing/out-of-range permutation
  entries, or a change of one entry in \(u\) or \(v\);
* \(u\nleq v\), \(v\nleq v_{10}\), or use of the reverse rank inequality;
* an empty coefficient list, a trailing zero, a negative coefficient, a
  changed coefficient, a shifted coefficient list, or a polynomial different
  from the independent ordinary-\(R\) substitution check;
* negative or nonintegral factor indices, unsorted indices, forbidden
  noncanonical unit indices (0,1), or an index larger than the derived bound
  \(2\deg H+1\);
* a deleted, inserted, or changed factor index, a false \(g\), a factor product
  that creates a negative \(q\)-exponent, or a product unequal to \(P\);
* a `nonmember` label for a polynomial for which the complete backtracking
  finds a product (especially the \(F_5\) regression case);
* for a full-run manifest: a missing pair, duplicate pair, out-of-order pair,
  extra out-of-scope pair, false pair count, truncated final record, or a
  supplied universe/pair-list hash that disagrees with the independently
  regenerated universe.

Positive controls must include \(u=v\mapsto1\), the \(S_2\) cover
\(e<s_1\mapsto q\), a second-branch call with \(us\nleq vs\), the source's
\((e,v_n)\) formula for several \(n\), and at least one polynomial with repeated
allowed factors. The tamper suite passes only if every malformed input is
rejected and each unchanged control is accepted.

## 10. Release-blocking findings

* **Fatal:** treating irreducible-factor recognition, a greedy division
  failure, or a CAS failure as proof of nonmembership.
* **Fatal:** using discovery output as the trusted polynomial in the purported
  independent verifier.
* **Fatal:** claiming all \(n=10\) pairs while trusting a supplied incomplete
  universe or pair list.
* **Major:** ambiguity between degree normalization and valuation
  normalization.
* **Major:** omitting the \(u\nleq v\mapsto0\) recursion base.
* **Major:** imposing \(h_i\le n\), or allowing negative \(h_i\), contrary to
  the well-formed source statement.
* **Major:** leaving equality, unit factors, and empty products unspecified.
* **Major:** a Bruhat rank test with untested inequality direction or a mismatch
  between left/right multiplication conventions.
* **Major:** not independently reproducing the \(n=9\) last-known baseline.
* **Local source repair:** replace the paper's typed expression
  \(\sigma_2\le\Omega_n\) by \(\sigma_2\le v_n\), recording that
  \(\Omega_n\) is the chosen reduced word for \(v_n\).

No proof assistant (Lean, Coq, Isabelle, or otherwise) was used in this referee
audit. This document specifies exact finite verifiers; it does not itself
claim that the \(n=10\) enumeration has been completed.

### 11.3 Complete-universe and recurrence audit: PASS

The verifier does not trust a discovery-supplied universe or list of pairs.
For every \(2\le n\le10\), it:

1. enumerates all \(n!\) permutations and filters them by the exact
   rank-matrix test against \(v_n\);
2. independently enumerates products of all subexpressions of the canonical
   reduced word \(\Omega_n\), deduplicates them, and requires equality of the
   two lower-set encodings;
3. constructs every ordered comparable pair by applying the rank test to all
   two-element choices in that regenerated lower set;
4. processes upper endpoints in increasing Coxeter length, so every recurrence
   source has already been certified.

The normalized \(\widetilde R\) implementation is correct. In the
non-descent branch, if
\(B_0=B_{u,vs}\) and \(B_1=B_{us,vs}\), then the code constructs
\(B_0+xB_1\); when \(us\nleq vs\), the missing pair is treated as the exact
zero term. In the common-descent branch it copies the same-gap source.

The ordinary recurrence is genuinely different and is also implemented
correctly. With ordinary variable \(Q\), lines 544--570 construct

\[
(Q-1)R_{u,vs}(Q)+Q R_{us,vs}(Q),
\]

omitting the last term exactly when \(us\nleq vs\). In the common-descent
branch it copies \(R_{us,vs}\), whose length gap is unchanged.

The exact change-of-variables implementation also matches the source. For a
term \(b_jq^{d-2j}\) in \(\widetilde R\), expanding

\[
z^d b_j(z-z^{-1})^{d-2j}
=\sum_{k=0}^{d-2j}b_j(-1)^k
  {d-2j\choose k}Q^{d-j-k},\qquad Q=z^2,
\]

gives exactly the index, sign, and binomial coefficient used in
`ordinary_from_normalized_tilde`. The program separately proves injectivity
on its finite class table and requires both recurrences to resolve to the same
class ID at every pair. Fixed-width arithmetic is acceptable here because
every coefficient-producing operation uses a wider intermediate and rejects
overflow; the successful run encountered none.

### 11.4 Fibonacci-monoid completeness audit: PASS

The previous irreducible-factor fatal finding is closed for the positive
\(n=10\) result. The verifier never asks whether irreducible factors are
themselves Fibonacci polynomials. Instead it constructs \(F_h\) exactly for
\(0\le h\le17\) and enumerates every nondecreasing sequence of nonunit factors
whose total degree is at most eight.

This enumeration is complete because:

* \(\deg B\le d/2\le8\) for \(n=10\);
* every nonunit factor has
  \(\deg F_h=\lfloor h/2\rfloor\ge1\), so any factor in such a product has
  \(2\le h\le17\), and recursion terminates after at most eight factors;
* sorting factor indices loses only order, since multiplication is
  commutative;
* the memoized state `(start_h,current_polynomial)` contains all information
  that determines its possible future extensions, so merging an identical
  state cannot remove a distinct product;
* membership is an exact full-coefficient-array lookup, with checked integer
  multiplication.

I independently regenerated this monoid in a short Python calculation, without
reading verifier output as input. It also contained exactly 434 products of
degree at most eight, reproduced
\(F_5=(1,4,3)\), and found no collision in the ordinary change-of-variables
images for any gap \(0\le d\le16\). The number of admissible images by gap was

\[
1,1,3,3,8,8,18,18,38,38,74,74,139,139,249,249,434.
\]

In particular, the full \(n=7\) run necessarily includes the pair
\((e,v_7)\), so the reducible \(F_5\) regression case that invalidated the
original factor test is accepted by the new monoid test.

remain **FAIL** until the patched source and test hashes are re-audited and

\[
minimum\_h\le h\le remaining\_sum,

properties.

* It includes the empty sequence and every prefix whose index sum is strictly
  below the budget, so it implements \(\sum h_i\le n-2\), not merely equality.
* Passing \(h\), rather than \(h+1\), as the next lower bound permits repeated
  factors. The lower bound excludes only reorderings, giving every

* Every recursive edge subtracts \(h\ge2\) from a nonnegative integer budget.
