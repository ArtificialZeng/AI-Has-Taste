# Structural reduction: finite generators for the invariant ideal

All tensor products and spans in this note are over a characteristic-zero field \(K\).  The purpose of the argument is to justify the exact \(4n\)-generator ideal used by the finite computation; no computational output is used in the proof.

## Proposition

Let
\[
A_n=K[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\otimes
\Lambda(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n,\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)
\]
with the diagonal \(S_n\)-action.  For \(U\in\{1,\theta,\xi,\theta\xi\}\), write
\[
p_{r,U}=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^rU_i,
\qquad U_i\in\{1,\theta_i,\xi_i,\theta_i\xi_i\}.
\]
Then the Hilbert ideal \(I_n=\langle(A_n^{S_n})_+\rangle\) is
\[
J_n=\left\langle
p_{r,1}\ (1\le r\le n),\quad
p_{r,U}\ (0\le r<n,\ U\in\{\theta,\xi,\theta\xi\})
\right\rangle.
\]

## Step 1: all invariants are generated by one-slot power sums

Put \(P=K[x]\otimes\Lambda(\theta,\xi)\).  There is a canonical superalgebra identification \(A_n=P^{\otimes n}\), under which \(S_n\) acts by super place permutations.  For a homogeneous \(a\in P\), define
\[
p(a)=\sum_{i=1}^n a^{(i)},
\]
where \(a^{(i)}\) is \(a\) in the \(i\)-th tensor slot and \(1\) elsewhere.

For homogeneous positive-degree monomials \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\in P\), with \(k\le n\), let
\[
M(a_1,\ldots,a_k)
=\sum_{f:\{1,\ldots,k\}\hookrightarrow\{1,\ldots,n\}}
\varepsilon(f;a_1,\ldots,a_k)
a_1^{(f(1))}\cdots a_k^{(f(k))}.
\]
Here \(\varepsilon\) is precisely the Koszul sign obtained when the product is put in increasing tensor-slot order.  Thus \(M\) is invariant.  Conversely, the Reynolds average of every monomial with \(k\) occupied slots is \((n-k)!\) times one such \(M\), up to the canonical Koszul sign.  The sum \(M\), and hence that Reynolds image, is allowed to be zero when an odd stabilizer acts by \(-1\).  Reynolds images of the ambient monomial basis span the invariant subspace, and \((n-k)!\ne0\); therefore the (possibly zero) sums \(M\) span \(A_n^{S_n}\).

Expand \(p(a_1)\cdots p(a_k)\) and classify each summand by the kernel partition \(\pi\) of its slot map \(f:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\{1,\ldots,n\}\).  If \(B=\{j_1<\cdots<j_s\}\) is a block, put \(a_B=a_{j_1}\cdots a_{j_s}\), retaining the displayed order.  The expansion has the triangular form
\[
p(a_1)\cdots p(a_k)
=M(a_1,\ldots,a_k)
+\sum_{\substack{\pi\text{ a partition of }[k]\\|\pi|<k}}
\varepsilon_\pi M((a_B)_{B\in\pi}),
\]
with the relevant Koszul signs \(\varepsilon_\pi\); a term is simply zero when an odd square occurs.  The discrete partition is the unique term with \(k\) blocks and has coefficient \(1\).  Induction on \(k\) therefore expresses every \(M\) as a superpolynomial in elements \(p(a)\).  It follows that
\[
A_n^{S_n}=K[p(a):a\text{ a positive-degree monomial of }P].
\]

Every monomial of \(P\) is exactly one of
\[
x^r,\quad x^r\theta,\quad x^r\xi,\quad x^r\theta\xi
\qquad(r\ge0),
\]
with \(r=0\) excluded only in the first family because \(p(1)=n\) is a scalar.  Hence the full Hilbert ideal is generated by all \(p_{r,U}\) in these four infinite families.

## Step 2: Cayley--Hamilton truncates the bosonic exponent

Let \(e_j=e_j(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\).  Newton's identities in characteristic zero show that
\[
\langle p_{1,1},\ldots,p_{n,1}\rangle
=\langle e_1,\ldots,e_n\rangle.
\]
For each \(i\), substitution of \(t=x_i\) in \(\prod_{j=1}^n(t-x_j)\) gives the polynomial identity
\[
x_i^n-e_1x_i^{n-1}+e_2x_i^{n-2}-\cdots+(-1)^ne_n=0.
\]
Multiplying by \(x_i^{r-n}U_i\), summing over \(i\), and using that every \(e_j\) is even gives, for every \(r\ge n\),
\[
p_{r,U}=e_1p_{r-1,U}-e_2p_{r-2,U}+\cdots+(-1)^{n+1}e_np_{r-n,U}.
\]
Every term on the right belongs to \(J_n\), because \(e_j\in J_n\).  Thus all \(p_{r,U}\) with \(r\ge n\) already lie in \(J_n\).  The remaining positive one-slot generators are exactly those displayed in the proposition.  Therefore \(I_n\subseteq J_n\).  The reverse inclusion is immediate because every displayed generator is a positive-degree \(S_n\)-invariant.  Hence \(I_n=J_n\).

## Audited edge cases and dependencies

- The Reynolds spanning step uses characteristic zero both for the Reynolds projection and to divide by \((n-k)!\); the source endpoint is over \(\mathbb C\), so this is valid.  A monomial with a sign-reversing stabilizer has zero Reynolds image and contributes nothing to the spanning set.
- Newton's identities use division by integers \(1,\ldots,n\), again valid in characteristic zero.
- All signs are confined to the orbit-sum/product identity.  The induction only needs its triangular support and the coefficient \(1\) of the discrete partition; zero collision terms cause no division.
- The case \(k=0\) is the scalar invariant and is excluded by the positive-degree subscript.
- The case \(n=1\) yields the four displayed families \(p_{1,1},p_{0,\theta},p_{0,\xi},p_{0,\theta\xi}\); the last is redundant but harmless.

This proves the exact structural reduction for every \(n\ge1\), not merely for \(n=5\).
