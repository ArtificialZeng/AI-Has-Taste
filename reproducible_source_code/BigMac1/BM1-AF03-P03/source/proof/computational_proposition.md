# Exact finite proposition for \(n=3,4,5\)

## Proposition

Over \(\mathbb Q\), the residue classes of Lentfer's monomials \(B_n^{(1,2)}\) form a basis of \(R_n^{(1,2)}\) for \(n=3,4,5\).  The exact dimensions are
\[
\dim R_3^{(1,2)}=24,\qquad
\dim R_4^{(1,2)}=192,\qquad
\dim R_5^{(1,2)}=1920.
\]
Consequently the same claims hold after scalar extension to every characteristic-zero field, in particular \(\mathbb C\).

## Certified computation

For each \(n\), the structural proposition in `generator_lemma.md` replaces the ideal generated by all positive invariants by its exact \(4n\)-element generating family.  Singular 4.4.1 constructs the supercommutative quotient with its `superCommutative` implementation, computes a two-sided standard basis with `twostd`, and performs every coefficient operation over \(\mathbb Q\) (`ring R=0,...`).  No prime-field, floating-point, randomized, or heuristic rank is used.

Let \(G_n\) be the resulting standard basis and \(K_n=\operatorname{kbase}(G_n)\) its standard-monomial basis.  The verifier performs three exact checks:

1. every one of the \(4n\) input generators reduces to zero by \(G_n\);
2. `vdim(G_n)=|K_n|=|B_n^{(1,2)}|`;
3. if \(N_n\) is the list of exact normal forms of the candidate monomials, `coeffs(N_n,K_n)` reconstructs \(N_n\) and has full rank \(|B_n^{(1,2)}|\).

The recorded results are:

| \(n\) | generators | candidate count | quotient dimension | candidate normal-form rank | standard-basis size |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 12 | 24 | 24 | 24 | 19 |
| 4 | 16 | 192 | 192 | 192 | 59 |
| 5 | 20 | 1920 | 1920 | 1920 | 271 |

Full normal-form rank proves linear independence of the candidate residue classes.  Equality of their number with the quotient dimension then proves they are a basis.  Because both the ideal and all candidates are multihomogeneous, this global computation is the direct sum of 95 nonempty candidate multidegree blocks at \(n=5\); their counts are serialized in `certificates/n5_input.json`.

The declared degree-product order does not make every candidate a standard monomial: at \(n=5\), 1488 of the 1920 candidate monomials are unchanged normal forms in the discovery run.  This does not weaken the basis conclusion, because the exact coordinate matrix of all 1920 normal forms has rank 1920.  Accordingly, the result is stated as a basis theorem, not as the stronger and order-dependent assertion that this particular `dp` order has \(B_5^{(1,2)}\) as its standard monomials.

## Reproduction

From the project root:

```sh
sh experiments/run_exact.sh
```

The command reconstructs the serialized inputs for \(n=3,4,5\), runs the fail-closed verifier, runs five destructive-input tests, and prints SHA-256 hashes.  The decisive \(n=5\) input hash recorded on the first certified run is
`09700e99a7add186ea0f04877b33f041bcd9e01c8633cf83aa15131f596bf25f`.
