\begin{abstract}
Let $H$ be any infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space.  We construct a
point-ultraweakly closed proper convex cone $\Kcone$ of normal completely
positive maps on $\B(H)$ whose $(Q,P)$-compression cone is the full cone of
completely positive maps $\B(PH)\to\B(QH)$ for every pair of finite-rank
projections $P,Q$.  The cone is an explicit homogeneous half-space inside the
normal completely positive cone, and an explicit rank-one map shows that it is
not cut--pad stable.  Thus point-ultraweak closedness does not suffice for
finite-corner reconstruction when cut--pad stability is removed.  No
separability assumption or computational evidence is used.
\end{abstract}

\section{Context and statement}

Completely positive maps have a global representation theory through
Stinespring's theorem~\cite{Stinespring1955}, while in matrix algebras complete
positivity is detected by the Choi matrix~\cite{Choi1975}.  Kian and Kittaneh
recently formulated a finite-corner substitute for the latter test on maps
between infinite-dimensional operator algebras~\cite{KianKittaneh2026}.  Their
Theorem~3.9 shows that a convex cone of normal maps is reconstructed from its
finite corner cones when it is both point-ultraweakly closed and cut--pad
stable.  Their Proposition~5.5 shows that closedness cannot be deleted while
cut--pad stability is retained.  The result below gives the complementary
sharpness statement: closedness is retained and cut--pad stability is deleted.

Explicit low-rank witnesses can be particularly effective at exposing a
failed dimension-free assertion.  In a different matrix problem,
Zeng--Liu--Ratnavelu give an exact counterexample carried by two real
$2\times2$ rank-one matrices~\cite{ZengLiuRatnavelu2026}.  Here rank-one maps
instead let us alter a global half-space constraint while leaving any
prescribed finite input corner unchanged.

We now fix the notation needed for the precise statement.  Let $H$ be a
complex Hilbert space and let
\[
 \CP^\sigma(H)=\{\Phi:\B(H)\to\B(H):
     \Phi\text{ is complex-linear, normal, and completely positive}\}.
\]
The ambient vector space is the space of normal maps.  It carries the
point-ultraweak topology: $\Phi_\alpha\to\Phi$ when
\[
 \Tr(S\Phi_\alpha(T))\longrightarrow \Tr(S\Phi(T))
 \quad(T\in\B(H),\ S\in\T(H)).
\]
All topological closedness below is relative to this ambient space.
Here ``proper'' means strict inclusion in $\CP^\sigma(H)$.

For finite-rank orthogonal projections $P,Q$, the $(Q,P)$-compression of a
normal map is
\[
 \Phi_{Q,P}(X)=Q\Phi(X)Q,
 \qquad X\in P\B(H)P\cong\B(PH),
\]
and, for a set $\mathcal C$ of maps,
\[
 \mathcal C_{Q,P}=\{\Phi_{Q,P}:\Phi\in\mathcal C\}.
\]
This compression-image notation remains meaningful without cut--pad
stability.  Write $\CP_{Q,P}$ for all completely positive maps
$\B(PH)\to\B(QH)$.  The cut--pad map associated with $\Phi$ is
\[
 \Phi^{(Q,P)}(T)=Q\Phi(PTP)Q,
 \qquad T\in\B(H).
\]
These are the conventions of~\cite[Definitions~3.1 and 3.3,
Remark~5.4]{KianKittaneh2026}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space $H$, there is a
point-ultraweakly closed convex cone
\[
  \Kcone\subsetneq\CP^\sigma(H)
\]
such that
\[
  \Kcone_{Q,P}=\CP_{Q,P}
\]
for every pair of finite-rank orthogonal projections $P,Q$.  Moreover,
$\Kcone$ is not cut--pad stable.
\end{theorem}

In particular, the theorem disproves the assertion that every
point-ultraweakly closed convex subcone of $\CP^\sigma(H)$ is determined by
its finite corner cones without the cut--pad hypothesis.

\section{The half-space construction}

Fix a unit vector $e\in H$, let $E=|e\rangle\langle e|$ be the orthogonal
projection onto $\mathbb Ce$, and put
\[
 A=2E-I.
\]
For a unit vector $z\in H$, denote by $\omega_z$ the normal vector state and
define
\[
 \Theta_z(T)=\omega_z(T)E,
 \qquad T\in\B(H).
\]
This notation is independent of which argument of the Hilbert-space inner
product is taken to be linear.

Each $\Theta_z$ is normal: for $S\in\T(H)$,
\[
 \Tr(S\Theta_z(T))=\Tr(SE)\,\omega_z(T),
\]
an ultraweakly continuous functional of $T$.  It is also completely positive.
Indeed, if $[T_{ij}]\in M_n(\B(H))$ is positive, then
$[\omega_z(T_{ij})]\in M_n(\mathbb C)$ is positive, and hence
\[
 [\Theta_z(T_{ij})]=[\omega_z(T_{ij})]\otimes E\geq0.
\]

On normal maps define the point-ultraweakly continuous functional
\[
 f(\Phi)=\omega_e(\Phi(A))=\Tr(E\Phi(A)).
\]
For a positive map $\Phi$, the self-adjoint operator $A$ is sent to a
self-adjoint operator, so $f(\Phi)$ is real.  Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cone}
 \Kcone=\{\Phi\in\CP^\sigma(H): f(\Phi)\leq0\}.
\end{equation}

We first verify the topological point that is needed here.  The cone
$\CP^\sigma(H)$ is point-ultraweakly closed in the ambient space of normal
maps.  Suppose that normal completely positive maps $\Phi_\alpha$ converge
point-ultraweakly to a normal map $\Phi$.  For every
$[T_{ij}]\in M_n(\B(H))_+$, the block matrices
$[\Phi_\alpha(T_{ij})]$ converge ultraweakly in $\B(H^n)$ to
$[\Phi(T_{ij})]$.  (The matrix size is finite, so a trace-class block
functional has only finitely many trace-class entries.)  The positive cone of
$\B(H^n)$ is ultraweakly closed; consequently
$[\Phi(T_{ij})]\geq0$ for every $n$.  Thus $\Phi$ is completely positive.
Because $f$ is continuous, \eqref{eq:cone} is point-ultraweakly closed.
Linearity of $f$ and the conic convexity of $\CP^\sigma(H)$ show that
$\Kcone$ is a convex cone.

The cone is proper.  The rank-one map $\Theta_e$ belongs to
$\CP^\sigma(H)$, whereas
\[
 f(\Theta_e)=\omega_e(A)\omega_e(E)=1>0.
\]
Hence $\Theta_e\notin\Kcone$.

\section{All finite corner cones are full}

Fix arbitrary finite-rank projections $P,Q$.  Compression preserves complete
positivity, so
\[
 \Kcone_{Q,P}\subseteq\CP_{Q,P}.
\]
For the reverse inclusion, let $\psi\in\CP_{Q,P}$.  Regard its output as an
operator on $H$ acting by zero on $(QH)^\perp$, and define
\[
 \widehat\psi(T)=\psi(PTP),
 \qquad T\in\B(H).
\]
This is completely positive: at every matrix level it is the composition of
compression to $(PH)^n$, the amplification of $\psi$, and extension by zero
from $(QH)^n$ to $H^n$.  It is normal as well.  Indeed, $T\mapsto PTP$ has
finite-dimensional range, and entrywise ultraweak convergence on this range
is convergence in its unique finite-dimensional linear topology.  Applying
$\psi$ and the zero embedding therefore preserves ultraweak convergence.

The space $PH+\mathbb Ce$ is finite-dimensional.  Since $H$ is
infinite-dimensional, choose a unit vector
\[
 v\perp(PH+\mathbb Ce).
\]
For $X\in P\B(H)P$ we have $Xv=0$, so
$(\Theta_v)_{Q,P}(X)=0$.  Also $Av=-v$, and therefore
\[
 f(\Theta_v)=\omega_v(A)\omega_e(E)=-1.
\]
Let
\[
 b=f(\widehat\psi)\in\mathbb R,
 \qquad t=\max\{0,b\},
 \qquad \Phi=\widehat\psi+t\Theta_v.
\]
Then $\Phi$ is normal and completely positive, while
\[
 f(\Phi)=b-t\leq0.
\]
Thus $\Phi\in\Kcone$.  Since the perturbation has zero $(Q,P)$-corner,
\[
 \Phi_{Q,P}=\widehat\psi_{Q,P}+t(\Theta_v)_{Q,P}=\psi.
\]
This proves $\CP_{Q,P}\subseteq\Kcone_{Q,P}$ and hence equality.  The same
argument includes $P=0$ or $Q=0$, when the corresponding CP cone contains
only the zero map.  No vector chosen here must work for two different
corners: the definition of $\Kcone_{Q,P}$ permits the representing global map
to depend on $(Q,P)$, exactly as emphasized in
\cite[Remark~5.4]{KianKittaneh2026}.

\section{Failure of cut--pad stability}

Choose a unit vector $u\perp e$, set $w=(e+u)/\sqrt2$, and let
$\Phi=\Theta_w$.  Since $\omega_w(A)=0$, we have $f(\Phi)=0$ and hence
$\Phi\in\Kcone$.  Take $P=Q=E$.  For every $T\in\B(H)$,
\begin{align*}
 \Phi^{(E,E)}(T)
  &=E\Theta_w(ETE)E \\
  &=\omega_w(ETE)E \\
  &=\tfrac12\omega_e(T)E
   =\tfrac12\Theta_e(T).
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\[
 f(\Phi^{(E,E)})=\tfrac12>0,
\]
so $\Phi^{(E,E)}\notin\Kcone$.  This explicitly proves that $\Kcone$ is not
cut--pad stable and completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

The excluded map $\Theta_e$ makes the reconstruction failure concrete:
$\Theta_e\notin\Kcone$, but every finite compression of $\Theta_e$ belongs to
$\CP_{Q,P}=\Kcone_{Q,P}$.  Thus the finite corner data alone cannot detect its
nonmembership.

\section*{Scope and assistance disclosure}

The proof is analytic and uses no numerical experiment, computer algebra, or
finite search.  It establishes the stated counterexample for arbitrary,
possibly nonseparable, infinite-dimensional $H$.  It does not make a priority
