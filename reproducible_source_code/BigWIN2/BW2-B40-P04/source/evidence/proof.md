# Candidate proof that \(K^*_{7,4}=24\)

This note uses the precise reading in `problem.md`: correlations are counted in
both ordered orientations and, because the ambient dimension is four, the
exponent in the frozen objective is exactly \(\gamma c_i^Tc_j\).

## Theorem

For
\[
 V_\gamma(7,4)=\min_{(c_1,\ldots,c_7)\in(S^3)^7}
 \sum_{i\ne j}e^{\gamma c_i^Tc_j},
\]
one has \(\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}V_\gamma(7,4)=24\).

## Upper bound

Let \(q_\gamma=\log(4\gamma)/(4\gamma)\),
\(r_\gamma=(1-q_\gamma^2)^{1/2}\), and let
\(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\) be the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^4\). For all
sufficiently large \(\gamma\), take the seven unit vectors
\[
 x_{a,s}=-q_\gamma e_4+s r_\gamma e_a
 \quad(1\le a\le3,\ s\in\{-1,1\}),
 \qquad y=e_4.
\]
Two shifted vectors on different axes have correlation \(q_\gamma^2\)
(24 ordered pairs); opposite shifted vectors on the same axis have correlation
\(2q_\gamma^2-1\) (6 ordered pairs); and a pair involving \(y\) has correlation
\(-q_\gamma\) (12 ordered pairs). Hence this code has energy
\[
 24e^{\gamma q_\gamma^2}
 +6e^{\gamma(2q_\gamma^2-1)}+12e^{-\gamma q_\gamma}\longrightarrow24,
\]
because \(\gamma q_\gamma\to\infty\) and
\(\gamma q_\gamma^2\to0\). Thus \(\limsup V_\gamma(7,4)\le24\).

## A weighted balance lemma

Suppose \((z_i)_{i\in P}\) and \((z_j)_{j\in N}\) are unit vectors,
\(\alpha=(\alpha_i)_{i\in P}\) and \(\beta=(\beta_j)_{j\in N}\) are
positive probability vectors, and
\[
 \sum_{i\in P}\alpha_i z_i=
 \sum_{j\in N}\beta_j z_j=\mu.
\]
For arbitrary positive costs \(d_{ij}\), weighted AM--GM, with weights
\(w_{ij}=\alpha_i\beta_j\), gives
\[
 \sum_{i\in P,j\in N}d_{ij}e^{\gamma z_i^Tz_j}
 \ge
 \frac{e^{\gamma\|\mu\|^2}\prod_{i,j}d_{ij}^{\alpha_i\beta_j}}
 {\prod_i\alpha_i^{\alpha_i}\prod_j\beta_j^{\beta_j}}. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the weighted mean of the correlations is \(\|\mu\|^2\), and
\(\prod_{i,j}(\alpha_i\beta_j)^{\alpha_i\beta_j}
=\prod_i\alpha_i^{\alpha_i}\prod_j\beta_j^{\beta_j}\).
Also
\[
 \prod_i\alpha_i^{\alpha_i}\le\|\alpha\|_\infty,
 \qquad
 \prod_j\beta_j^{\beta_j}\le\|\beta\|_\infty. \tag{2}
\]

## Classification of every bounded-energy limit

It suffices to consider an arbitrary sequence \(\gamma_m\to\infty\) along
which the energies are bounded. Write its vectors as \(c_i^{(m)}\), pass to a
subsequence on which all seven vectors converge, and call the limit
\(c_i^{(0)}\). If \(B\) bounds the ordered energy and \(\rho_m\) is the
largest correlation, then the two orientations of a maximizing pair give
\(2e^{\gamma_m\rho_m}\le B\). Consequently \(\rho_m=O(1/\gamma_m)\), so
all off-diagonal entries of the limiting Gram matrix \(G_0\) are nonpositive.

Join two limiting vectors when their correlation is strictly negative. The
Gram matrix is block diagonal across the connected components. For a
component Gram matrix \(H\), the matrix \(I-H\) is nonnegative and irreducible.
If \(H\) is singular, Perron--Frobenius applied to the eigenvalue 1 of \(I-H\)
shows that \(\ker H\) is one-dimensional and generated by a vector whose
coordinates are all strictly positive. Thus each component has nullity zero
or one.

Since \(G_0\) has size 7 and rank at most 4, at least three components are
dependent. Each dependent component contains at least two vertices, so there
can be at most three. Hence there are exactly three dependent components,
denoted \(B_1,B_2,B_3\), the rank is exactly four, and at most one further
vertex remains. Equivalently, the dependent-component sizes are
\((2,2,2)\), with one singleton left over, or \((3,2,2)\).

For each \(a\), extend the positive generator of the kernel of the
\(B_a\)-block by zero to all seven coordinates and normalize it to have
coordinate sum one; call it \(u^a\). Then
\[
 \sum_{i\in B_a}u_i^a c_i^{(0)}=0. \tag{3}
\]
Every coordinate satisfies
\[
 u_i^a=\left\|-\sum_{h\in B_a\setminus\{i\}}u_h^a c_h^{(0)}\right\|
 \le1-u_i^a,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \|u^a\|_\infty\le\tfrac12. \tag{4}
\]

## Persistence of the three balances

Let \(A_m\) be the \(5\times7\) matrix whose \(i\)-th column is
\((c_i^{(m)},1)\). The kernel of the four-row vector matrix at the limit is
exactly \(\operatorname{span}\{u^1,u^2,u^3\}\). Imposing coordinate sum zero
therefore gives
\[
 \ker A_0=\left\{\theta_1u^1+\theta_2u^2+\theta_3u^3:
 \theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3=0\right\}, \tag{5}
\]
which has dimension two. Thus \(A_0\) has full row rank five. For all large
\(m\), so does \(A_m\), and the two-dimensional spaces \(L_m=\ker A_m\)
converge to \(L_0\). (For example, their orthogonal projectors are
\(I-A_m^T(A_mA_m^T)^{-1}A_m\).)

Fix an unordered component pair \(\{a,b\}\). Project \(u^a-u^b\) onto
\(L_m\), obtaining \(\ell_m^{ab}\to u^a-u^b\). Because
\(\ell_m^{ab}\in\ker A_m\), its coordinates sum to zero and its vector-weighted
sum is zero. Normalize its positive and negative parts to probability vectors
\(\alpha_m^{ab}\) and \(\beta_m^{ab}\). They have a common barycenter and
\[
 \alpha_m^{ab}\longrightarrow u^a,\qquad
 \beta_m^{ab}\longrightarrow u^b. \tag{6}
\]
Strict positivity of the coordinates of \(u^a,u^b\) makes the indicated signs
stable on \(B_a,B_b\); coordinates outside those two blocks may have either
sign but carry total mass tending to zero. In particular, by (4),
\[
 \limsup_m\|\alpha_m^{ab}\|_\infty\le\tfrac12,\qquad
 \limsup_m\|\beta_m^{ab}\|_\infty\le\tfrac12. \tag{7}
\]

Let
\[
 U_m=\sum_{i<j}e^{\gamma_m(c_i^{(m)})^Tc_j^{(m)}}
\]
be the unordered energy; it is bounded by assumption. Let \(\delta_m\) be
the \(\alpha_m^{ab}\otimes\beta_m^{ab}\)-mass outside the rectangle
\(B_a\times B_b\). By (6), \(\delta_m\to0\). Put
\[
 \epsilon_m=\max(\delta_m,1/m),
 \qquad \eta_m=e^{-1/\sqrt{\epsilon_m}}.
\]
Then \(\eta_m\to0\) and
\(\eta_m^{\delta_m}=e^{-\delta_m/\sqrt{\epsilon_m}}\to1\).
Apply (1) with cost one on \(B_a\times B_b\) and cost \(\eta_m\) elsewhere.
Equations (1), (2), and (7), together with \(e^{\gamma_m\|\mu_m\|^2}\ge1\),
show that the costed cross-sign sum is at least \(4-o(1)\). On the other hand,
that sum is at most
\[
 S_{ab,m}+\eta_m U_m,
 \qquad
 S_{ab,m}:=\sum_{i\in B_a,j\in B_b}
 e^{\gamma_m(c_i^{(m)})^Tc_j^{(m)}}.
\]
Since \(U_m\) is bounded and \(\eta_m\to0\), this proves
\[
 S_{ab,m}\ge4-o(1). \tag{8}
\]

There are three component pairs. Their rectangles are disjoint sets of
unordered codeword pairs, so (8) yields
\[
 U_m\ge S_{12,m}+S_{13,m}+S_{23,m}\ge12-o(1).
\]
The frozen objective counts both orientations and therefore equals \(2U_m\).
Every bounded-energy sequence consequently has ordered liminf at least 24.

Finally, exact minimizers exist by compactness. The upper-bound construction
makes their energies bounded, so applying the preceding argument to a
subsequence realizing their liminf gives
\(\liminf_{\gamma\to\infty}V_\gamma(7,4)\ge24\). Combined with the explicit
upper bound, this proves the theorem.

## Audit focus

The decisive point is the cost \(\eta_m\) in (1): it prevents vanishing
coefficients leaking into the third component or singleton from making the
three Radon cross-sums overlap at full weight. The argument uses boundedness
of \(U_m\) only to remove the resulting \(\eta_m U_m\) error. No
differentiability or assumed \(1/\gamma\)-scale expansion of the Gram matrix is
used.
