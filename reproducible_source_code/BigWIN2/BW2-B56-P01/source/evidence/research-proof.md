# Factor-two stability bound: candidate proof

This note proves the immutable assertion in `source.md`, conditional only on
the optimization characterization, monotonicity, and supremum identity recorded
in `problem.md` and proved in the copied primary source
`evidence/external/2609.03200.pdf`.  Put
\[
 d=\dim \mathbb S^r=\binom{r+1}{2},\qquad N'_r=2^{r-1}d.
\]

## 1. Row-sign normalization

Fix integers \(n\ge r\ge1\), and choose a maximizing feasible pair
\((U,w)\) in Theorem 2.1.  Write \(x_i^T\) for row \(i\) of \(U\), and set
\[
 \varepsilon_i=\begin{cases}1,&(x_i)_1\ge0,\\-1,&(x_i)_1<0,\end{cases}
 \qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n),
 \qquad V=DU.
\]
Let \(y_i^T=\varepsilon_i x_i^T\) be the rows of \(V\).  Then
\[
 V^TV=U^TD^2U=U^TU=I_r,
 \qquad |\langle y_i,y_j\rangle|=|\langle x_i,x_j\rangle|.
\]
The vector \(w\) is unchanged, so it remains nonnegative and unit, and
\(\sigma(V,w)=\sigma(U,w)=\lambda(r,n)\).  Thus \((V,w)\) is again a
maximizing pair.  Moreover \((y_i)_1\ge0\) for every \(i\), including the
case \((x_i)_1=0\).  With the convention \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=+1\), every
row therefore lies in an orthant whose first sign is \(+1\).  The zero vector
lies in the all-positive orthant.

The signed graph needed in Claims 2.2--2.3 must be defined from the normalized
rows, rather than silently identified with the old graph.  Define its sign
matrix \(Q\) by
\[
 Q_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,&\langle y_i,y_j\rangle\ge0,\\
                     -1,&\langle y_i,y_j\rangle<0.
       \end{cases}
\]
This treats a zero inner product as positive, exactly as in the source.  The
proof of Claim 2.1 applies to the normalized maximizing pair because its
absolute Gram matrix is unchanged.  The Ky--Fan equality argument of Claim
2.2, now using \(Q=I+S(G)\), supplies a symmetric \(H\) with
\(M(G,w)V=VH\).  Claim 2.3 then gives
\[
 y_i^THy_i=\lambda(r,n)w_i^2\quad(1\le i\le n).
\]
No switching identity for the old graph is required (and such an identity
would need a separate convention on zero inner products).  Hence all inputs
to the cloning claim hold after normalization.

## 2. Half-orthant cloning

Let
\[
 \mathcal S_+=\{s\in\{\pm1\}^r:s_1=+1\};
 \qquad |\mathcal S_+|=2^{r-1}.
\]
For a current normalized maximizing pair and \(s\in\mathcal S_+\), let
\(C_s\) be the indices of rows in the orthant \(O_s\).  Two rows in the same
orthant have nonnegative coordinatewise products, hence nonnegative inner
product; this includes all zero-coordinate boundary cases.  Thus condition
(i) of Claim 2.4 holds.

In the \(d\)-dimensional space \(\mathbb S^r\), the matrix
\[
 A_s=\sum_{i\in C_s}y_i y_i^T
\]
belongs to the cone generated by \(\{y_i y_i^T:i\in C_s\}\).  Conic
Caratheodory gives a set \(I_s\subseteq C_s\) with \(|I_s|\le d\) and
coefficients \(\beta_i\ge0\) such that
\[
 A_s=\sum_{i\in I_s}\beta_i y_i y_i^T.
\]
(Zero generators may be omitted; if \(A_s=0\), take \(I_s=\varnothing\).)
Define \(c_i=\beta_i\) on \(I_s\), \(c_i=0\) on
\(C_s\setminus I_s\), and \(c_i=1\) outside \(C_s\).  This is exactly
condition (ii) of Claim 2.4.  Cloning replaces
\[
 (y_i,w_i)\longmapsto (\sqrt{c_i}\,y_i,\sqrt{c_i}\,w_i)
\]
and, by Claim 2.4, produces another maximizing pair with at most \(d\)
nonzero rows in \(O_s\).

This operation may be repeated over all \(s\in\mathcal S_+\).  Indeed, if
\(c_i>0\), a nonzero row retains its complete sign vector; if \(c_i=0\), it
becomes the zero row in the all-positive orthant.  Thus cloning never creates
a nonzero row in a new orthant and never creates a row with negative first
coordinate.  Rows outside the cell being processed are unchanged.  A zero row
which migrates to the all-positive cell remains zero under every later
scaling, so it cannot spoil any previously obtained *nonzero-support* bound.
After every cloning step Claims 2.1--2.3 apply afresh because the new pair is
maximizing; the graph and \(H\) are allowed to change.  Consequently, after
all \(2^{r-1}\) cells have been processed, there is a maximizing pair
\((\widehat U,\widehat w)\) with
\[
 |J|\le 2^{r-1}d=N'_r,
 \qquad J=\{i:\text{row }i\text{ of }\widehat U\text{ is nonzero}\}.
\]

For completeness, every zero row of any maximizing pair has zero weight.
Its row in the absolute Gram matrix \(B\) is zero, whereas Claim 2.1 gives
\(B\widehat w=\lambda(r,n)\widehat w\); since
\(\lambda(r,n)\ge1\), the corresponding coordinate of \(\widehat w\) is
zero.  Hence deleting all indices outside \(J\) preserves
\(\|\widehat w\|_2=1\), the objective, and
\(\widehat U^T\widehat U=I_r\).  The resulting matrix has \(k=|J|\) rows;
its rank is \(r\), so
\[
 r\le k\le N'_r.
\]
It is therefore a feasible pair for the \(k\)-row optimization and yields
\[
 \lambda(r,n)\le\lambda(r,k)\le\lambda(r,N'_r),
\]
where the last inequality is monotonicity.  (Padding from \(k\) to \(N'_r\)
can equivalently be done by adjoining zero rows and zero weights.)

## 3. Passage to the absolute constant

The preceding inequality holds for every \(n\ge r\).  Taking the supremum
and using \(\lambda(r)=\sup_{n\ge r}\lambda(r,n)\) gives
\[
 \lambda(r)\le\lambda(r,N'_r)\le\lambda(r),
\]
so \(\lambda(r,N'_r)=\lambda(r)\).  Since \(\lambda(r,n)\) is nondecreasing
and is bounded above by its supremum \(\lambda(r)\),
\[
 \lambda(r,n)=\lambda(r)\qquad\text{for every }n\ge N'_r.
\]
Finally, \(N'_r\ge r\) for every \(r\ge1\), so every optimization invoked
above is in its stated range.  For \(r=1\), the argument has one half-orthant
and \(N'_1=1\), with no exceptional case.
