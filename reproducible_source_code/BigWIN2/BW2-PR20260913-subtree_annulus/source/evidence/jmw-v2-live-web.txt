L347:  | $$B_{m}(\gamma_{n})\leq\frac{m-1}{a_{n}^{m-1}}.$$  |
L348: 
L349: Consider the composition $(1,1,\dots,1)\in\mathcal{C}(m-1)$. Note that $B_{1}(\gamma_{n})=0$, $\lambda_{1}(\gamma_{n})=\gamma_{n}$ and $\lambda_{1}(\gamma_{n})-1=a_{n}$. Then
L350: 
L351:  | $$\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{1}(\gamma_{n})\left(1+B_{1}(\gamma_{n})\right)}{\gamma_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\lambda_{1}(\gamma_{n})-1\right)}=\frac{(m-1)\lambda_{1}(\gamma_{n})}{\gamma_{n}\bigl(\lambda_{1}(\gamma_{n})-1\bigr)^{m-1}}=\frac{m-1}{a_{n}^{m-1}}.$$  |
L352: 
L353: Thus
L354:  | $$B_{m}(\gamma_{n})\geq\frac{m-1}{a_{n}^{m-1}}.$$  |
L355: 
L356: Consequently,
L357: 
L358:  | $$B_{m}(\gamma_{n})=\frac{m-1}{a_{n}^{m-1}}.$$  |
L359: 
L360: It remains to determine the compositions attaining the maximum when $m=n$. Let $(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathcal{C}(n-1)$ be a composition that attains the maximum of $B_{n}(\gamma_{n})$, that is,
L361: 
L362:  | $$B_{n}(\gamma_{n})=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})\bigl(1+B_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})\bigr)}{\gamma_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(\lambda_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})-1\bigr)}.$$  |
L363: By Equation (cite38†2.6 ) and the fact $B_{n}(\gamma_{n})=1$, we have
L364: 
L365:  | $$\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(a_{n}^{n_{i}-1}+n_{i}-1\bigr)=(n-1)\prod_{i=1}^{d}q_{n_{i}}.$$  |
L366: 
L367: By the characterization of equality in Lemma cite37†2.5 , we have $(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathcal{C}(n-1)$ or $(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})=(n-1)\in\mathcal{C}(n-1)$. Note that the exceptional case in Lemma cite37†2.5 cannot occur here, since the sum of the parts is $n-1$.
L368: 
L369: This completes the proof. ∎
L370: ## 3 Proof of Theorem cite31†1.3 L371: 
L372: The next lemma is the central comparison estimate.
L373: 
L374: ###### Lemma 3.1.
L375: 
L376: Let $T$ be a tree of order $n$, let $u\in V(T)$, and let $z\in\mathbb{C}$ satisfy $|z|=r\geq 2$. Then
L377: 
L378:  | $$|\Phi_{T-u}(z)|\leq B_{n}(r)|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|.$$  |
L379: ###### Proof.
L380: 
L381: We proceed by induction on $n$. If $n=1$, then $T-u$ is empty and the left-hand side is zero.
L382: 
L383: Assume $n\geq 2$. Let $T_{1},\dots,T_{d}$ be the components of $T-u$, rooted at the corresponding neighbors $u_{1},\dots,u_{d}$ of $u$, and let $n_{i}=|V(T_{i})|$. For convenience, set
L384: 
L385:  | $$L_{i}=\Phi_{T_{i},u_{i}}(z)$$  |
L386: 
L387: and
L388: 
L389:  | $$F_{i}=\Phi_{T_{i}-u_{i}}(z).$$  |
L390: 
L391: By the induction hypothesis,
L392: 
L393:  | $$|F_{i}|\leq B_{n_{i}}(r)|L_{i}|.$$  |  | (3.1)
L394: Since $\Phi_{T_{i}}(z)=L_{i}+F_{i}$ and $\Phi_{T-u}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Phi_{T_{i}}(z)$, we obtain
L395: 
L396:  | $$|\Phi_{T-u}(z)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(|F_{i}|+|L_{i}|\bigr)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(1+B_{n_{i}}(r)\bigr)|L_{i}|.$$  |
L397: 
L398: On the other hand, the product formula (cite39†2.2 ) and the reverse triangle inequality give
L399: 
L400:  | $$|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|=r\prod_{i=1}^{d}|1+L_{i}|\geq r\prod_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(|L_{i}|-1\bigr).$$  |
L401: 
L402: By Lemma cite40†2.1 , we have
L403: 
L404:  | $$|L_{i}|\geq\lambda_{n_{i}}(r)>1,$$  |
L405: and then $|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|>0$. Consequently,
L406: 
L407:  | $$\frac{|\Phi_{T-u}(z)|}{|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|}\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}|L_{i}|\bigl(1+B_{n_{i}}(r)\bigr)}{r\prod_{i=1}^{d}(|L_{i}|-1)}\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{n_{i}}(r)\bigl(1+B_{n_{i}}(r)\bigr)}{r\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\lambda_{n_{i}}(r)-1)},$$  |  | (3.2)
L408: 
L409: where the last inequality holds by Lemma cite32†2.2 . By the definition of $B_{n}(r)$, we have
L410:  | $$\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{n_{i}}(r)\bigl(1+B_{n_{i}}(r)\bigr)}{r\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\lambda_{n_{i}}(r)-1)}\leq B_{n}(r).$$  |
L411: 
L412: Thus
L413: 
L414:  | $$\frac{|\Phi_{T-u}(z)|}{|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|}\leq B_{n}(r).$$  |
L415: 
L416: This completes the proof. ∎
L417: 
L418: We now prove Theorem cite31†1.3 .
L419: ###### Proof of Theorem cite31†1.3 .
L420: 
L421: For $n=2$, the only tree is $K_{1,1}$, and
L422: 
L423:  | $$\Phi_{K_{1,1}}(z)=z(z+2).$$  |
L424: 
L425: Thus the unique nonzero root is $z=-2$, and $|z|=2=1+(2-1)^{1/(2-1)}$. Hence equality holds, as $n=2$ is even.
L426: 
L427: For $n=3$, the only tree is $K_{1,2}$, and
L428: 
L429:  | $$\Phi_{K_{1,2}}(z)=z(z^{2}+2z+3).$$  |
L430: 
L431: Its two nonzero roots have modulus $\sqrt{3}<1+\sqrt{2}$, so equality does not occur.
L432: For $n=4$, the required radius is $1+\sqrt[3]{3}$. By Theorem cite29†1.1 , equality holds only for $K_{1,3}$. Moreover,
L433: 
L434:  | $$\Phi_{K_{1,3}}(z)=z\bigl((z+1)^{3}+3\bigr),$$  |
L435: 
L436: and hence $z=-1-\sqrt[3]{3}$ is the unique root satisfying
L437: 
L438:  | $$|z|=1+\sqrt[3]{3}.$$  |
L439: 
L440: Thus the conclusion holds for $n=2,3,4$. Now assume that $n\geq 5$.
L441: 
L442: Let $z\in\mathbb{C}$ satisfy $|z|=r>\gamma_{n}$, and choose any vertex $u\in V(T)$. Since $\gamma_{n}>2$, Lemma cite41†3.1 gives
L443:  | $$|\Phi_{T-u}(z)|\leq B_{n}(r)|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|.$$  |
L444: 
L445: By Lemmas cite42†2.3 and cite43†2.6 ,
L446: 
L447:  | $$B_{n}(r)<B_{n}(\gamma_{n})=\frac{n-1}{a_{n}^{n-1}}=1.$$  |
L448: 
L449: Moreover, by Lemma cite40†2.1 , we have
L450: 
L451:  | $$|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|\geq r(r-1)^{n-1}>0.$$  |
L452: 
L453: Consequently,
L454: 
L455:  | $$|\Phi_{T-u}(z)|<|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|.$$  |
L456: 
L457: By Equation (cite44†2.1 ) and the reverse triangle inequality, we have
L458: 
L459:  | $$|\Phi_{T}(z)|\geq|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|-|\Phi_{T-u}(z)|>0.$$  |
L460: 
L461: Thus $\Phi_{T}(z)\neq 0$ whenever $|z|>\gamma_{n}$.
L462: We next characterize the boundary case. Suppose that $T$ is a tree of order $n$ such that $\Phi_{T}(z)=0$ and $|z|=\gamma_{n}$. For any vertex $u\in V(T)$, let $T_{1},\dots,T_{d}$ be the components of $T-u$. Let $u_{i}$ be the unique neighbor of $u$ in $T_{i}$, and set $n_{i}=|V(T_{i})|$ and $L_{i}=\Phi_{T_{i},u_{i}}(z)$. Then
L463: 
L464:  | $$(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})\in\mathcal{C}(n-1).$$  |
L465: 
L466: By Lemma cite40†2.1 , we have $|L_{i}|\geq\lambda_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})>1$. Hence, by Equation (cite39†2.2 ), we have
L467:  | $$|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|=\gamma_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{d}|1+L_{i}|\geq\gamma_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(|L_{i}|-1)>0.$$  |
L468: 
L469: Since $\Phi_{T}(z)=0$, Equation (cite44†2.1 ) yields $\Phi_{T-u}(z)=-\Phi_{T,u}(z)$, and therefore
L470: 
L471:  | $$\frac{|\Phi_{T-u}(z)|}{|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|}=1.$$  |
L472: 
L473: By Lemma cite41†3.1 , for each $i$,
L474: 
L475:  | $$|\Phi_{T_{i}-u_{i}}(z)|\leq B_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})|\Phi_{T_{i},u_{i}}(z)|.$$  |
L476: Replacing the induction-hypothesis Inequality (cite45†3.1 ) in the proof of Lemma cite41†3.1 by above inequalities and repeating the derivation of Inequality (cite46†3.2 ) with $r=\gamma_{n}$, we have
L477: 
L478:  | $$1=\frac{|\Phi_{T-u}(z)|}{|\Phi_{T,u}(z)|}\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})\bigl(1+B_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})\bigr)}{\gamma_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(\lambda_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})-1\bigr)}\leq B_{n}(\gamma_{n})=1.$$  |
L479: 
L480: Hence
L481:  | $$\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})\bigl(1+B_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})\bigr)}{\gamma_{n}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(\lambda_{n_{i}}(\gamma_{n})-1\bigr)}=B_{n}(\gamma_{n}),$$  |
L482: so the composition $(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathcal{C}(n-1)$ attains the maximum in the definition of $B_{n}(\gamma_{n})$. By the condition for equality in Lemma cite43†2.6 , we have $(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})=(1,\dots,1)$ or $(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})=(n-1)$, which implies that $d_{T}(u)\in\{1,n-1\}$. Since $u$ was arbitrary, every vertex of the tree $T$ has degree either $1$ or $n-1$. Thus $T\cong K_{1,n-1}$.
L483: 
L484: When $T\cong K_{1,n-1}$, we have
L485: 
L486:  | $$\Phi_{K_{1,n-1}}(z)=z\bigl((z+1)^{n-1}+n-1\bigr).$$  |
L487: Hence, its nonzero subtree roots are
L488: 
L489:  | $$z_{k}=a_{n}\cos\left(\frac{2k-1}{n-1}\pi\right)-1+\mathrm{i}\cdot a_{n}\sin\left(\frac{2k-1}{n-1}\pi\right),\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,n-1.$$  |
L490: 
L491: Then
L492: 
L493:  | $$|z_{k}|^{2}=a_{n}^{2}+1-2a_{n}\cos\left(\frac{2k-1}{n-1}\pi\right).$$  |
L494: 
L495: Thus $|z_{k}|=1+a_{n}$ if and only if
L496: 
L497:  | $$\cos\left(\frac{2k-1}{n-1}\pi\right)=-1,$$  |
L498: which is equivalent to $2k=n$. Consequently, there exists a nonzero subtree root of $K_{1,n-1}$ with modulus $1+a_{n}$ if and only if $n$ is even. In this case $k=n/2$, and hence the unique boundary root is $z_{n/2}=-1-a_{n}$. Conversely, if $n$ is even, then $k=n/2$ is an integer and $z_{n/2}=-1-a_{n}$ attains the upper bound.
L499: 
L500: This completes the proof. ∎
L501: ## 4 A lower bound and sharpness results
L502: 
L503: To establish a lower bound on the modulus of nonzero subtree roots, the following result is essential.
L504: 
L505: ###### Lemma 4.1.
L506: 
L507: [cite13†8 ] Let $T$ be a tree of order $n$. Then $s_{1}(T)=n$ and for $k=2,\ldots,n$,
L508: 
L509:  | $$n-k+1\leq s_{k}(T)\leq\binom{n-1}{k-1}.$$  |
L510: 
L511: We now give the lower bound on the modulus of nonzero subtree roots.
L512: ###### Theorem 4.2.
L513: 
L514: Let $T$ be a tree of order $n\geq 2$. Then every nonzero subtree root of $T$ satisfies
L515: 
L516:  | $$|z|>\sqrt[n-1]{n-1}-1.$$  |
L517: ###### Proof.
L518: 
L519: The conclusion is immediate for $n=2$. We now assume that $n\geq 3$. It suffices to prove that for $0<|z|\leq\sqrt[n-1]{n-1}-1$,
L520: 
L521:  | $$\left|\frac{\varPhi_{T}(z)}{z}\right|\geq 2.$$  |
L522: 
L523: Note that $s_{1}(T)=n$ for any tree $T$ of order $n$. By the reverse triangle inequality and Lemma cite47†4.1 , we have
L524:  | $\displaystyle\left|\frac{\varPhi_{T}(z)}{z}\right|$  | $\displaystyle=\left|s_{n}(T)z^{n-1}+s_{n-1}(T)z^{n-2}+\cdots+s_{2}(T)z+n\right|$  |
L525:  |  | $\displaystyle\geq n-\left(s_{n}(T)|z|^{n-1}+s_{n-1}(T)|z|^{n-2}+\cdots+s_{2}(T)|z|\right)$  |
L526:  |  | $\displaystyle\geq n-\left(\binom{n-1}{n-1}|z|^{n-1}+\binom{n-1}{n-2}|z|^{n-2}+\cdots+\binom{n-1}{1}|z|+1-1\right)$  |
L527:  |  | $\displaystyle=n+1-(|z|+1)^{n-1}$  |
L528:  |  | $\displaystyle\geq 2.$  |
L529: 
L530: This completes the proof. ∎
L531: We examine the sharpness of the upper and lower bounds on the modulus of subtree roots. In this section, for any integer $n\geq 2$, define
L532: 
L533:  | $$a_{n}=(n-1)^{1/(n-1)}.$$  |
L534: 
L535: Recall that for the star $K_{1,n-1}$,
L536: 
L537:  | $$\Phi_{K_{1,n-1}}(z)=z\bigl((z+1)^{n-1}+n-1\bigr),$$  |
L538: 
L539: and its nonzero subtree roots are
L540: 
L541:  | $$z_{k}=a_{n}\cos\left(\frac{2k-1}{n-1}\pi\right)-1+\mathrm{i}\cdot a_{n}\sin\left(\frac{2k-1}{n-1}\pi\right),\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,n-1.$$  |
L542: We first show that the upper bound in Theorem cite31†1.3 is asymptotically sharp as $n\to\infty$ through odd integers. Suppose that $n$ is odd, and set $k=(n-1)/2$. Then
L543: 
L544:  | $$\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{n-1}=\pi-\frac{\pi}{n-1},$$  |
L545: 
L546: and hence
L547: 
L548:  | $$z_{k}=-a_{n}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)-1+\mathrm{i}\cdot a_{n}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right).$$  |
L549: 
L550: It follows that
L551: 
L552:  | $\displaystyle|z_{k}|^{2}=(1+a_{n})^{2}-2a_{n}\left(1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)\right).$  |
L553: 
L554: Therefore,
L555:  | $$\frac{|z_{k}|^{2}}{(1+a_{n})^{2}}=1-\frac{2a_{n}\left(1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)\right)}{(1+a_{n})^{2}}.$$  |
L556: 
L557: Since $a_{n}\rightarrow 1$ and $1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)\rightarrow 0$ as $n\to\infty$, we have
L558: 
L559:  | $$\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
L560: n\ {\rm odd}\end{subarray}}\frac{|z_{k}|^{2}}{(1+a_{n})^{2}}=1,$$  |
L561: 
L562: and consequently
L563: 
L564:  | $$\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
L565: n\ {\rm odd}\end{subarray}}\frac{|z_{k}|}{1+a_{n}}=1.$$  |
L566: Thus the upper bound in Theorem 1.3 is asymptotically sharp as $n\to\infty$ through odd integers.
L567: 
L568: We next consider the lower bound in Theorem 4.2. Taking $k=1$, we have
L569: 
L570:  | $$z_{1}=a_{n}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)-1+\mathrm{i}\cdot a_{n}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right).$$  |
L571: 
L572: Then
L573: 
L574:  | $\displaystyle|z_{1}|^{2}=(a_{n}-1)^{2}+2a_{n}\left(1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)\right),$  |
L575: 
L576: and
L577:  | $$\frac{|z_{1}|^{2}}{(a_{n}-1)^{2}}=1+\frac{2a_{n}\left(1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)\right)}{(a_{n}-1)^{2}}.$$  |
L578: 
L579: Since $\frac{\ln(n-1)}{n-1}\rightarrow 0$ as $n\to\infty$, we have
L580: 
L581:  | $$a_{n}-1=e^{\frac{\ln(n-1)}{n-1}}-1\sim\frac{\ln(n-1)}{n-1}.$$  |
L582: 
L583: Moreover,
L584: 
L585:  | $$1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)\sim\frac{\pi^{2}}{2(n-1)^{2}}.$$  |
L586: 
L587: Since $a_{n}\to 1$ as $n\to\infty$, it follows that
L588:  | $$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2a_{n}\left(1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)\right)}{(a_{n}-1)^{2}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\cdot\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{2(n-1)^{2}}}{\left(\dfrac{\ln(n-1)}{n-1}\right)^{2}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\pi}{\ln(n-1)}\right)^{2}=0.$$  |
L589: 
L590: Therefore,
L591: 
L592:  | $$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|z_{1}|^{2}}{(a_{n}-1)^{2}}=1,$$  |
L593: 
L594: and consequently
L595: 
L596:  | $$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|z_{1}|}{a_{n}-1}=1.$$  |
L597: 
L598: Hence the lower bound in Theorem 4.2 is asymptotically sharp.
L599: ## 5 Concluding remarks
L600: 
L601: In this paper, we prove the Brown-Mol conjecture on subtree roots and completely characterize the equality case. We also establish an asymptotically sharp lower bound for the moduli of nonzero subtree roots.
L602: Brown and Mol observed numerically in [cite28†2 ] that the subtree roots appear to be distributed approximately around the point $-1/2$. They proposed the following conjecture, which strengthens Theorem cite31†1.3 , and verified it computationally for all trees of order at most $18$.
L603: ###### Conjecture 5.1.
L604: 
L605: [cite28†2 ] If $T$ is a tree of order $n\geq 2$, then the subtree roots of $T$ are contained in the annulus
L606: 
L607:  | $$\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\frac{1}{2}\leq\left|z+\frac{1}{2}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt[n-1]{n-1}\right\}.$$  |

The green circles $|z|=1+a_{13}$ and $|z|=a_{13}-1$ correspond to the bounds in Theorems cite31†1.3 and cite49†4.2 , respectively. The red circles $|z+1/2|=1/2$ and $|z+1/2|=1/2+a_{13}$ correspond to the two bounds in Conjecture cite50†5.1 , respectively.
L609: For comparison, Figure cite51†1 shows the subtree roots of all nonisomorphic trees of order $13$, together with the boundary circles arising from Theorems cite31†1.3 and cite49†4.2 and those appearing in Conjecture cite50†5.1 . The figure is included to illustrate the relationship between the bounds proved in this paper and the stronger translated annulus conjectured by Brown and Mol. We emphasize that Brown and Mol [cite28†2 ] carried out more extensive computations for all trees of order at most $18$.
L610: The method developed in this paper does not appear to extend directly to Conjecture cite50†5.1 . A different approach may therefore be required in the future.
