## Subsidiary theorem and scope

Let \(P=\mathrm{SW2}\) with the face numbering of Allcock--Devlin--
Felikson--Kontorovich--Whitehead, *Arithmetic Polyhedra*, arXiv:2609.05349v1.
If an ideal, right-angled \(P\)-polyhedron \(Q\) is the union of at most 84
chambers in the reflective \(P\)-tiling, then \(Q\) has exactly 48 chambers
and is isometric to the ideal right-angled octahedron \(\mathcal O\).
Consequently \(Q\) is tiled by one copy of \(\mathcal O\).

This is a finite subsidiary classification.  It neither bounds a minimal
counterexample above 84 chambers nor resolves Conjecture 7.11.

## Reduction to two exhaustive branches

Lemma 7.9 and Proposition 7.10 of the cited primary source give a dichotomy.
If every boundary face of \(Q\) is tiled by SW2 face 3, the canonical
decomposition is a reflective tiling by 12-chamber copies of
\(\tfrac14\mathcal O\).  If no boundary face is tiled by face 3, the canonical
blocks are 12-, 24-, or 48-chamber copies of
\(\tfrac14\mathcal O,\tfrac12\mathcal O,\mathcal O\), respectively, centered
at images of the finite 1-3-4 vertex.  Thus 84 SW2 chambers are seven
quarter-octahedron volume units, and the two certificates cover all cases.

The exact Lorentz model uses form \(J=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)\) and
oriented SW2 facet normals

\[
n_1=(0,-1,1,0),\quad n_2=(1,1,1,1),\quad
n_3=(0,0,0,-1),\quad n_4=(0,0,-1,1).
\]

Their Coxeter orders are exactly

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&2&2&3\\2&1&4&2\\2&4&1&4\\3&2&4&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The subgroup generated by faces 1, 3, 4 has 48 chambers.  Its outer planes
are \((1,\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)\), which recover the standard ideal right-angled
octahedron exactly.

## The all-face-3 branch

`sw2_quarter_census.py` constructs the no-face-3 quarter-octahedron and checks
that its scale-free facet Gram matrix is isomorphic to that of the
all-face-3 quarter.  It then enumerates every rooted convex union of at most
seven chambers in the reflective quarter-octahedron Coxeter tiling.

For completeness, a connected union of at most seven chambers containing the
base chamber lies in the complete Cayley ball of radius six.  Closure under
all chambers on all minimal galleries is the Coxeter-complex
intersection-of-roots criterion for convexity.  Repeatedly adjoining every
adjacent target chamber and taking this closure therefore reaches every
rooted convex target by induction.  The exact counts at sizes 1 through 7 are

\[
1,\ 6,\ 18,\ 57,\ 150,\ 450,\ 1218.
\]

Only three rooted states survive the ideal/right-angle tests, all at size
four; they are symmetry duplicates of an ideal right-angled octahedron.

## The no-face-3 mixed-block branch

Every SW2 chamber contains a unique image of the 1-3-4 vertex, so the source's
canonical blocks partition the chamber set by their B3 center.  The mixed
certificate independently reconstructs all possible local blocks as follows.

At a fixed center there are six unoriented B3 mirrors of boundary type
\(\{1,4\}\), hence twelve oriented inward half-spaces.  Cutting the central
48-chamber octahedron by each of them gives exactly the twelve oriented
24-chamber halves.  Intersecting every orthogonal pair of these half-spaces
and retaining 12-chamber sectors gives exactly the twelve oriented quarters.
Together with the full star these are precisely 25 variants.  Their oriented
cut-plane signatures are pairwise distinct.  Among variants containing a
fixed chamber there are three quarters, six halves, and one full octahedron;
these are exactly the ten choices tested at each expansion.

It remains to justify rejecting an interface mismatch before the convexity
test.  Let \(e_0=(1,0,0,0)\) be the base B3 center and \(R_2\) reflection in
the type-2 plane.  Since \(\langle n_2,n_2\rangle=2\),

\[
R_2e_0=(2,1,1,1),\qquad
\langle n_2,e_0\rangle=-1,\qquad
\langle n_2,R_2e_0\rangle=1.
\]

Lorentz equivariance gives the same signs at every translated interface.  If
a chamber has an unmatched type-2 facet while the neighboring B3 center is
represented elsewhere in the assembly, that center lies strictly beyond the
facet's oriented half-space.  The assembly cannot be convex.  Hence every
convex canonical assembly necessarily passes `interfaces_match`.

The block adjacency graph of any connected chamber union is connected:
within-center blocks are connected, and only a type-2 chamber adjacency
changes the B3 center.  Starting from one representative of each block size,
the program adjoins every neighboring center and every one of its ten local
variants that contains the required interface chambers.  Induction on the
connected block graph therefore enumerates every compatible assembly within
the volume bound.  A state is encoded by its centers and oriented local cut
planes; injectivity of all 25 local signatures makes this descriptor complete.
Normalizing in turn at every chamber gives a complete quotient by left
translation in the SW2 reflection group.

The resulting interface-compatible symmetry-class counts for one through
seven quarter units are

\[
1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 17,\ 52,\ 235,\ 1054.
\]

Exactly \(1,2,2,10,11,35,69\) of these, respectively, are convex.  Convexity
is checked by requiring every unmatched chamber facet to support every strict
chamber interior point.  This is necessary and sufficient because a Coxeter
chamber lies wholly on the side of any mirror selected by one strict interior
point.  Vertices are then recovered from every independent triple of integer
Lorentz planes; their exact norm distinguishes finite from ideal vertices,
and exact inner products test boundary right angles.

There are exactly two ideal right-angled survivors.  Both have four quarter
units, 48 SW2 chambers, eight facets, twelve edges, and six four-valent ideal
vertices.  Their block patterns are `[4]` and `[1,1,1,1]`.  For each survivor,
the full ordered Gram matrix of its eight oriented boundary normals equals
that of \((1,\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)\).  The listed normals span Lorentz four-space
(the stored 4-by-4 minor has determinant \(-8\)); Gram equality therefore
extends to a Lorentz isometry carrying all oriented half-spaces to those of
the standard octahedron.  Thus both survivors are copies of \(\mathcal O\).

Combining this with the all-face-3 branch proves the stated theorem.
