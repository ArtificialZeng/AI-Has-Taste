## Objects and definitions

Fix an integer `n >= 3` and write `[n] = {1,...,n}`. A two-black-square
configuration is an unordered two-element subset

`B = {(i_1,j_1),(i_2,j_2)} \subset [n] x [n]`.

The associated `n x n` crossword grid `G_B` has precisely the squares of `B`
black and every other square white. Rows and columns use matrix coordinates.
As in Section 2 of Lewis--Won, no American-crossword conditions are imposed:
there is no rotational-symmetry, connectivity, or minimum-word-length
condition, and words of length one or two are allowed.

An **across word** is a maximal nonempty contiguous interval of white squares
in one row; a **down word** is the analogous interval in one column. A complete
non-attacking rook placement is a subset `R` of the white squares that meets
every across word and every down word exactly once. `RP(G_B)` is the finite set
of all such `R`. Equivalently, form the bipartite graph whose two vertex parts
are the across and down words, with one edge for each white square at their
intersection. Then `RP(G_B)` is its set of perfect matchings and
`|RP(G_B)|` is the permanent of its biadjacency matrix. In particular the
count is zero when the two word classes have unequal cardinalities.

## Quantified target

For every integer `n >= 3`, define

`M_2(n) = max_{B \subset [n] x [n], |B|=2} |RP(G_B)|`.

Determine `M_2(n)` by a closed exact expression valid for every such `n`, with
separate parity cases and finitely many explicitly stated exceptional small
values allowed. Also determine every maximizing unordered configuration `B`,
modulo the group generated by horizontal reflection, vertical reflection, and
matrix transposition. On a square grid this is the full eight-element dihedral
symmetry group; configurations in different orbits must not be identified.

The requested proof must cover every relative-position type of the two distinct
black squares (including shared rows or columns, edge/corner positions,
edge-adjacent squares, and separated interior squares), give exact counting or
permanent formulas for the relevant types, and prove the resulting discrete
maximum for all `n`. Exact exhaustive enumeration for each `3 <= n <= 12`, by
two independently implemented counting methods and with symmetry-orbit checks,
is regression evidence only. It neither proves the all-`n` assertion nor may it
be used to infer an omitted equality case. A single larger configuration or a
single missing maximizing orbit refutes a proposed answer.

## Source status and nearest prior results

The source status is **open-supported**, not proved open in an absolute sense.
The primary paper is Joel Brewster Lewis and Robert Won, *Non-attacking rook
placements on crossword grids*, arXiv:2609.03081v1
(<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03081v1>), retrieved 2026-09-07. The local PDF hash
was verified as `bba65269d01007a2e51349e035181c869df993fc3cd0651716490d550c8a8911`.
Its Section 2, Question 2.2 (printed page 5) asks the extremal question for each
fixed number `k` of black squares, so the frozen claim is exactly its first
untreated specialization `k=2`. Immediately before that question, the paper
gives exact counts for `k=0` and for all placements of one black square, and
maximizes the latter. Conjecture 2.4 (printed page 6) proposes, for `k=o(n)`,
the general upper bound `n^(4k) 2^(-4k) (n-3k)!` with asymptotic attainment.
Proposition 3.1 (printed page 8) identifies placements on sparse grids with no
edge black squares with alternating-sign matrices having `-1`s at the black
positions; this supplies an equivalent language for part of the `k=2` case but
not its extremal formula or equality classification.

A targeted primary-literature search on 2026-09-07 using the exact title and
combinations of “crossword rook placements,” “fixed black squares,” and
“permanent” located the cited arXiv paper but no separate paper resolving its
two-black-square specialization. This limited negative search is not a proof of
novelty or priority.
