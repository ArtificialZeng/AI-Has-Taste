\begin{abstract}
For $m,n\ge2$, let $\Gamma_{m\oplus n}=\{(g,h)\in\Sym_m\times\Sym_n:\sgn(g)=\sgn(h)\}$. Fernandes conjectured that this group has rank two apart from four listed small cases, one of which has rank one. We prove the conjecture. More precisely, for $m\ge n\ge2$, $\Gamma_{m\oplus n}$ is two-generated unless $(m,n)$ is one of $(3,3),(4,3),(4,4)$; these three groups have rank three, while $\Gamma_{2\oplus2}$ has rank one. The proof gives explicit generators and uses only an elementary subdirect-product lemma and the normal subgroups of symmetric groups.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and notation}
Write permutations as functions, with the right factor applied first. For $k\ge2$ set
\[
t_k=(1\ 2),\quad c_k=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ k),\quad
e_k=\begin{cases}c_k,&k\text{ odd},\\t_kc_k,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Then $t_k$ is odd, $e_k$ is even, and $\langle t_k,e_k\rangle=\Sym_k$: this is the familiar generating pair $t_k,c_k$, since $c_k=t_ke_k$ in the even case.

Fernandes introduced the groups $\Gamma_{m\oplus n}$ in the above form, proved a universal three-generator bound, determined several infinite families, and posed the rank-two conjecture \cite{Fernandes2026}. His exact-rank formulation excludes $(2,2)$ because $\Gamma_{2\oplus2}\cong C_2$. If ``two-generated'' means generated by at most two elements, that cyclic case satisfies the conclusion.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $m\ge n\ge2$. The group $\Gamma_{m\oplus n}$ is generated by two elements if and only if
\[
(m,n)\notin\bigl\{(3,3),(4,3),(4,4)\bigr\}.
\]
Its rank is one for $(m,n)=(2,2)$, two in every other nonexceptional case, and three in the three exceptional cases.
\end{theorem}

\section{Subdirect products}
We record the needed form of Goursat's argument, including its short proof.
\begin{lemma}[Subdirect-product lemma]\label{lem:goursat}
Let $H\le G\times K$ project onto both factors, and put
\[
N_G=\{g\in G:(g,1)\in H\},\qquad N_K=\{k\in K:(1,k)\in H\}.
\]
Then $N_G\trianglelefteq G$, $N_K\trianglelefteq K$, and there is an isomorphism $\phi:G/N_G\to K/N_K$ for which
\[
H=\{(g,k):\phi(gN_G)=kN_K\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Normality follows by lifting a conjugating element to $H$. If $(g,k)\in H$, define $\phi(gN_G)=kN_K$. Multiplying two possible lifts by inverses shows that this is well defined; the same argument with the factors reversed gives injectivity and surjectivity. The displayed description is immediate.
\end{proof}

We use the standard normal-subgroup lists
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
G&\text{normal subgroups}\\ \hline
\Sym_2&1,\Sym_2\\
\Sym_3&1,\Alt_3,\Sym_3\\
\Sym_4&1,V_4,\Alt_4,\Sym_4\\
\Sym_k\ (k\ge5)&1,\Alt_k,\Sym_k.
\end{array}
\]
For completeness, the last line follows from simplicity of $\Alt_k$: if $N\trianglelefteq\Sym_k$, then $N\cap\Alt_k$ is either $1$ or $\Alt_k$; in the first case $[N,\Alt_k]=1$, and the trivial centralizer of $\Alt_k$ in $\Sym_k$ gives $N=1$, while the other cases are immediate. The lists for $k\le4$ are checked directly.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:obstruction}
Suppose $H\le\Gamma_{m\oplus n}$ projects onto both symmetric factors. If $m\ne n$, then $H=\Gamma_{m\oplus n}$ unless $\{m,n\}=\{4,3\}$. If $m=n=k\ge5$, then either $H=\Gamma_{k\oplus k}$ or $H$ is the graph of an automorphism of $\Sym_k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In Lemma~\ref{lem:goursat}, containment in $\Gamma_{m\oplus n}$ forces $N_{\Sym_m}\le\Alt_m$ and $N_{\Sym_n}\le\Alt_n$. The normal-subgroup lists show that, for unequal degrees, the only common nontrivial quotient larger than the common sign quotient is $\Sym_4/V_4\cong\Sym_3$. Thus outside $\{m,n\}=\{4,3\}$ both kernels are the alternating groups, and the resulting fiber product is precisely $\Gamma_{m\oplus n}$. For equal $k\ge5$, the only remaining proper choice has both kernels trivial and hence gives an automorphism graph. (The trivial common quotient would give the full direct product, which is not contained in $\Gamma_{k\oplus k}$.)
\end{proof}

\section{Explicit generators}
First suppose $m>n\ge2$ and $(m,n)\ne(4,3)$. Both
\[
x=(t_m,t_n),\qquad y=(e_m,e_n) \tag{1}
\]
belong to $\Gamma_{m\oplus n}$, and their two projections generate the corresponding symmetric groups. Lemma~\ref{lem:obstruction} therefore says $\langle x,y\rangle=\Gamma_{m\oplus n}$.

Now let $m=n=k\ge5$. Use instead
\[
x=(t_k,t_ke_k),\qquad y=(e_k,e_k). \tag{2}
\]
These elements again have matching coordinate parities, and both projected pairs generate $\Sym_k$. If their subgroup were a proper automorphism graph, some $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Sym_k)$ would satisfy $\alpha(t_k)=t_ke_k$. This is impossible: $t_k$ has order two, whereas
\[
\operatorname{ord}(t_ke_k)=\begin{cases}k-1,&k\text{ odd},\\k,&k\text{ even},\end{cases}
\]
which is greater than two. This order argument also covers the outer automorphisms of $\Sym_6$.

Finally $\Gamma_{2\oplus2}=\langle(t_2,t_2)\rangle$. We have proved two-generation in every claimed case. Apart from $(2,2)$, the group maps onto a noncyclic symmetric factor, so its rank is exactly two.

\section{The exceptional groups}
We include short non-generation proofs, so no computational assertion is needed to complete the classification.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:exceptions}
None of $\Gamma_{3\oplus3}$, $\Gamma_{4\oplus3}$, and $\Gamma_{4\oplus4}$ is two-generated.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that two elements generate $\Gamma_{3\oplus3}$. Their parity values generate $C_2$. By invertible Nielsen moves (interchanging the generators and replacing one by its product with the other), arrange their parities as odd and even. The odd generator has two transposition coordinates and hence squares to one. The even generator lies in $\Alt_3\times\Alt_3\cong C_3^2$; both coordinates are nontrivial because each projected pair generates $\Sym_3$. Conjugation by the odd generator inverts both coordinates. The generated subgroup therefore meets $C_3^2$ only in the cyclic group generated by that even element, not in all of $C_3^2$, a contradiction.

For the other cases let $q:\Sym_4\to\Sym_4/V_4\cong\Sym_3$. The sign of $q(g)$ equals the sign of $g$, as is seen on a transposition. We use the following elementary observation: two ordered generating pairs of $\Sym_3$ with the same entrywise parity vector differ by an automorphism of $\Sym_3$. Indeed, after applying the same Nielsen normalization determined by that nonzero parity vector, each pair consists of a transposition followed by a three-cycle; all such ordered pairs are simultaneously conjugate. Reversing the Nielsen moves proves the observation in the original coordinates.

If two elements generated $\Gamma_{4\oplus3}$, their $q$-images in the first coordinate and their second-coordinate images would be generating pairs of $\Sym_3$ with equal parity vectors. The observation identifies them by an automorphism, so the generated group lies in the proper fiber product imposing this additional equality of $\Sym_3$ quotients. The same argument, applying $q$ to both coordinates, treats $\Gamma_{4\oplus4}$. Thus neither group is two-generated.
\end{proof}

Fernandes's three-generator construction \cite[Proposition~3]{Fernandes2026} applies to every $m,n\ge2$. Proposition~\ref{prop:exceptions} therefore shows that the three exceptional ranks are exactly three and completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section*{Reproducibility note}
The proof is entirely deductive and does not depend on a computer calculation. Exact finite-group scripts accompanying this article independently check the displayed generators and the exceptional low-degree groups; those checks are diagnostic corroboration, not proof dependencies.
