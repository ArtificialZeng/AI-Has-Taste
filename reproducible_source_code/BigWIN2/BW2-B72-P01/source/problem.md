# Precise problem reading

## Frozen mathematical target

Let
\[
b=(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^4,
\qquad
a=(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^4,
\]
where `positive` means strictly positive and both tuples are ordered. Define the
integer signatures
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_b(b)&=\bigl(
b_0+b_1+b_2+b_3,\\
&\quad 3b_0b_1-5b_0b_3+3b_1b_2+3b_2b_3,\\
&\quad 8b_0b_3(b_1+b_2),\\
&\quad 4b_0b_1b_2b_3\bigr),\\
\sigma_a(a)&=\bigl(
a_0+a_1+a_2+a_3,\\
&\quad 3(a_0a_1+a_0a_2+a_0a_3+a_1a_2),\\
&\quad 2a_1a_2(a_3-2a_0),\\
&\quad a_0a_1a_2a_3\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
A solution is exactly an ordered pair \((b,a)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^4\times
\mathbb Z_{>0}^4\) for which \(\sigma_b(b)=\sigma_a(a)\). Write
\[
\mathcal S_s=\{(b,a):\sigma_b(b)=\sigma_a(a),\ 
\textstyle\sum_i b_i=\sum_i a_i=s\}.
\]
The requested quantities are
\[
s_{\min}=\min\{s\in\mathbb Z_{\ge4}:\mathcal S_s\ne\varnothing\}
\quad\text{and the complete ordered set }\mathcal S_{s_{\min}}.
\]
The minimum exists because the source supplies a solution at \(s=61\).

No zero, negative, rational, or real coordinates are allowed. Equality is
literal integer equality, not numerical tolerance. Since the left side of the
third coefficient equation is positive, every solution necessarily satisfies
\(a_3>2a_0\); this is a consequence and may be used for pruning, not a change
of domain. Also \(s\ge4\) and the known witness gives \(s_{\min}\le61\).

## Classification convention

The indices are fixed labels inherited from the indicated vertex orderings of
the two base graphs. The full ordered list must be produced first. Afterwards,
one may additionally report orbits under only the commuting involutions
\[
R_b(b,a)=((b_3,b_2,b_1,b_0),a),\qquad
R_a(b,a)=(b,(a_0,a_2,a_1,a_3)).
\]
Thus the permitted reporting group is generated by \(R_b,R_a\), of order at
most four on any pair. No other coordinate permutation, graph isomorphism,
scaling, or unordered-tuple identification is permitted.

## Scope and relation to the source

The target concerns only the positive-integer coefficient system (3.3) for the
specified \(P_4\) and paw independent-set blow-ups. It does **not** ask for the
least order among every connected distance-cospectral graph pair of opposite
bipartiteness, nor for all positive solutions of (3.3) at every order.

The nearest prior result is Gong--Wang--Wang, *The Distance Spectrum Does Not
Determine Bipartiteness*, arXiv:2608.17311v1. Equations (3.1)--(3.3) establish
that signature equality is precisely coefficientwise equality of the reduced
quartics (once the common order is matched), and Theorem 3.1 proves the witness
\[
b=(1,16,8,36),\qquad a=(4,9,16,32).
\]
Direct integer substitution gives the common signature
\((61,1116,6912,18432)\). The paper proves existence at 61, but does not prove
absence below 61 or classify the solutions on the 61-layer; its Conclusion
instead lists minimum order and classification of positive solutions as
follow-up problems.

Official arXiv abstract and HTML were inspected on 2026-09-08:

- <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17311> (submission history showed v1 only);
- <https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17311> (equations (3.1)--(3.3), Theorem 3.1,
  and Conclusion).

Exact web searches on 2026-09-08 for the title/arXiv ID, both displayed tuples,
the phrase “coefficient-matching equations,” and the minimum-order formulation
found no additional primary paper resolving this restricted least-layer target.
That limited negative search is not a priority or open-status proof. The source
supports the broader follow-up; the restricted least-layer question is best
treated as a precise, currently status-uncertain refinement until a fuller
literature audit is done.

## Proposed contribution and decisive route

Nearest prior result: an explicit order-61 solution. Proposed delta: prove the
sharp least sum and classify every ordered pair on that least layer. Verification
route: independently enumerate each side's positive ordered four-tuples with
sum at most 61, compute the four-component signatures exactly, and join equal
signatures. Each side has
\(\sum_{s=4}^{61}\binom{s-1}{3}=\binom{61}{4}=521855\) tuples, so this is a
bounded exhaustive test. A second independently implemented verifier must check
coverage, all hits, and nonexistence below the first hit before the mathematical
claim can advance to review.
