## Frozen assertion

Work over `Q` with exactly the source's Section 3.2 definitions. The algebra `R_0`
has basis `z_i=q^i/(1-q)^i` (`i>=0`), and the corresponding formal symbols are
identified with `z_i=x^i z_0` in `H=Q<x,z_0>`. Put
`H^0=Q direct-sum xHz_0`, identified with `QS(R_0^+,R_0)`, and give it the
induced quasi-shuffle product `*`. Let `tau` be the anti-involution
`tau(uv)=tau(v)tau(u)`, `tau(x)=z_0`, `tau(z_0)=x`, and define

```
Z_f^* = H_*^0 / (tau(u)-u : u in H^0).
```

Give this quotient the induced filtration from
`wt(z_{s_1}...z_{s_k}) = sum_i max(s_i,1)`. Theorem 3.5 makes the q-series map
descend to a canonical surjection `Z:Z_f^* -> Z_{q,0}`. Prove or disprove

```
ker(Z restricted to W_8 Z_f^*) = {0}.
```

Equivalently, every rational linear combination of admissible words of total
weight at most eight that maps to the zero formal q-series must already belong
to the quasi-shuffle ideal generated by all duality relations `tau(u)-u`.

## Nearest result and exact delta

Theorem 3.5 proves every duality relation maps to zero. Conjecture 3.6 states that
these relations generate the entire kernel in all weights. The frozen assertion
is only its weight-at-most-eight instance. Equation (44) is conjectural and cannot
be used as a proved quotient dimension.

The source attributes the global conjecture to Takeyama. Triage must check the
primary Takeyama 2013 source and later computational work for any previously
verified range. If weight eight is already covered, the correct outcome is known
result/parked, not rediscovery; increasing the bound requires a new cost estimate.

## Cheapest decisive attack

1. Enumerate every admissible word of weight at most eight.
2. Compute `I intersect W_8`, where `I` is the full `*`-ideal generated by the
   duality relations; include all quasi-shuffle multiples needed in this weight.
3. Produce a checkable rational basis for `W_8 Z_f^*`.
4. Expand each basis image exactly in `Q[[q]]` and add coefficient rows until the
   rational matrix has full column rank or a stable relation candidate appears.

Full column rank of one finite coefficient matrix proves injectivity on this
finite-dimensional domain. Rank deficiency at any finite truncation proves
nothing by itself. A disproof requires a quotient-nonzero exact element together
with a symbolic proof that every q coefficient vanishes.

## Failure boundaries

- Bare spans of `tau(u)-u` are not the full quasi-shuffle ideal.
- Floating-point rank is not a certificate.
- Equation (44) is conditional, not a known target dimension.
- A bounded verification does not settle Conjecture 3.6 globally.
