# Exact proof: the first independent-row K-basis closure failure is in degree four

## Statement and scope

Use exactly the independent-row convention in `problem.md`: for
\(\alpha=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\vdash n\), the subgroup \(G_\alpha\leq S_n\)
is the direct product of the cyclic groups generated by the disjoint row cycles.
Then:

1. For every \(n\leq 3\) and every \(\alpha,\beta\vdash n\), the species
   \(\mathbf K_\alpha\times\mathbf K_\beta\) is a nonnegative integral
   coproduct of independent-row species \(\mathbf K_\lambda\).
2. In degree four no such coproduct exists for
   \(\mathbf K_{(2,2)}\times\mathbf K_{(4)}\).  Its unique cycle-index
   expansion in the degree-four independent-row K-basis is
   \[
   2Z_{\mathbf K_{(2,2)}}-2Z_{\mathbf K_{(2,1,1)}}
      +2Z_{\mathbf K_{(1,1,1,1)}}.
   \]

Thus degree four is the first failure in the precise sense frozen in
`problem.md`.  This does not decide whether a closed formula exists in a larger
molecular-species basis and does not settle every aspect of Open Problem 5.1.

## Coset-product lemma

Let \(S=S_n\) and let \(H,L\leq S\).  On the standard label set, the structures
of \(X^n/H\) form the transitive left \(S\)-set \(S/H\).  Every diagonal
\(S\)-orbit in \(S/H\times S/L\) has a representative \((H,rL)\).  Two such
representatives have the same orbit precisely when their parameters lie in the
same double coset \(HrL\), and
\[
 \operatorname{Stab}_S(H,rL)=H\cap rLr^{-1}.
\]
Consequently there is a species isomorphism
\[
 (X^n/H)\times(X^n/L)
 \cong \coprod_{r\in H\backslash S/L} X^n/(H\cap rLr^{-1}). \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, left translation sends any pair to the displayed form; the equivalence
and stabilizer assertions follow directly from the coset equalities.  This also
proves the usual size formula
\[
 |HrL|=\frac{|H||L|}{|H\cap rLr^{-1}|}. \tag{2}
\]

## The two degree-four orbits

Put
\[
 H=G_{(2,2)}=\langle(12),(34)\rangle,\qquad
 L=G_{(4)}=\langle(1234)\rangle,
 \quad \pi=(23).
\]
For the identity representative, \(H\cap L=\{e\}\): the square of the
generator of \(L\) is \((13)(24)\), while the nonidentity elements of \(H\)
are \((12),(34),(12)(34)\).  Thus (2) gives \(|HL|=16\).

Conjugation by \(\pi\) sends the generator of \(L\) to \((1324)\), whose
square is \((12)(34)\).  The two order-four elements of this conjugate cyclic
group are not in \(H\), so
\[
 H\cap\pi L\pi^{-1}=\{e,(12)(34)\}
\]
and \(|H\pi L|=8\).  The two double cosets are distinct (their sizes differ),
and their sizes sum to \(|S_4|=24\), so they exhaust \(S_4\).  Formula (1)
therefore gives the more precise molecular decomposition
\[
 \mathbf K_{(2,2)}\times\mathbf K_{(4)}
 \cong \mathbf K_{(1,1,1,1)}
 \;\coprod\; X^4/\langle(12)(34)\rangle. \tag{3}
\]
The second stabilizer is not conjugate to any independent-row \(G_\lambda\).
Indeed the orders of the five degree-four row groups are respectively
\(4,3,4,2,1\), and the only order-two one is
\(G_{(2,1,1)}\), generated by a transposition.  A double transposition is not
conjugate to a transposition.

## Cycle-index calculation and uniqueness

In the power-sum order
\((p_4,p_{31},p_{22},p_{211},p_{1^4})\), the five row-group cycle indices are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Z_{\mathbf K_4}&=\tfrac14(p_{1^4}+p_{22}+2p_4),\\
 Z_{\mathbf K_{31}}&=\tfrac13(p_{1^4}+2p_{31}),\\
 Z_{\mathbf K_{22}}&=\tfrac14(p_{1^4}+2p_{211}+p_{22}),\\
 Z_{\mathbf K_{211}}&=\tfrac12(p_{1^4}+p_{211}),\\
 Z_{\mathbf K_{1^4}}&=p_{1^4}.
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
The matrix whose columns are (4), in partition order
\((4),(31),(22),(211),(1^4)\), is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1/2&0&0&0&0\\
 0&2/3&0&0&0\\
 1/4&0&1/4&0&0\\
 0&0&1/2&1/2&0\\
 1/4&1/3&1/4&1/2&1
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
with determinant \(1/24\).  Hence these five cycle indices are linearly
independent.

Using \(p_\lambda\star p_\mu=\delta_{\lambda\mu}z_\lambda p_\lambda\),
\(z_{1^4}=24\), and \(z_{22}=8\), the only common types in
\(Z_{\mathbf K_{22}}\) and \(Z_{\mathbf K_4}\) give
\[
 Z_{\mathbf K_{22}}\star Z_{\mathbf K_4}
   =\tfrac32p_{1^4}+\tfrac12p_{22}. \tag{5}
\]
This also follows from (3), since the second summand there has cycle index
\(\tfrac12(p_{1^4}+p_{22})\).

Solving (5) in (4), the \(p_4,p_{31},p_{22},p_{211},p_{1^4}\) coefficients in
turn force
\[
 (c_4,c_{31},c_{22},c_{211},c_{1^4})=(0,0,2,-2,2). \tag{6}
\]
By linear independence, (6) is the unique K-cycle-index expansion.  If the
species were a coproduct with nonnegative multiplicities, its cycle index would
give a nonnegative expansion, contradicting the coefficient \(-2\).  This proves
the degree-four failure at the species level; no converse injectivity assertion
about arbitrary cycle indices is being used.

## Complete verification below degree four

The coset-product lemma gives the following species isomorphisms.  These tables
list all unordered pairs, since the Cartesian product is symmetric.

For \(n=1\):
\[
 \mathbf K_1\times\mathbf K_1\cong\mathbf K_1.
\]

For \(n=2\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\times&\mathbf K_2&\mathbf K_{11}\\\hline
\mathbf K_2&\mathbf K_2&\mathbf K_{11}\\
\mathbf K_{11}&&2\mathbf K_{11}
\end{array}
\]
Here \(G_2=S_2\) and \(G_{11}=1\), so the assertions are immediate from (1).

For \(n=3\), write \(A=G_3=A_3\), \(B=G_{21}\) (a transposition subgroup),
and \(T=G_{111}=1\).  Formula (1) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\times&\mathbf K_3&\mathbf K_{21}&\mathbf K_{111}\\\hline
\mathbf K_3&2\mathbf K_3&\mathbf K_{111}&2\mathbf K_{111}\\
\mathbf K_{21}&&\mathbf K_{21}+\mathbf K_{111}&3\mathbf K_{111}\\
\mathbf K_{111}&&&6\mathbf K_{111}.
\end{array} \tag{7}
\]
For completeness: \(A\) is normal of index two, giving two \(A\)-stabilized
orbits in the \((A,A)\) product; \(|A||B|=6\) and every conjugate of \(B\)
meets \(A\) trivially, giving one free orbit for \((A,B)\); and for \((B,B)\)
the identity double coset has stabilizer \(B\) and size two, while the remaining
double coset has trivial stabilizer and size four.  Pairing any subgroup with
\(T\) gives its index many free orbits.  Thus every entry of (7) is a genuine
species decomposition, not merely a cycle-index identity.

Together with (6), these exhaustive tables prove that degree four is first.

## Reproducible exact audit

The hand proof above is independently backed by two exact implementations.

- `degree4_exact_enumerator.py` constructs all row groups, explicitly partitions
  every \(S_n\) into double cosets for all 39 ordered partition pairs with
  \(1\leq n\leq4\), records all stabilizer elements and conjugators, computes
  rational cycle indices, and solves every product expansion.  Its certificate
  is `degree4_exact_certificate.json` (SHA-256
  `cbbb3ea7f10dc65a1a3c106644387cca3f89bcb058ee473e000fe56e9556a03f`).
- `degree4_independent_replay.py` imports no producer code.  It constructs row
  groups as Cartesian products of rotations, recomputes double cosets as BFS
  orbits of the \(H\times L\) action, and solves the basis systems using Leibniz
  determinants and Cramer's rule.  It replayed 39 ordered pairs, 167 double
  cosets, and 663 recorded double-coset element memberships; the passed report is
  `degree4_replay_report.json`.

Both computations use exact integers and `fractions.Fraction`; neither uses
floating-point arithmetic.  Their degree-wise basis determinants are
\(1,1/2,1/3,1/24\).  They also explicitly identify every orbit stabilizer for
\(n\leq3\) with a unique row group and find no row-group conjugate for the target
double-transposition stabilizer.
