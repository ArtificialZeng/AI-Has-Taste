\begin{abstract}
No accepted resolution of the original Latin Tableau/CDS-colorability problem
was located in our bounded search through 4 September 2026; within that stated
literature frontier the original problem remains unresolved, and the two
conjectures formulated here are unproved.  We isolate the restrictions
to Young shapes having at most two or at most three distinct positive row
lengths.  Exact computations supply direct CDS colorings for 5,252 recorded
instances (5,127 distinct shapes), feasible block allocations for a complete
8,478-instance three-level boundary, and further adversarial suites aimed
specifically at the two restrictions.  These finite results, together with
elementary boundary constructions and an unaudited owner-context
block-allocation argument, motivate the conjectures but do not prove an
infinite family.  We record failed coloring rules, the strongest
counterexample attacks, the bounded literature search, the unsearched region,
and direct routes by which either conjecture could be falsified.
\end{abstract}

\section{Original problem and verified frontier}

A finite Young shape is a partition
\[
  \lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r),
  \qquad \lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_r\geq 1.
\]
Its cells are the pairs $(i,j)$ with $0\leq i<r$ and
$0\leq j<\lambda_{i+1}$.  The rook graph $R_\lambda$ has these cells as
vertices, with two different cells adjacent when they share a row or a
column.  For a finite graph $G$, set $\rho_0(G)=0$ and
\[
 \rho_k(G)=\max\bigl\{|I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_k|:
 I_1,\ldots,I_k\text{ are independent sets of }G\bigr\}
 \quad(k\geq1),
\]
and write $\delta_k(G)=\rho_k(G)-\rho_{k-1}(G)$.  Trailing zero
increments are suppressed.

The original conjecture asks whether, for every finite Young shape
$\lambda$, there is one proper coloring
$c:Y_\lambda\to\mathbb N_0$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \bigl|\{v:c(v)<k\}\bigr|=\rho_k(R_\lambda)
 \qquad\text{for every integer }k\geq1.
 \label{eq:ltc-cds}
\end{equation}
The use of zero-based colors is essential for the threshold formulation.
Equivalently, after ordering the color classes by nonincreasing size, their
sizes are the full chromatic difference sequence
$\delta(R_\lambda)$.  The empty shape satisfies the statement
vacuously, but the two restricted conjectures below are deliberately stated
for nonempty shapes.

Let $B_\lambda$ be the Ferrers bipartite graph whose left vertices are rows,
whose right vertices are columns, and whose edges are cells.  Then
$R_\lambda=L(B_\lambda)$, so a CDS coloring is an edge-coloring of
$B_\lambda$ into matchings of sizes $\delta_1,\delta_2,\ldots$.
The exact corner formula used throughout the computations is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_k(R_\lambda)=
 \min_{0\leq i\leq r,\ 0\leq j\leq\lambda_1}
 \left(k(i+j)+\sum_{t=i+1}^{r}\max(\lambda_t-j,0)\right).
 \label{eq:corner}
\end{equation}
This is the partition-graph corner formula stated as Theorem~14 by
Chow and Tiefenbruck~\cite{ChowTiefenbruck2025}.

The source paper reports a computation for shapes inside a $12$ by $12$
square and proves that, for every shape, the first four CDS parts can be
attained simultaneously; it leaves the full conjecture open
\cite{ChowTiefenbruck2025}.  A recorded search through 4 September 2026
found no accepted proof or counterexample to the full CDS-colorability
statement.  The older wide-partition problem
\cite{ChowFanGoemansVondrak2003} and the allocation result for wide Young
diagrams with three distinct row lengths
\cite{AllsopKotlarWanless2025} concern a weaker Latin-filling requirement,
not realization of every CDS part.  Thus those results do not prove either
conjecture in Section~\ref{sec:conjectures}.  Accordingly, within the stated
accepted-literature search boundary, the original problem
\eqref{eq:ltc-cds} remains unresolved.

\section{Research routes and failure diagnosis}

\subsection{Compatible matching layers}

Formula~\eqref{eq:corner} computes the best union of $k$ matchings for each
$k$ separately, but separate optima need not be nested or split into one
compatible sequence of matching layers.  This is the fatal gap in the direct
max-flow route.  An exact obstruction occurs for
$\lambda=(5,4,3,3)$.  If the first matching is forced to be
\[
 \{(0,4),(1,3),(2,0),(3,1)\},
\]
then, on deletion, column $3$ has the unique residual edge $(0,3)$.
Every residual matching of size $4$ must use that edge, so two disjoint
residual matchings of size $4$ cannot coexist, although the residual CDS
demands sizes $(4,4,2,1)$.  The shape itself has a CDS coloring.  Hence this
is a counterexample only to arbitrary greedy choice of the first maximum
matching, not to the original conjecture.

\subsection{Local coloring rules}

Three tempting universal constructions fail exactly:
\begin{itemize}
 \item row-major First Fit gives $(2,2,1,1)$ on $(2,2,2)$ instead of the
 CDS $(2,2,2)$;
 \item the fixed cyclic rule gives $(3,2,2,1)$ on $(3,3,1,1)$ instead of
 $(3,3,1,1)$; and
 \item none of the $4P3=24$ injective choices of cyclic row shifts gives
 the CDS $(3,3,1,1)$ on $(4,2,2)$.
\end{itemize}
These are proved obstructions to the named routes.  They neither oppose nor
support the truth of the original statement except by showing that a proof
needs a more global compatibility mechanism.

\subsection{The compressed allocation kernel}

Group equal-length rows into blocks.  If the distinct lengths are
$a_1>\cdots>a_t>0$ with multiplicities $r_1,\ldots,r_t$, put
$a_{t+1}=0$ and $c_j=a_j-a_{j+1}$.  Row block $i$ meets column block $j$
precisely for $i\leq j$, in a full $r_i\times c_j$ rectangle.  The owner
research produced the following candidate block-allocation system for
nonnegative integers $z_{ij\ell}$:
\begin{align}
 \sum_\ell z_{ij\ell}&=r_ic_j &&(i\leq j),\nonumber\\
 \sum_{j\geq i}z_{ij\ell}&\leq r_i &&(i,\ell),\nonumber\\
 \sum_{i\leq j}z_{ij\ell}&\leq c_j &&(j,\ell),\nonumber\\
 \sum_{i\leq j}z_{ij\ell}&=\delta_\ell &&(\ell).
 \label{eq:ba}
\end{align}
Counting gives necessity.  The proposed sufficiency forms an edge-colored
amalgamated multigraph and successively splits row and column block vertices,
using the colored-detachment lemma restated as Lemma~3.1 by
Bahmanian~\cite{Bahmanian2026}.  That dependency mapping and its
sequential application have not passed an independent proof audit.
Consequently, this note treats \eqref{eq:ba} as a promising compressed
kernel, not as an established equivalence.  A genuine two-level shape gives
three nonempty incidence rectangles; a genuine three-level shape gives six.

For $\lambda=(a^p,b^q)$ with $a>b\geq1$ and $p,q\geq1$, the owner
argument derives
\begin{equation}
 \rho_k=\min\{pa+qb,ka,k(p+q),k(p+b),kb+p(a-b),kp+qb\}.
 \label{eq:six-line}
\end{equation}
It then proposes explicit quotas satisfying \eqref{eq:ba}, separately for
$a\geq p+q$ and $p+q\geq a$.  The formula and quotas have extensive exact
checks, but the proposed infinite-family proof remains unaudited.  It is not
used as proof of Conjecture~\ref{conj:C001}.

\section{Proved partial results}

The following elementary boundary facts have direct constructions and corner
upper bounds.  They are strictly weaker than the two conjectures.

For a rectangle $\lambda=(a^b)$, with
$m=\min(a,b)$ and $M=\max(a,b)$,
\[
 \rho_k=m\min(k,M),\qquad
 \delta=(\underbrace{m,\ldots,m}_{M\text{ terms}}).
\]
When $b\leq a$, coloring cell $(i,j)$ by $i+j\pmod a$ gives the required
classes; transposition covers the other case.

For a hook $(M,1^{m-1})$ with $M\geq m$, the CDS is
\[
 (\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{m-1},
  \underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{M-m+1}).
\]
Pair the tail cell $(i,0)$ with $(0,i)$ for $1\leq i<m$ and leave the
remaining long-row cells as singleton colors.  The same pairing gives, after
the evident adjustment, the familiar formula for every two-row shape
$(a,b)$:
\[
 \delta=(\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{b},
          \underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{a-b}).
\]
Transposition covers shapes with at most two columns.

Finally, for the staircase $\lambda=(n,n-1,\ldots,1)$,
\[
 \rho_k=kn-\frac{k(k-1)}2\quad(0\leq k\leq n),
 \qquad \delta=(n,n-1,\ldots,1).
\]
The antidiagonal coloring $(i,j)\mapsto i+j$ supplies the matching classes,
and the corner $i=0$, $j=n-k$ in \eqref{eq:corner} supplies the matching
upper bound.  None of these arguments controls arbitrary two-level or
three-level shapes.

\section{Robust candidate conjectures}\label{sec:conjectures}

Every item in this section is unproved.  The designation robust candidate
records passage through an independent communication-quality and
falsification audit; it is not a probability estimate and does not assert
truth.

\begin{conjecture}[Registry C001]\label{conj:C001}
Every finite nonempty Young shape with at most two distinct positive row lengths is CDS-colorable. Equivalently, this holds for every rectangle \(\lambda=(a^p)\) with \(a,p\ge 1\), and for every \(\lambda=(a^p,b^q)\) with integers \(a>b\ge 1\) and \(p,q\ge 1\).
\par\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Plain-language statement.} Every finite nonempty Young shape with at most two distinct positive row lengths has a proper coloring whose color-class sizes equal its full chromatic difference sequence; this includes all positive rectangles and all genuine two-level shapes.
\end{conjecture}

Conjecture~\ref{conj:C001} is a strict restriction of
\eqref{eq:ltc-cds}, but an unbounded four-parameter restriction.  It reaches
beyond rectangles, hooks, and two-row or two-column shapes.  Its three-block
structure is the first level at which the simplest cyclic-shift ansatz already
fails.  If true, it would supply a natural full-CDS infinite family and a test
case for the compatibility mechanism absent from greedy matching deletion.
If false, one exact two-level counterexample would refute the original
universal conjecture.

\begin{conjecture}[Registry C002]\label{conj:C002}
Every finite nonempty Young shape with at most three distinct positive row lengths is CDS-colorable.
\par\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Plain-language statement.} For every finite nonempty Young diagram whose positive row lengths take at most three distinct values, its partition graph has a proper coloring whose color-class sizes equal the full chromatic\linebreak \mbox{difference sequence}.
\end{conjecture}

Conjecture~\ref{conj:C002} properly contains
Conjecture~\ref{conj:C001} but excludes all shapes with four or more distinct
row lengths, so it is not the original target renamed.  It is the first
genuinely multi-corner, six-incidence-rectangle case.  A proof could expose
an inductive invariant for the general allocation kernel.  A certified false
case would be a counterexample to both Conjecture~\ref{conj:C002} and the
original problem.

The primary-source and terminology searches recorded on 4 September 2026
found neither restricted full-CDS statement.  This is only a bounded
\emph{not found in the recorded search} conclusion.  The weaker two- and
three-row-length Latin-filling results in
\cite{ChowFanGoemansVondrak2003,AllsopKotlarWanless2025} do not state these
full-CDS conjectures.

\section{Exact evidence and reproducibility}

All numerical counts in this section come from integer data and direct
checkers.  No floating-point inequality is used as evidence.  The four
primary cell-level runs contain 5,252 records, representing 5,127 distinct
shapes after overlaps.  Every record stores a zero-based coloring.  A checker
recomputes the CDS, verifies every row and column conflict constraint and
every class total, and compares every prefix from \eqref{eq:corner} with a
separate integral max-flow evaluator.

\section{Adversarial falsification}

In addition to the baseline data, dedicated direct attacks targeted each
conjecture rather than merely extending a convenient square box.  One
candidate-specific suite checked 96 two-level and 96 three-level cases, with
2,634 independent prefix comparisons.  A subsequent fresh audit checked
34 additional cases for each candidate.  For
Conjecture~\ref{conj:C001}, these cases reached width $20$, $26$ rows, and
area $348$, with 600 prefix comparisons.  For
Conjecture~\ref{conj:C002}, they reached width $18$, $28$ rows, and area
$252$, with 589 comparisons.  Every returned coloring passed direct
verification.

The targeted cases include the one-cell shape, rectangles, hooks, two-row
and two-column boundaries, multiplicity-one degeneracies, balanced and
extreme ratios, near rectangles, transposition stress, and deterministic
random instances.  The C001 suite includes $(4,2,2)$, where all distinct
cyclic row shifts fail.  The C002 suite includes the smallest genuine
three-level staircase $(3,2,1)$ and the greedy obstruction
$(5,4,3,3)$.  The fresh attack seeds were 5224396 for C001 and 5224397
for C002.  Solver retries were retained as such and did not convert
\texttt{UNKNOWN} into negative evidence.

The fastest direct falsifier for Conjecture~\ref{conj:C001} is to enumerate
$a>b\geq1$ and $p,q\geq1$ by increasing dimension and area, compute the
CDS from \eqref{eq:corner}, and solve the exact prescribed-profile
edge-coloring instance.  For Conjecture~\ref{conj:C002}, the six-block
allocation system offers a cheaper screening problem, followed by a direct
cell-coloring instance.  A timeout or solver \texttt{UNKNOWN} is not a
counterexample.  Any claimed infeasibility must be accompanied by an
independently checkable exact certificate; until the detachment reduction is
independently proved, direct coloring infeasibility is the decisive
falsifier.

\section{Limitations and open scope}

The evidence does not prove either conjecture.  In particular:
\begin{itemize}
 \item finite feasibility, even with exact witnesses, has no implication for
 all unbounded parameters;
 \item the proposed proof of the two-row-length case and its colored
 detachment dependency have not passed a fresh proof audit;
 \item for three row lengths there is no symbolic construction or proof of
 feasibility for the unbounded six-block system;
 \item three-level shapes outside the direct $6$ by $6$ box and the
 simultaneous width-$15$/row-count-$15$ allocation boundary are not
 exhaustively covered;
 \item the full $13$ by $13$ boundary was not enumerated, and an interrupted
 width-$20$ sample produced no serialized result and is excluded;
 \item all shapes with four or more distinct row lengths lie outside C002,
 apart from incidental finite data relevant only to the original problem;
 and
 \item no proof assistant was used.
\end{itemize}

The novelty search was also bounded.  It inspected the source article and
arXiv version, the 2003 wide-partition paper, the 2025 three-row-length
preprint, the 2026 detachment paper, an author publication list, and
exact/synonym searches.  It did not exhaust MathSciNet, zbMATH, subscription
citation networks, or the older CDS-coloring literature.  Accordingly this
note makes no priority or exhaustive novelty claim.

Within the stated accepted-literature search boundary, the original problem
remains unresolved.  Conjectures~\ref{conj:C001} and \ref{conj:C002} are
unproved and may be refuted by future exact computation or construction.  The
most informative next proof test is an independent
audit of the block-detachment reduction and every two-level quota inequality.
The most informative next discovery test is to reduce the three-level
six-block quotas to a prescribed-degree factor criterion and attack all of
its subset inequalities.
