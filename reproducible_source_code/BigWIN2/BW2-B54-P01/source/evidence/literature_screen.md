# Focused literature comparison (2026-09-07)

The decisive primary source is Emmanuel Jeandel, *Combinatorial Search for
Strong Shift Equivalence*, arXiv:2609.03567v1. A byte-identical local copy is
`evidence/external/2609.03567.pdf` (SHA-256
`1843a1ff933bc44898c238d36ee1e67c9832e36e78530e98f6d82b33e261a58b`).

Relevant source statements:

- Definition 2 and the following paragraph (PDF p. 3) define shift equivalence
  at lag and explain how an SSE produces an SE.
- Problem 3 and Theorem 2 (PDF pp. 4--5) give the Baker family and the proved
  SSE cases through `k<=10` and at `k=12`, while treating failed searches for
  the other tested parameters as nonproof.
- The discussion immediately after Theorem 2 (PDF p. 5) gives lag-4 SEs for
  `k=7` and `k=12` and says only that preliminary work suggests those are the
  only parameters above 2 admitting lag 4. It supplies neither a lag-minimality
  result nor a proof for `k=11`.

Exact-phrase and equivalent-language web searches on 2026-09-07 for the Baker
matrices at `k=11`, shift equivalence at lag 22, and minimum lag retrieved the
arXiv paper and the author's companion SSE-certificate page, but no primary
source stating the fixed-`k=11` minimum-lag theorem proved in
`lag1_22_2x2_minimality.md`. This is a limited comparison, not proof of novelty,
priority, or continuing open status.
