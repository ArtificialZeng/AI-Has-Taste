# Frozen source statement: win-00054-p01

Let
\[
A_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}1&12\\11&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}1&132\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Determine whether `A_11` and `B_11` are strong shift equivalent over the semiring of nonnegative integers. Thus an affirmative answer must give a finite chain whose consecutive matrices are `RS` and `SR` for nonnegative integer rectangular matrices `R,S`; a negative or partial result must state exactly what class of chains it excludes and provide a reproducible certificate. Do not weaken this to shift equivalence or to similarity.

Primary source: Emmanuel Jeandel, *Combinatorial Search for Strong Shift Equivalence*, arXiv:2609.03567v1, Problem 3, Theorem 2, Sections 2.1 and 4. The paper supplies chains for `k<=10` and `k=12` and states that no chain was found for any `k>=11`; this is not a proof for `k=11`.
