\begin{abstract}
Let $V=\F_2^5$, and let $\cF_1,\cF_2,\cF_3$ be families of
three-dimensional linear subspaces such that
$\dim(U_1\cap U_2\cap U_3)\geq 1$ whenever $U_i\in\cF_i$.
We prove
\[
  |\cF_1|\,|\cF_2|\,|\cF_3|\leq 35^3=42875,
\]
and classify equality: all three families must be the complete star through
one common one-dimensional subspace.  The proof is an exact finite
certification.  It enumerates the 155 three-subspaces, encodes the forbidden
triples and lossless symmetry reductions in two Boolean formulas, and uses
binary DRAT streams containing independently checked refutation prefixes for
the upper bound and for the non-star equality cases.  The archived streams
have disclosed trailing data, including incomplete final records.  The result
concerns only the displayed finite parameter instance; no broader parameter
or priority claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{Context and statement}

For a prime power $q$, write $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}V\\k\end{smallmatrix}\right]_q$ for the set of $k$-dimensional
linear subspaces of $V$.  Families
$\cF_i\subseteq \left[\begin{smallmatrix}V\\k_i\end{smallmatrix}\right]_q$ are called $r$-cross $t$-intersecting when
\[
 \dim(F_1\cap\cdots\cap F_r)\geq t
 \qquad(F_i\in\cF_i,\ 1\leq i\leq r).
\]
Frankl and Tokushige proposed the corresponding product conjecture for
vector spaces as Conjecture~8.1 of \cite{FranklTokushige2016}.  In the equal
dimension, $t=1$ case it predicts
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^r |\cF_i|\leq \left[\begin{smallmatrix}n-1\\k-1\end{smallmatrix}\right]_q^r
 \quad\text{when}\quad (r-1)n\geq rk,
\]
and a common full star attains the right-hand side.

Two recent results locate the present parameter point relative to the known
range.  Li, Lv, and Wen prove the product theorem, with equality
classification, under
$n\geq \frac{r}{r-1}(k_1+t)+1$ \cite[Theorem~1.1]{LiLvWen2026}; here that
condition is $n\geq 7$.  Tokushige's two-family theorem applies when
$n\geq 2k$ \cite[Theorem~2]{Tokushige2013}.  Applying it cyclically to
nonempty $r$-cross families gives the $r$-family product bound for equal
dimensions in that range, as observed explicitly in
\cite[after Theorem~3]{ShimizuTokushige2026}; here it starts at $n=6$.
Neither comparison reaches $n=5$.

The computation below follows a proof-carrying finite-model pattern: the
mathematical reduction, symmetry quotient, generated instance, and exact
checker are kept distinct.  Exact predicates, symmetry-reduced exhaustion,
and an independent verifier are also used for finite discrete-geometric
models in \cite{ZengEtAl2026}.  That citation is methodological; the
combinatorial objects and certificates here are different.

Our result is the following precise instance.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $V=\F_2^5$.  For $i=1,2,3$, let
$\cF_i\subseteq \left[\begin{smallmatrix}V\\3\end{smallmatrix}\right]_2$, and suppose that
\[
 \dim(U_1\cap U_2\cap U_3)\geq 1
 \qquad\text{for every }U_i\in\cF_i.
\]
Then
\[
 |\cF_1|\,|\cF_2|\,|\cF_3|\leq 35^3=42875.
\]
Equality holds if and only if there is a one-dimensional subspace $L\leq V$
such that, for all $i$,
\[
 \cF_i=\cS(L):=\{U\in \left[\begin{smallmatrix}V\\3\end{smallmatrix}\right]_2:L\leq U\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The rest of the paper gives the complete correspondence between this theorem
and the two refuted Boolean formulas.  A checked refutation is used only
after the finite encoding and every normalization have been justified.

\section{The exact finite model}

The Gaussian counts are
\[
 |\left[\begin{smallmatrix}V\\3\end{smallmatrix}\right]_2|=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}5\\3\end{smallmatrix}\right]_2=155,
 \qquad
 |\cS(L)|=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}4\\2\end{smallmatrix}\right]_2=35.
\]
Over $\F_2$, each nonzero vector determines a distinct one-space, so there are
31 possible stars.  Three copies of $\cS(L)$ are cross intersecting and attain
$35^3$.

Let $\cG=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}V\\3\end{smallmatrix}\right]_2$.  The generator enumerates $\cG$ by canonical
row-reduced echelon bases.  Each resulting space is also represented by the
32-bit mask of its eight vectors.  Independent triples of nonzero vectors
give the same 155 distinct spaces.  For every ordered
$(A,B,C)\in\cG^3$, declare the triple \emph{forbidden} when
\[
 A\cap B\cap C=\{0\}.
\]
The mask test finds 2,499,840 forbidden triples among
$155^3=3,723,875$.  On every triple it agrees with a dual calculation: after
orthogonal complementation, the six generators of the three resulting
two-spaces have rank five exactly in the forbidden case.

Introduce a membership variable $x_{i,U}$ for $1\leq i\leq3$ and
$U\in\cG$.  For each forbidden triple include the clause
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forbidden-clause}
  \lnot x_{1,A}\vee\lnot x_{2,B}\vee\lnot x_{3,C}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:base}
The projections onto the 465 membership variables of the satisfying
assignments of all clauses \eqref{eq:forbidden-clause} are exactly the ordered
triples of cross-intersecting families in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Given three families, set $x_{i,U}=1$ precisely when $U\in\cF_i$.  Clause
\eqref{eq:forbidden-clause} fails precisely when its forbidden triple is
chosen, which is precisely a choice with zero common intersection.  Thus all
clauses hold if and only if every allowed choice has positive-dimensional
common intersection.
\end{proof}

This correspondence is exhaustive: the edge stream was independently
reconstructed and compared record by record, and every generated clause of
both final formulas was regenerated semantically and compared in order.

\section{Lossless cardinality and symmetry reductions}

For each family, the 155 membership bits are sorted in descending order by a
Boolean insertion network.  Every comparator has outputs
\[
   h=p\vee q,\qquad \ell=p\wedge q,
\]
and six clauses encode these two equivalences.  Consequently the $j$th output
$y_{i,j}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unary}
 y_{i,j}=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |\cF_i|\geq j
 \qquad(1\leq j\leq155).
\end{equation}
The implications $y_{3,j}\Rightarrow y_{2,j}\Rightarrow y_{1,j}$ impose
$a\geq b\geq c$, where $(a,b,c)=(|\cF_1|,|\cF_2|,|\cF_3|)$.  This loses
nothing because the family labels may be permuted.

For positive product, choose a member of the largest family.  The group
$\operatorname{GL}(5,2)$ is transitive on $\cG$, so a simultaneous linear
change of coordinates sends that member to a fixed vertex $U_0$.  The
stabilizer of $U_0$ has three orbits on $\cG$, distinguished by
$\dim(U_0\cap U)=1,2,3$.  Complete enumeration of its 64,512 elements gives
orbit sizes
\[
 112,\quad 42,\quad 1,
\]
and fixed representatives $R_1,R_2,R_3$, respectively.  Choosing a member of
the second family and applying an element of the stabilizer therefore permits
the additional clauses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalization}
 x_{1,U_0},\qquad
 x_{2,R_1}\vee x_{2,R_2}\vee x_{2,R_3}.
\end{equation}
Transitivity witnesses for all 155 vertices and every stabilizer element were
constructed directly over $\F_2$; the computed orbits agree exactly with the
three intersection-dimension classes.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:normalization}
Every cross-intersecting triple with positive product is carried, by a family
permutation followed by one simultaneous element of $\operatorname{GL}(5,2)$,
to a triple satisfying the cardinality ordering and
\eqref{eq:normalization}.  These operations preserve family sizes,
cross-intersection, and the property of being three copies of one full star.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The preceding construction proves existence of the normalized triple.  A
simultaneous invertible linear map preserves all intersection dimensions and
cardinalities, and maps a full star through $L$ to the full star through the
image of $L$.  Family permutation has the same invariance properties.
\end{proof}

\section{The certified product bound}

On the complete sorted cardinality cube
$1\leq c\leq b\leq a\leq155$, exhaustive integer enumeration gives 829
componentwise-minimal triples $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ whose upward closure is
exactly
\[
  abc\geq42876.
\]
For each such boundary triple, a Tseitin variable is made equivalent to
\[
 y_{1,\alpha}\wedge y_{2,\beta}\wedge y_{3,\gamma},
\]
and one clause requires at least one of the 829 terms.  By
\eqref{eq:unary}, this disjunction is true exactly when the product exceeds
$42875$.

Let $\Phi_{>}$ be the conjunction of the exact sorting-network clauses, the
cardinality ordering, \eqref{eq:normalization}, all forbidden-triple clauses,
the term equivalences, and their final disjunction.  The generated DIMACS
formula has 72,904 variables and 2,718,299 clauses.  CaDiCaL 1.9.5 produced a
343,067,661-byte binary DRAT stream containing a checked refutation prefix
ending at byte 343,035,643.  The remaining 32,018 bytes include an incomplete
final record and are not part of that prefix.  A system description of the
CaDiCaL solver line is given in \cite{BiereEtAl2024}.  A separate
\texttt{drat-trim} run reached the contradiction in the prefix and returned
\texttt{s VERIFIED} after 603,299,890 resolution steps.  The DRAT format and
the checker's role in validating unsatisfiability proofs are described in
\cite{WetzlerHeuleHunt2014}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:upper}
No cross-intersecting triple has product at least $42876$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The checked DRAT refutation proves that $\Phi_{>}$ is unsatisfiable.  If a
cross-intersecting triple with product at least $42876$ existed, all three
families would be nonempty.  Lemma~\ref{lem:normalization} would normalize it.
Lemma~\ref{lem:base} supplies all forbidden-triple clauses; the exact sorting
network supplies \eqref{eq:unary}; and the exhaustive boundary equivalence
would make the final disjunction true.  Extending by the uniquely determined
comparator and Tseitin variables would give a satisfying assignment of
$\Phi_{>}$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Since products are integers, Proposition~\ref{prop:upper} gives the asserted
upper bound.  Together with the common-star construction, it also proves
attainment.

\section{Certified equality classification}

The sorted positive integer triples bounded by 155 whose product is exactly
$42875$ are, exhaustively,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:size-triples}
 (35,35,35),\qquad (49,35,25),\qquad (125,49,7).
\end{equation}
Using adjacent unary outputs in \eqref{eq:unary}, a second formula imposes the
disjunction of these three \emph{exact} cardinality patterns.

It remains to ask whether an equality assignment can fail to be a common
star.  For every one-space $L$, include the single 465-literal clause
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star-exclusion}
 D_L:=\mathop{\vee}_{i=1}^3\left(
   \mathop{\vee}_{U\in\cS(L)}\lnot x_{i,U}
   \ \vee\!
   \mathop{\vee}_{U\notin\cS(L)}x_{i,U}
 \right).
\end{equation}
The clause $D_L$ is false on exactly one membership assignment: all three
families are the complete star $\cS(L)$.  The 31 constructed stars are
distinct, feasible, and have size 35.  Thus conjoining all $D_L$ excludes
exactly the common-star equality triples and nothing else.

Let $\Phi_{=}^{\mathrm{ns}}$ contain the base encoding and normalizations, the
exact disjunction \eqref{eq:size-triples}, and the 31 clauses
\eqref{eq:star-exclusion}.  It has 72,078 variables and 2,715,035 clauses.
CaDiCaL produced a 59,270,689-byte binary DRAT stream whose checked
refutation prefix ends at byte 59,266,795.  The remaining 3,894 bytes include
an incomplete final record and are not part of that prefix.  An independent
\texttt{drat-trim} run reached the contradiction in the prefix and returned
\texttt{s VERIFIED} after 183,039,602 resolution steps.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:equality}
Every cross-intersecting triple with product $42875$ consists of three copies
of one full star.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose instead that a non-star equality triple exists.  Its product is
positive, so Lemma~\ref{lem:normalization} produces a normalized equality
triple.  The common-star property is invariant under both normalization
operations; hence the normalized triple is still non-star and satisfies every
$D_L$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:base}, the exact unary network, and
\eqref{eq:size-triples} then extend it to a satisfying assignment of
$\Phi_{=}^{\mathrm{ns}}$.  This contradicts the checked refutation.
\end{proof}

Propositions~\ref{prop:upper} and \ref{prop:equality}, together with the
explicit common-star construction, prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

The decisive replay commands are
\begin{verbatim}
python3 evidence/build_rref_hypergraph.py
python3 evidence/verify_reductions.py
drat-trim evidence/product_above_bound.cnf \
  evidence/product_above_bound.bdrat -i
drat-trim evidence/nonstar_equality.cnf \
  evidence/nonstar_equality.bdrat -i
\end{verbatim}
An independent audit additionally enumerated the spaces as eight-element
spans rather than from submitted RREF bases, compared the complete forbidden
edge stream, regenerated every CNF clause from its semantics, and replayed
both refutation prefixes.  All decisive predicates are Boolean or integer; no
floating-point conclusion, unverified solver status, or heuristic search
claim enters the theorem.

OpenAI Codex assisted with literature search, finite-model and encoding
design, draft code, adversarial audit organization, and manuscript
preparation.  Language-model output is not mathematical evidence.  The
theorem rests on the stated reductions, archived exact instances, and checked
refutations.  No proof assistant was used.

Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is restricted to
$(q,n,k,r,t)=(2,5,3,3,1)$.  It neither proves another parameter case nor
asserts that the finite instance was not resolved elsewhere.  The literature
comparison above records only what the cited results cover.
