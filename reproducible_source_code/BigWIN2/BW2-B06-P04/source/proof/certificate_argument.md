# Why the four-way certificate is conclusive

The discovery executable is not trusted by the theorem. Its only outputs
accepted by the independent verifier are positive finite objects or directly
checkable structural predicates.

- `2 g r1 r2`: both masks are surjective NAC-colourings of `g`, verified by
  transitive closure, and are neither equal nor complements within `g`.
- `T g`: an exact reverse degree-two deletion reaches \(K_2\), every deleted
  vertex had adjacent neighbours, and the graph has nine triangles.
- `B g`: an exact reverse degree-two deletion reaches \(K_2\), exactly one
  deletion has nonadjacent neighbours, and the graph has eight triangles.
- `A g`: all stable vertex subsets are enumerated and none disconnects the
  complement; all six-vertex subsets are checked and exactly one induces a
  cubic graph containing a triangle, equivalently a triangular prism.

No row means “not found,” “timed out,” or “search count below a threshold.”
An unknown tag, malformed integer, graph mismatch, duplicated graph, failed
Laman inequality, invalid witness, or trailing/missing row causes rejection.

The verifier separately enumerates all 1,815 vertex subsets of sizes 4 through
10 for every graph. Sizes two and three satisfy the Laman upper bound
automatically for simple graphs; size eleven is checked by the exact total of
19 edges.
