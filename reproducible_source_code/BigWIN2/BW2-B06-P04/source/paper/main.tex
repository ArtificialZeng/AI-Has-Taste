\begin{abstract}
Clinch, Garamvölgyi, Haslegrave, Huynh, Legerský, and Nixon conjectured a
structural characterization of minimally rigid graphs having a unique
NAC-colouring modulo global colour exchange, and reported a computer
verification through ten vertices.  We certify the next case.  Among all
$2{,}039{,}273$ isomorphism classes of eleven-vertex Laman graphs, exactly
$23{,}499$ have one NAC-colouring modulo colour exchange, and every one belongs
to one of the two conjectured families.  The exhaustive certificate assigns
each graph either to a positive structural family, to the class of $2$-trees,
or supplies two explicit inequivalent NAC-colourings.  An independent checker
verifies every Laman inequality and every certificate row using exact integer
and bitset operations.  This proves the conjecture at order eleven; it does not
settle the conjecture for arbitrary order.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A NAC-colouring is a surjective red--blue edge-colouring in which every cycle
is monochromatic or contains at least two edges of each colour.  Such
colourings were introduced by Grasegger, Legerský, and Schicho in their study
of flexible graph realizations~\cite{grasegger2019}.  For a graph $G$, let
$\NACnum(G)$ be half the number of its NAC-colourings; thus only global colour
exchange is removed, not graph automorphisms.

Clinch, Garamvölgyi, Haslegrave, Huynh, Legerský, and Nixon recently studied
this parameter for minimally rigid graphs~\cite{clinch2026}.  Their
Conjecture~6.1 states that $\NACnum(G)=1$ precisely for two structural classes:

\begin{enumerate}
\item $G\in\Gsc$ and $G$ has exactly one triangular-prism subgraph; or
\item $G$ is a $0$-extension graph admitting exactly one open step.
\end{enumerate}

They proved the forward construction statements and reported verification of
the converse for graphs on at most ten vertices~\cite[Conjecture~6.1 and the
following paragraph]{clinch2026}.  The same paper reports NAC-count
distributions at orders eleven and twelve, so a new finite result must certify
the structural classification, not merely repeat those counts.

Our main result is the complete next order.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $G$ be an eleven-vertex minimally rigid graph in the plane.  Then
$\NACnum(G)=1$ if and only if either
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G\in\Gsc$ and $G$ has exactly one triangular-prism subgraph, or
\item $G$ is a $0$-extension graph with exactly one open step.
\end{enumerate}
There are exactly $23{,}499$ isomorphism classes satisfying these equivalent
conditions.
\end{theorem}

The proof is computer-assisted but finite and exact.  Its trust boundary is
small: a canonical graph universe is cross-checked against an independently
published dataset, and a separate verifier checks a four-way certificate.  In
particular, no conclusion is drawn from floating-point output, a search limit,
or failure to find a colouring.

The literature search for this work was repeated after the computation on
August 30, 2026.  We found no primary source in the recorded searches that
certified Conjecture~6.1 at order eleven.  This is a bounded search statement,
not a claim of absolute priority.

\section{Definitions and structural facts}

All graphs are finite, undirected, and simple.  For $X\subseteq V(G)$, write
$G[X]$ for the induced subgraph.

\begin{definition}
A graph $G=(V,E)$ is a \emph{Laman graph} if
\[
 |E|=2|V|-3
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 |E(G[X])|\leq 2|X|-3
 \quad\text{whenever }2\leq |X|\leq |V|.
\]
\end{definition}

By the Pollaczek--Geiringer--Laman characterization, the Laman graphs are
exactly the generically minimally rigid graphs in the plane~\cite{laman1970}.
At order eleven they have nineteen edges.

\begin{definition}
A colouring $c:E(G)\to\{\mathrm{red},\mathrm{blue}\}$ is a
\emph{NAC-colouring} if it is surjective and every cycle is monochromatic or
has at least two red and at least two blue edges.  We set
\[
 \NACnum(G)=\frac12\#\{\text{NAC-colourings of }G\}.
\]
\end{definition}

The following elementary form is useful for independent verification.

\begin{lemma}[Connectivity criterion]\label{lem:connectivity}
A surjective red--blue edge-colouring is a NAC-colouring if and only if no edge
of either colour has endpoints connected by a path using only the other
colour.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An edge and an opposite-coloured path between its endpoints contain an
almost-monochromatic cycle: take a shortest such path.  Conversely, deleting
the uniquely coloured edge from an almost-monochromatic cycle leaves an
opposite-coloured path between its endpoints.
\end{proof}

A $0$-extension adds a new vertex adjacent to exactly two old vertices.  The
step is \emph{open} if those two vertices are non-adjacent.  A $0$-extension
graph is obtained from $K_2$ by a sequence of such steps.  A $2$-tree is a
$0$-extension graph with no open step.

\begin{lemma}[Open-step invariant]\label{lem:open}
If $G$ is an $n$-vertex $0$-extension graph and $t(G)$ is its number of
triangles, then every $0$-extension construction of $G$ has
\[
 n-2-t(G)
\]
open steps.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
There are $n-2$ extension steps.  A closed step creates exactly one triangle,
while an open step creates none.  Conversely, every triangle in the final
graph has a unique last-added vertex; the step adding that vertex was closed
and created the triangle.  Thus triangles are in bijection with closed steps.
\end{proof}

This also removes a possible ambiguity in the phrase ``exactly one open
step.''  At order eleven it is equivalent to being a $0$-extension graph with
eight triangles.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:h1}
A $2$-tree has no NAC-colouring.  A $0$-extension graph with exactly one open
step has exactly one NAC-colouring modulo global colour exchange.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every triangle is monochromatic in a NAC-colouring.  In a $2$-tree, the closed
construction propagates one colour from the initial edge to every edge, which
contradicts surjectivity.

Now consider a construction with one open step.  Before that step the graph is
a $2$-tree, so all its edges have one colour, say blue, in the restriction of
any NAC-colouring.  Let the open step add $x$ adjacent to non-adjacent vertices
$u$ and $v$.  The new edges cannot both be blue, since later closed steps
cannot introduce a second colour.  They also cannot have different colours:
a blue $u$--$v$ path in the connected $2$-tree, together with $xu$ and $xv$,
would have exactly one red edge.  Hence $xu$ and $xv$ are both red.

This gives a NAC-colouring: every cycle through $x$ contains both red edges
and a blue $u$--$v$ path of length at least two.  At each later closed
extension, the two new edges must have the colour of the old edge that joins
their endpoints, because they form a triangle.  Conversely, extending in this
forced way preserves the NAC property: replace every two-edge passage through
the new vertex by that old edge, of the same colour.  Thus the colouring is
unique up to exchange.
\end{proof}

The class $\Gsc$ starts from $K_2$ and recursively glues a triangle or a
triangular prism along an edge or a triangle.  Le and Pfender characterized
graphs with $2n-3$ edges and no stable cut in these terms~\cite{lepfender2013};
a gap in their proof was subsequently repaired by Rauch and
Rautenbach~\cite{rauch2025}.  We use the resulting equivalence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gsc}
  G\in\Gsc
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  G\text{ has no stable cut},
\end{equation}
for graphs with $2|V(G)|-3$ edges.  Here a stable cut is an independent vertex
set whose deletion disconnects the graph.

For $G\in\Gsc$, the prior calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-a}
 \NACnum(G)=2^p-1,
\end{equation}
where $p$ is the number of prism attachments in a recursive construction of
$G$~\cite[following Conjecture~6.1]{clinch2026}.  Briefly, after fixing the
colour of a gluing edge, a triangle attachment adds no choice, whereas a prism
attachment permits the monochromatic choice or the prism's unique compatible
NAC-colouring.  Every attached prism remains a prism subgraph, and different
attachments produce distinct copies.  A construction with no prism is a
$2$-tree and cannot contain the cubic prism as a subgraph.  Consequently, a
member of $\Gsc$ with exactly one prism subgraph has $p=1$ and hence
$\NACnum(G)=1$.

\section{The exact four-way certificate}

The certificate contains one row for every isomorphism class.  Graphs are
stored as 55-bit integers obtained by reading the upper triangle of the
$11\times11$ adjacency matrix row by row.  A red-edge mask uses the same bit
positions; its complement within the graph is the blue-edge mask.

Each row has one of four meanings.

\begin{description}
\item[Type 2.] Two literal red-edge masks are supplied.  The checker verifies
both by Lemma~\ref{lem:connectivity} and verifies that they are neither equal
nor global colour exchanges.  Hence $\NACnum(G)\geq2$.
\item[Type T.] A reverse degree-two elimination reaches $K_2$, with adjacent
neighbours at every deletion.  Thus $G$ is a $2$-tree and
$\NACnum(G)=0$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:h1}.
\item[Type B.] A reverse degree-two elimination reaches $K_2$, exactly one
deleted vertex has non-adjacent neighbours, and $G$ has eight triangles.
Lemma~\ref{lem:open} and Proposition~\ref{prop:h1} give $\NACnum(G)=1$.
\item[Type A.] Direct enumeration finds no stable cut and exactly one
six-vertex subgraph isomorphic to the triangular prism.  Equation
\eqref{eq:gsc} and \eqref{eq:family-a} give $\NACnum(G)=1$.
\end{description}

For a Laman graph, a prism subgraph is automatically induced: the prism already
has nine edges on six vertices, the maximum allowed by Laman sparsity.
The positive types are disjoint.  Indeed, every $0$-extension graph is
$2$-degenerate, while the triangular prism has minimum degree three; hence a
Type~B graph cannot contain the prism required for Type~A.

The certificate does not contain a ``not found'' type.  Malformed data,
unknown types, missing rows, repeated graph encodings, invalid colour masks,
or a failed structural test cause the independent checker to reject.

\section{Exhaustiveness and independent verification}

Larsson's nauty plugin generates nonisomorphic $(2,3)$-tight graphs directly
using canonical augmentation~\cite{larsson,nauty2014}.  A fresh run produced
$2{,}039{,}273$ graph6 records at order eleven.  As an independent universe,
we used the 2018 dataset of Capco, Gallet, Grasegger, Koutschan, Lubbes, and
Schicho, which was produced by a different combinatorial computation and
contains all Laman graphs through order twelve~\cite{capco2018data}.  After
canonical relabelling, sorting, and duplicate checks, the two eleven-vertex
files agreed exactly.

The release checker then performs the following operations from serialized
input, without importing discovery output as trusted values.

\begin{enumerate}
\item It confirms exactly $2{,}039{,}273$ distinct graph encodings.
\item For every graph, it checks the nineteen-edge count and all $1{,}815$
vertex subsets of sizes four through ten.  Subsets of sizes two and three
satisfy Laman sparsity automatically in a simple graph.
\item It verifies every Type~2 colouring by bitset transitive closure.
\item It recomputes every Type~A, B, and T structural predicate directly.
\item It rejects any missing or trailing certificate row.
\end{enumerate}

The discovery code uses disjoint-set closure; the checker instead uses a
separately implemented reachability closure.  Thus the decisive NAC predicate
is evaluated by two different decompositions.  The verified partition is
shown in Table~\ref{tab:partition}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The universe comparison and the direct Laman checks show that the certificate
covers every eleven-vertex minimally rigid graph up to isomorphism.  Type~A
and Type~B rows satisfy the two conjectured structural conditions and have
$\NACnum=1$ by the results of Section~2.  Type~T rows have $\NACnum=0$.
Every Type~2 row has two independently verified NAC-colourings after quotienting
global colour exchange, so it cannot have $\NACnum=1$.  The four types are
exhaustive.  Finally, $1{,}196+22{,}303=23{,}499$.
\end{proof}

\section{Reproducibility and limitations}

The accompanying archive contains the integer graph dataset, the four-way
certificate, discovery source, independent verifier, build script, generation
cross-check, and complete logs.  Running
\begin{center}
\texttt{./scripts/build\_and\_verify.sh}
\end{center}
recompiles the checker, verifies the full certificate, and runs eight
fail-closed mutation tests.  Enabling \texttt{RUN\_DISCOVERY=1} also reruns
discovery and requires byte-for-byte equality with the released certificate.
All decisive operations are exact integer, bitset, graph-connectivity, or
finite enumeration operations.  No proof assistant was used.

This theorem is limited to eleven vertices.  It neither proves the infinite
converse of Conjecture~6.1 nor gives a new asymptotic characterization.  The
reported order-eleven NAC-count distribution in the prior paper remains prior
work; the contribution here is the exhaustive family classification and its
independently checkable certificate.
