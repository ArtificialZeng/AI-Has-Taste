                                          Abstract
                                          The transformation of the h-vector of a finite simplicial complex under an F-uniform subdi-
                                          vision is encoded by a transformation matrix. Mu and Welker conjectured that the transfor-
                                          mation matrix of the barycentric subdivision is totally positive. In this paper, we give a new
                                          combinatorial proof of this conjecture. We also prove the total positivity of the transforma-
                                          tion matrix of the interval subdivision. In addition, we establish a sufficient condition for
                                          the transformation matrix of a uniform subdivision to be totally positive of order 2 (TP2 ),
                                          thereby partially answering a question of Mu and Welker. As an application, we show that
                                          the transformation matrix of the r-colored barycentric subdivision is TP2 .
                                          Keywords: Totally positive matrix, F-uniform subdivision, Neville elimination
                                          2020 MSC: 05E45, 05A20, 15B48


                                          1. Introduction
                                              The h-vector is one of the central enumerative invariants of a finite simplicial complex.
                                          Since Stanley’s work on the enumerative theory of simplicial subdivisions [27], a fundamental
                                          problem has been to understand how the h-vector changes under subdivision operations. This
                                          viewpoint has motivated the study of h-vector transformations for several important families
                                          of subdivisions, including the edgewise subdivision [1, 11], the barycentric subdivision [10, 18,
                                          25], the interval subdivision [5, 6] and the antiprism triangulation [4, 17]. Athanasiadis [3]
                                          introduced the concept of F-uniform subdivision, which provides a unified framework for
                                          these classical operations. Let
                                                                                 h∆ = (h∆            ∆
                                                                                        0 , . . . , hd )

                                          be the h-vector of the (d − 1)-dimensional finite simplicial complex ∆. Athanasiadis [3]
                                          showed that if a simplicial complex ∆′ is an F-uniform subdivision of ∆, then the h-vector
                                          of ∆′ can be expressed via a transformation matrix HF ,
                                                                                          ′
                                                                                       h∆ = HF h∆ .

    Mu and Welker [24] proved that the total positivity properties of HF are closely related to
the preservation of specific coefficient inequalities under F-uniform subdivisions. Following
Pinkus [26], a finite or infinite real matrix is called totally positive of order r (or TPr for
short), if its minors of all orders ≤ r are non-negative. It is called totally positive (or TP for
short) if its minors of all orders are non-negative. The theory of total positivity provides a
powerful mathematical framework with fundamental applications across diverse fields such
as statistics, mechanics, and representation theory [19].
    There has been much interest in the total positivity of the transformation matrix HF .
For example, the transformation matrix HF for the r-th edgewise subdivision is TP2 , as
implied by the earlier work of Diaconis and Fulman [12]. Furthermore, the second author
and Wang [22] obtained the stronger result that HF for the r-th edgewise subdivision is TP.
Mu and Welker [24] showed that when F is the barycentric subdivision, its transformation
matrix HF is TP2 . Then they raised the following conjecture.
Conjecture 1.1 ([24, Conjecture 2.7]). Let F be the barycentric subdivision. Then the
transformation matrix HF is TP.
    Very recently, Mu, Wang and Zhu [23] settled this conjecture by verifying sufficient con-
ditions for total positivity. In this paper, we give an independent combinatorial proof of
Conjecture 1.1. Moreover, we prove the total positivity of the transformation matrix of the
interval subdivision. We also establish a sufficient condition for the TP2 property of trans-
formation matrices of uniform subdivisions by the interlacing property. As an application,
we show that the transformation matrix of the r-colored barycentric subdivision is TP2 .
    The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 collects the necessary background
on Neville elimination, planar networks, and totally positive matrices. Section 3 is devoted
to the barycentric subdivision. More precisely, we obtain a bidiagonal decomposition of HF
for the barycentric subdivision and use planar networks to give a combinatorial proof of
Conjecture 1.1. In Section 4, we prove the total positivity of the transformation matrix of
the interval subdivision. We decompose the HF for the interval subdivision into three TP
matrices, one of which is the HF for the barycentric subdivision. Then the result follows by
Cauchy-Binet formula. In Section 5, we establish a general criterion for the TP2 property of
transformation matrices of uniform subdivisions, and apply it to the r-colored barycentric
subdivision.

2. Preliminaries
    Following Athanasiadis [3], a triangulation ∆′ of a simplicial complex ∆ is said to be F-
uniform if, when restricted to any two faces of ∆ having the same dimension, the resulting
triangulations have the same number of faces in every dimension. Equivalently, the combina-
torial enumeration of the restriction depends only on the dimension of the original face, and
not on the particular face chosen. The F-uniform subdivisions include the edgewise subdi-
vision, the barycentric subdivision, the interval subdivision and the antiprism triangulation.
We write ∆F for ∆′ in this case and speak of F as a uniform triangulation. The following is
due to Athanasiadis [3].

     Planar networks provide a powerful tool for proving the total positivity of a matrix. Let
(V, A) be a directed graph (or digraph, for short) with vertex set V and arc set A without
loops or multiple edges. A path in (V, A) is a sequence π = u0 u1 · · · uk of vertices such that
(ui , ui+1 ) ∈ A, for i = 0, . . . , k − 1. In this case, we say that π is a path from u0 to uk . The
digraph is called locally finite if, for any two vertices u, v ∈ V, the number of paths from u


                                                 4
to v is finite. If the digraph is planar, locally finite and has a non-negative weight function
w : A → R≥0 , then we call D = (V, A, w) a planar network.
    Let D = (V, A, w) be a planar network. The weight of a path is defined to be the product
of the weights of all its components. For u, v ∈ V, let PD (u, v) be the sum of the weights of
all paths from u to v. Given two vertex subsets

                               u = (u0 , u1 , . . . , un ),          v = (v0 , v1 , . . . , vn ),

we say that u and v are compatible if, for every non-identity permutation on {0, 1, . . . , n},
there are no (n + 1)-tuples of paths from (u0 , u1 , . . . , un ) to (vσ(0) , vσ(1) , . . . , vσ(n) ) that are
non-intersecting. Moreover, u and v are called fully compatible if every pair of subsequences

                                   (ui0 , ui1 , . . . , uis ),       (vj0 , vj1 , . . . , vjs )

is compatible for all i0 < · · · < is , j0 < · · · < js .

Theorem 2.3 ([9, Theorem 3.1]). Let A = [a(i, j)]0≤i,j≤n be a real matrix. Then A is
TP if and only if there exists a planar network D = (V, A, w) and two vertex sequences
u = (u0 , u1 , . . . , un ), v = (v0 , v1 , . . . , vn ) that are fully compatible, such that

                                  a(i, j) = PD (ui , vj )             for 0 ≤ i, j ≤ n.
