\begin{abstract}
We study a restricted periodic layered model motivated by the $k=6$ case of a
conjectured diameter bound for $K_7$-free graphs that is not decided by the
counterexamples discussed below.  Consider a
cyclic weighted graph with 13 nonempty layers, at most four vertices per
layer, and edges only within a layer or between consecutive layers.  Suppose
that all weights are nonnegative, every weighted open neighborhood has weight
at least one, the graph is $K_7$-free, and exactly one consecutive pair of
layers has total cardinality at least seven.  We prove, by a symmetry-reduced
exact computation, that the total weight is at least
\[
   \frac{4154}{851}=\frac{39}{8}+\frac{43}{6808}.
\]
The reduction leaves 480 linear programs.  A numerical solver is used only to
find candidate dual vectors; every retained vector and every comparison is
then checked over the rational numbers.  Thus a four-slot period capable of
beating the critical mass $39/8$ must have at least two high boundaries.  This
is a result about the stated layered model only; it does not decide the
unrestricted $K_7$-free diameter problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Context and contribution}

For a connected graph $G$, write $n=|V(G)|$, $\delta(G)$ for its minimum
degree, and $\operatorname{diam}(G)$ for its diameter.  Erd\H{o}s, Pach,
Pollack, and Tuza initiated the relevant clique-free refinements of the
classical order--minimum-degree diameter bounds
\cite{ErdosPachPollackTuza1989}.  Czabarka, Singgih, and Sz\'ekely later
formulated the following unified conjecture: for fixed integers $k\geq 3$ and
$\delta\geq \lceil 3k/2\rceil-1$, every connected $K_{k+1}$-free graph of
order $n$ and minimum degree at least $\delta$ should satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diameter-conjecture}
 \operatorname{diam}(G)
 \leq \left(3-\frac{2}{k}\right)\frac{n}{\delta}+O_{k,\delta}(1).
\end{equation}
See Conjecture~2 of \cite{CzabarkaSinggihSzekely2021}.  Chen and Chen recently
constructed counterexamples for every $k\geq7$ and sufficiently large minimum
degree; their even-case theorem has
$k=2r$ and assumes $r\geq4$ \cite{ChenChen2026}.  Consequently that
construction does not decide the first even boundary $k=6$, where
\eqref{eq:diameter-conjecture} asks for coefficient $8/3$ and threshold
$\delta\geq8$.

Weighted layered graphs are useful because, for integral weights (or rational
weights after a common scaling), replacing a vertex of weight $a$ by an
independent set of $a$ twins turns weighted open-neighborhood bounds into
ordinary degree bounds; this is the clump-graph viewpoint used in
\cite{CzabarkaSinggihSzekely2021,ChenChen2026}.  In the cyclic 13-layer
periodic template considered here, after normalizing the minimum weighted
degree to one, a period of total mass $M$ advances the eventual diameter by
13 while contributing $M\delta$ vertices after scaling and blow-up.  Beating
the coefficient $8/3$ within that template therefore requires
$13>(8/3)M$, or
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-mass}
 M<\frac{39}{8}.
\end{equation}

Our contribution is an exact obstruction to \eqref{eq:critical-mass} in the
one-high-boundary, four-slot submodel.  It does not address periods with two
or more high boundaries, layers of cardinality greater than four, nonlocal
edges, or arbitrary $K_7$-free graphs.

\section{The weighted cyclic model}

Let $H$ be a finite simple graph whose vertex set is partitioned into 13
nonempty layers
\[
 V(H)=\bigsqcup_{i\in\Zthirteen}L_i,
 \qquad 1\leq c_i:=|L_i|\leq4.
\]
Edges are permitted only within one layer or between cyclically consecutive
layers.  A weight is a map $w:V(H)\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}$.  Its weighted open
degree at $v$ is
\[
 d_w(v)=\sum_{u\in N_H(v)}w(u),
\]
and its total mass is $M(w)=\sum_{v\in V(H)}w(v)$.  Call the boundary
$\{i,i+1\}$ \emph{high} when $c_i+c_{i+1}\geq7$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Suppose that $H$ is $K_7$-free, exactly one boundary is high, and
$d_w(v)\geq1$ for every $v\in V(H)$.  Then
\[
 M(w)\geq\frac{4154}{851}
       =\frac{39}{8}+\frac{43}{6808}>\frac{39}{8}.
\]
In particular, any graph in this four-slot cyclic model having total mass
strictly below $39/8$ has at least two high boundaries.
\end{theorem}

Allowing zero weights makes the statement at least as strong as the
positive-weight formulation used for literal blow-ups.  The zero weights are
also convenient in the monotonicity reduction below.

\section{Reduction to 480 cases}

Rotate the unique high boundary to $\{0,1\}$.  Reflection permits us to take
$(c_0,c_1)=(3,4)$ or $(4,4)$.  Every other adjacent sum is at most six.

\begin{lemma}[Edge completion]\label{lem:completion}
It is enough to consider graphs in which every permitted edge outside
$L_0\cup L_1$ is present and the graph induced by $L_0\cup L_1$ is an
edge-maximal $K_7$-free graph.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every clique in a 13-layer cyclic-local graph lies in one layer or in two
consecutive layers.  Away from $\{0,1\}$, each such two-layer union has at
most six vertices, so adding all permitted edges there cannot create a
$K_7$.  Within $L_0\cup L_1$, add edges only until the induced graph is
edge-maximal subject to being $K_7$-free.  Adding edges can only increase
weighted open degrees.  Hence the feasible set for the completed graph
contains the feasible set for the original graph, and a mass lower bound for
every completion is also a lower bound for the original graph.
\end{proof}

For a $3+4$ junction, an edge-maximal completion is $K_7$ with one edge
deleted.  Up to permutations inside the two layers, the deleted edge is
inside the 3-layer, inside the 4-layer, or crossing, giving three types.

For a $4+4$ junction, let $F$ be the set of edges missing from $K_8$.  The
graph is $K_7$-free exactly when, for each vertex $v$, some edge of $F$ avoids
$v$.  Equivalently, the edges in $F$ have empty common intersection.  Every
inclusion-minimal family of 2-subsets with empty common intersection is either
two disjoint edges or the three edges of a triangle: if no two edges are
disjoint, pairwise intersection together with empty total intersection forces
the triangle.  Direct orbit enumeration under $S_4\times S_4$ gives ten
types.  The verifier regenerates all 266 labelled minimal families of these
two forms and checks that the ten stored representatives are exhaustive.

It remains to reduce the possible layer-cardinality vectors.  Order vectors
coordinatewise, holding $c_0,c_1$ fixed and retaining the condition that no
other boundary is high.

\begin{lemma}[Cardinality dominance]\label{lem:dominance}
It suffices to check coordinatewise-maximal admissible cardinality vectors.
Such a vector is maximal exactly when every ordinary coordinate $c_i<4$
with $i\notin\{0,1\}$ is incident with an ordinary boundary whose endpoint
sum is six.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose an ordinary layer can be enlarged while preserving admissibility.
Give each added vertex weight zero.  Existing weighted degrees do not
decrease.  A new vertex has the old neighborhood of any old vertex in its
layer, together with that old vertex itself, so its weighted degree is at
least one.  Thus enlarging a layer cannot increase the minimum feasible mass.
Checking a maximal vector therefore lower-bounds every vector below it.

An ordinary coordinate below four can be increased precisely when neither of
its incident ordinary boundaries has sum six.  This proves the stated
characterization.
\end{proof}

Exhaustive integer enumeration gives 60 anchored maximal $3+4$ vectors and
51 anchored maximal $4+4$ vectors.  Reflection fixing the high boundary
reduces the latter to 30 representatives.  The topology types are closed
under this reflection.  The number of remaining cases is therefore
\[
 60\cdot3+30\cdot10=480.
\]

\section{Exact linear-program certificates}

Fix one completed graph from the preceding reduction and let $A$ be its
adjacency matrix.  Its minimum feasible mass is the optimum of the covering
linear program
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primal}
 \min\ \one^{\mathsf T}w
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad Aw\geq\one,\quad w\geq0.
\end{equation}
The dual packing program is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 \max\ \one^{\mathsf T}y
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad A^{\mathsf T}y\leq\one,\quad y\geq0.
\end{equation}
Thus any rational vector feasible for \eqref{eq:dual} is, by weak duality, an
exact lower-bound certificate for \eqref{eq:primal}.

The accompanying verifier constructs every cardinality vector, every
junction orbit, and every adjacency matrix.  SciPy's interface to HiGHS
\cite{VirtanenEtAl2020,HuangfuHall2018} is used only to discover a candidate
dual vector.  Each coordinate is then reconstructed as a
\texttt{fractions.Fraction}; the verifier checks nonnegativity, every one of
the inequalities $A^{\mathsf T}y\leq\one$, and the objective over
$\mathbb Q$.  If reconstruction or an exact inequality fails, execution
aborts.  This separation of search from exact checking follows the same
proof-carrying principle used in other symmetry-reduced finite graph
certificates, for example \cite{202608.1658}.

All 480 cases have a checked dual objective at least $4154/851$.  The least
objective among the generated certificates occurs for
\[
 (c_0,\ldots,c_{12})=(3,4,2,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3)
\]
with the missing junction edge inside $L_0$.  One certificate, grouped by
layers and with a common denominator 851, has numerator blocks
\begin{align*}
 &(98,0,98);\ (153,153,153,153);\ (98,98);\ (47,47,47);\\
 &(187,187,187);\ (112,112,112);\ (22,22,22);\\
 &(157,157,157);\ (157,157,157);\ (22,22,22);\\
 &(112,112,112);\ (187,187,187);\ (47,47,47).
\end{align*}
Its coordinates sum to $4154/851$.  The exact verifier checks its dual
inequalities as part of the same uniform run.  Since every reduced case has a
rational dual of value at least this number, weak duality proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Reproducibility, limitations, and disclosure}

Run the ancillary program from the project root with
\begin{center}
\texttt{python3 evidence/one\_high\_junction.py}.
\end{center}
The audited run used Python 3.10.12, NumPy 2.2.6, and SciPy 1.15.3.  It
reports the 60 and 30 cardinality representatives, the three and ten junction
types, all 480 exact certificate checks, and the least certified value above.
The floating-point optimizer is outside the proof's trust boundary: only the
subsequent rational inequalities are used as evidence.

The theorem leaves untouched all configurations with two or more high
boundaries.  Missing edges at multiple high boundaries can interact through
shared layers, so their topology and dominance reductions require a separate
analysis.  The theorem also says nothing about larger layers, edges outside
the cyclic locality rule, or general $K_7$-free graphs.  In particular, it
neither proves nor disproves the $k=6$ instance of
\eqref{eq:diameter-conjecture}.

OpenAI Codex assisted with literature search, the initial search-space and
code design, exact-computation organization, adversarial review, and
manuscript preparation.  Language-model output is not mathematical evidence.
The stated finite theorem rests on the human-readable reductions above and
the accompanying exact verifier; no proof assistant was used.
