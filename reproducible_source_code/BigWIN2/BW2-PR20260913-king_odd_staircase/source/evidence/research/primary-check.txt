Nonempty rule
An impartial chess game is a pair (M,\lambda) , where M\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}\setminus\{(0,0)\} and \lambda\in\mathbb{Y}^{+} . We refer to M as a moveset and an element of M as a move . On their turn, the players choose a move (i,j)\in M , such that \lambda[i,j]\in Y^{+} and move to \lambda[i,j] , passing the turn. The above implies that impartial chess games are finite and short 1 1 1 For a definition of finite and short impartial game we refer the reader to [ 4 ] . . In addition, we define (i,j)^{+} as the set \{(ki,kj)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{>0}} . The impartial chess games presented below, which we study in sections Sections 3.4 , 3.3 , 3.5 and 3.6 , differ in the choice of moveset.
