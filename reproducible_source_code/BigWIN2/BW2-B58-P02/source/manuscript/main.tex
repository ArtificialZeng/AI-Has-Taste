\begin{abstract}
Xu, Fu, Qiao, and Tao recently used a special $54$-uniform Brinkhuis
$952$-triple, exact deletion-event volumes, and an asymmetric Lov\'asz local
lemma to certify a non-strict normalized adjacent-interval
insertion--deletion gap of $1/227$ over the ternary alphabet.  We keep their
codebook, event and dependency construction, and charges
$\alpha=51/100000$ and $\beta=3/10$ fixed, and certify the sharper gap
$1/226$.  The denominator change creates two new low-deletion shells: total
length $227$ at edit distance one and total length $454$ at edit distance
two.  Exact exhaustive checks show that both shells are empty; a recomputed
exact-rational local-lemma envelope then has positive margins.  Thus, within
this fixed framework, every length admits a ternary word with weak gap
$1/226$, and hence an $\varepsilon$-synchronization string for every
$\varepsilon>225/226$.  No denominator below $226$ is tested here, so the
least denominator in the fixed framework remains unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and statement}

Synchronization strings were introduced as deterministic index strings for
insertion and deletion channels \cite{HaeuplerShahrasbi2017}.  Cheng,
Haeupler, Li, Shahrasbi, and Wu showed that four symbols suffice for a fixed
parameter while two symbols do not suffice for arbitrarily long strings
\cite{ChengEtAl2019}.  Xu, Fu, Qiao, and Tao subsequently settled the ternary
alphabet question and, in the quantitative part of their paper, certified a
weak adjacent-interval gap $1/227$ using a computer-assisted $54$-uniform
Brinkhuis construction \cite{XuEtAl2026}.  The present note concerns only a
one-step improvement of that quantitative certificate.  A bounded search of
the literature located no treatment of this exact $1/226$ refinement, but
that search is not a claim of priority.

For finite words $X,Y$, let
\[
  \ED(X,Y)=|X|+|Y|-2\LCS(X,Y)
\]
be insertion--deletion edit distance.  If $S$ has length $n$, put
\[
 g(S)=\min_{1\leq i<j<k\leq n+1}
 \frac{\ED(S[i,j),S[j,k))}{k-i}.
\]
Here intervals are half open, and both adjacent factors are nonempty.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Fix the canonical special $54$-Brinkhuis $952$-triple and the exact event,
dependency, and conditional-mass construction of Xu--Fu--Qiao--Tao, with
charges
\[
             \alpha=\frac{51}{100000},\qquad \beta=\frac3{10}.
\]
For every integer $n\geq1$, there exists $S\in\{0,1,2\}^n$ such that
\[
 \ED(S[i,j),S[j,k))\geq\frac{k-i}{226}
 \quad(1\leq i<j<k\leq n+1).
\]
Equivalently, $g(S)\geq1/226$.  This theorem does not assert that $226$ is
the least denominator for which the fixed framework works.
\end{theorem}

The strict synchronization-string convention asks for
$\ED(S[i,j),S[j,k))>(1-\varepsilon)(k-i)$.  Therefore
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} supplies an $\varepsilon$-synchronization string for
every $\varepsilon>225/226$, but makes no assertion at equality.

\section{The fixed framework}

We recall only the ingredients needed to identify what changes when the
denominator is lowered.  Sollami, Douglas, and Liebmann constructed the
special $54$-Brinkhuis $952$-triple used here \cite{SollamiEtAl2016}.  For a
ternary square-free outer word $T=T_1\cdots T_N$, independently choose one of
the $952$ length-$54$ images of each occurrence.  Every resulting word is
square-free.  The image family for one outer letter is closed under reversal,
and the other two image families are its cyclic alphabet shifts.

For adjacent lengths $a,b\geq1$, write $s=a+b$.  A direct bad event for
denominator $D$ is relevant when $s\geq D+1$ and
$D|a-b|<s$.  If $e=\ED(X,Y)$, introduce deletion counts
$u=a-\LCS(X,Y)$ and $v=b-\LCS(X,Y)$, so that
\[
 v-u=b-a,\qquad e=u+v,
\]
and the bad-event volume sums over the integer pairs satisfying $De<s$.
The two directional raw volumes are
\[
 \binom au\binom bv3^u
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \binom au\binom bv3^v.
\]
Multiplying by the appropriate fragment-aware conditional point masses and
taking the smaller directional estimate gives the exact bound $p_D(a,b)$ of
\cite[Appendices D--E]{XuEtAl2026}.  A retained ordered type receives charge
\[
             x_D(a,b)=p_D(a,b)e^{\alpha s+\beta}.
\]

The asymmetric local-lemma criterion used in the source paper
\cite{ErdosLovasz1975,XuEtAl2026} is the following fixed implication.  Put
\[
 R_0(D)=\sum_{a,b}\frac{x_D(a,b)}{1-x_D(a,b)},\qquad
 R_1(D)=\sum_{a,b}s\frac{x_D(a,b)}{1-x_D(a,b)},
\]
where the sums run over retained ordered types and include the certified
analytic tail.  If every omitted event is separately proved empty, every
charge is in the prescribed range, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lll}
             R_0(D)<\alpha,\qquad 109R_0(D)+R_1(D)<\beta,
\end{equation}
then the local lemma simultaneously avoids all direct bad events.  Together
with square-freeness and the separately empty shells, this yields words of
every length by taking prefixes.  We do not alter this implication, its
constant $109$, or its conditional probabilities.

\section{The two new shells}

\begin{lemma}[Shell difference]\label{lem:difference}
For deletion total $e$, a volume term present at denominator $226$ but absent
at denominator $227$ has
\[
       226e<s\leq227e,\qquad s\equiv e\pmod 2.
\]
Consequently the only newly relevant shell with $e=1$ is $s=227$, and the
only one with $e=2$ is $s=454$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first two inequalities are exactly the respective strict event-volume
conditions.  Since $e=s-2\LCS(X,Y)$, $e$ and $s$ have the same parity.  The
two stated conclusions follow by inspecting the integer in the indicated
interval for $e=1,2$.
\end{proof}

Terms newly admitted for $e\geq3$ are not discarded: they remain in the
recomputed exact charge sum or its denominator-dependent analytic tail.  The
published empty shells for denominator $227$ remain statements about the
fixed codebook.  Thus the complete exclusion set used below is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exclusions}
 \{227,229,231,\ldots,463\}\cup\{454,456,462,464\}.
\end{equation}
Only $227$ and $454$ in this set require new certificates.

The one-deletion verifier covers the two orientations with lengths $114$ and
$113$, all $54$ macroblock offsets, every deletion position, and all
normalized square-free outer contexts.  Every one of the $132{,}240$ cases
contains a local codebook incompatibility; there is no residual constraint
instance.  Its first incompatible-edge histogram is
\[
                 (99{,}804,\ 32{,}022,\ 414).
\]
As a control, the same implementation at the published total $229$ reproduces
all $135{,}240$ empty cases and the published histogram
$(100{,}219,27{,}077,7{,}944)$.

At total $454$, the possible length modes for edit distance two are
\[
                 (228,226),\quad(226,228),\quad(227,227).
\]
The verifier checks all offsets and all deletion pairs.  The exact number of
patterns is
\[
  2\cdot54\binom{228}{2}+54\cdot227^2=5{,}577{,}390.
\]
The three mode counts are respectively
$1{,}397{,}412$, $1{,}397{,}412$, and $2{,}782{,}566$.  Every pattern has a
local macroblock relation incompatible with each of the three possible outer
alphabet differences; hence all are universal kills and there are no outer-
context or constraint-satisfaction residuals.  At the published control total
$456$, the same code reproduces the expected $5{,}626{,}584$ universal kills,
split as $1{,}409{,}724$, $1{,}409{,}724$, and $2{,}807{,}136$.

These are finite exact statements, not statistical tests.  The implementation
uses integer equality relations.  Its compressed diagonal-run representation
was also expanded and compared with direct survivor-coordinate pairing on
$614{,}088$ patterns covering every mode, offset, and deletion pattern in the
tested half-length range, with no discrepancy.

\section{Exact envelope and proof}

With the exclusions in \eqref{eq:exclusions}, bilateral conditioning, and the
fixed charges, the denominator-$226$ calculation retains $34{,}946$ finite
ordered event types over totals $227$ through $4000$.  Exact rational
arithmetic gives the following outward-rounded decimal displays:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
quantity & certified bound \\
\midrule
$\max x_{226}(a,b)$ & $\leq0.000068906109636061649419<1/6000$ \\
$R_0(226)$ & $\leq0.000509381931109708656752$ \\
$R_1(226)$ & $\leq0.240748491924150728186391$ \\
$\alpha-R_0(226)$ & $\geq0.000000618068890291343247$ \\
$\beta-(109R_0(226)+R_1(226))$
 & $\geq0.003728877584891028227619$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The tail beyond the finite range is recomputed for $D=226$.  Its rational
decay exponent satisfies
\[
      c=\frac{100417}{4237500}>\frac1{43},
      \qquad e^{-c}<\frac{43}{44},
\]
and its first possible charge obeys
\[
             2\cdot952^2\left(\frac{43}{44}\right)^{4001}<\frac1{6000}.
\]
The corresponding geometric zeroth- and first-moment sums are included in
$R_0$ and $R_1$.  A second, range-reduced exact implementation independently
reproduces both positive margins.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:difference} and the exhaustive calculations prove that the two
new low-deletion shells are empty.  The other totals in
\eqref{eq:exclusions} are the denominator-independent empty codebook shells
proved in \cite[Appendix D]{XuEtAl2026}.  Every newly admitted term of higher
deletion total is retained in the recomputed finite sum or tail.  The displayed
strict margins verify \eqref{eq:lll}, so the fixed asymmetric local-lemma
criterion avoids all remaining direct bad events.

It remains to check that the case division covers every adjacent pair.  If
$s\leq226$ and $a\ne b$, then
$\ED(X,Y)\geq|a-b|\geq1\geq s/226$.  If $a=b$, square-freeness rules out
$\ED(X,Y)=0$; parity then gives $\ED(X,Y)\geq2\geq s/226$.  At an excluded
odd total up to $463$, a violation can only have edit distance one and is
excluded by the one-deletion shells.  At an excluded even total in
$\{454,456,462,464\}$, a violation can only have edit distance zero or two;
square-freeness excludes zero and the two-deletion shells exclude two.  Length
imbalance handles the non-relevant ordered types, and all other bad events are
among those avoided by the local lemma.

For a requested $n$, take an outer square-free word of length
$\lceil n/54\rceil$, choose a local-lemma-admissible vector of Brinkhuis
images, and take the length-$n$ prefix of the resulting word.  Every adjacent
pair in the prefix is an adjacent pair in the full word, so the required
inequality is inherited.
\end{proof}

\section{Reproducibility and limitations}

The supplementary calculation consists of the canonical $952$-word codebook,
exact one- and two-deletion shell verifiers, machine-readable result records,
and two exact-rational local-lemma implementations.  The shell controls at
totals $229$ and $456$ reproduce the source artifact's records.  Python
$3.10.12$ was used for the reported runs.  Node.js was unavailable, so the
two-deletion checker was translated from the source JavaScript verifier; the
pairing-equivalence comparisons and the reproduced $456$ control address that
translation risk.  No floating-point comparison is proof-critical.

Separating discovery code from exact certificate checking is also used in a
recent finite ternary-model study, where decisive comparisons are integer or
rational and a standard-library verifier reconstructs the finite objects
\cite{ZengEtAl2026}.  That work is cited only for this reproducibility design;
its geometric theorems and finite libraries play no role here.

The exact conclusion is restricted to the fixed codebook, event volumes,
dependency construction, and charges in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  No value
$D<226$ was tested or ruled out.  In particular, this note neither determines
the least working denominator in the fixed framework nor claims a globally
optimal ternary synchronization parameter.

\paragraph{Computational and AI assistance.}
The shell enumerations and local-lemma bounds were performed by deterministic
programs using exact integer or rational arithmetic, and the decisive claim
was separately audited before manuscript preparation.  AI assistance was used
for code inspection, literature screening, and drafting.  The mathematical
conclusion depends on the stated proofs and reproducible exact computations,
not on model output or confidence scores.
