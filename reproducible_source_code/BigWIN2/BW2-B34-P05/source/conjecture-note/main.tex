\begin{abstract}
The original problem---whether every finite simple triangle-free graph of
maximum degree at most six is five-colourable---remains unresolved, and the
partition conjecture formulated here is unproved.  The conjecture asks for a
partition into a bipartite induced subgraph of maximum degree at most two and
a subcubic induced subgraph.  It is motivated by the standard bounded-degree
decomposition and would imply the original five-colour statement.  We record
exact partial reductions, two exact obstructions to stronger proof routes,
directly checkable partitions for 2,298 finite graphs, bounded solver searches
through order ten, and an independent adversarial audit.  No proof trace was
exported for the bounded unsatisfiability results, no exhaustive search at
order eleven or above was completed, and no target-near critical catalogue
from order thirty-three was generated.  Thus the evidence is finite and does
not prove either claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Original problem and verified frontier}

All graphs in this note are finite, simple, and undirected.  A proper
$k$-colouring is a map from the vertex set to $\{1,\ldots,k\}$ that assigns
different colours to adjacent vertices.  We write $\chi(G)$ for the least
such $k$, $\Delta(G)$ for the maximum degree, and $G[X]$ for the subgraph
induced by $X\subseteq V(G)$.  Empty and disconnected graphs are included.

The original question is the following specialization of Reed's conjectured
bound \cite{Reed1998}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:original}
  G\text{ triangle-free and }\Delta(G)\leq 6
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \chi(G)\leq 5.
\end{equation}
The recorded source search found no accepted proof or counterexample to
\eqref{eq:original}.  In particular, the current triangle-free frontier is
still presented as a conjecture by Kang and Rosenfeld; their theorem applies
for maximum degree at least $524$, not at degree six \cite{KangRosenfeld2025}.
Accordingly, this note does not claim a resolution.

Brooks' theorem \cite{Brooks1941} makes the part with $\Delta(G)\leq5$
automatic, so only maximum degree exactly six is difficult for the
five-colour statement.  Goedgebeur found that the least order of an
unrestricted triangle-free $6$-chromatic graph lies between $32$ and $40$
\cite{Goedgebeur2020}.  Abrishami and Erfanian subsequently verified Reed's
bound through order $32$ for triangle-free graphs whose chromatic number is
not five \cite{AbrishamiErfanian2023}.  Hence a counterexample to
\eqref{eq:original}, which Brooks forces to have chromatic number six, has at
least $33$ vertices.  The known order-$40$ upper construction is not a
counterexample to \eqref{eq:original} unless its maximum degree also satisfies
the degree-six cap.

\section{Proved partial structure}

The following statements are partial reductions.  None resolves
\eqref{eq:original}.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded-degree decomposition]\label{prop:decomposition}
Every graph $G$ with $\Delta(G)\leq6$ has a partition
$V(G)=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$ such that
\[
  \Delta(G[A])\leq2
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \Delta(G[B])\leq3.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a partition minimizing the nonnegative integer
$4|E(G[A])|+3|E(G[B])|$.  If a vertex $v\in A$ has at least three neighbours
in $A$, then it has at most three neighbours in $B$.  Moving $v$ from $A$ to
$B$ changes the potential by at most $-4\cdot3+3\cdot3=-3$, a contradiction.
Similarly, if $v\in B$ has at least four neighbours in $B$, then it has at
most two neighbours in $A$, and moving it to $A$ changes the potential by at
most $-3\cdot4+4\cdot2=-4$.  Thus neither violation occurs.
\end{proof}

This is the $(2,3)$ instance of the classical maximum-degree decomposition
framework of Lov\'asz \cite{Lovasz1966}.  For comparison, Matamala's adjacent
theorem can make the first induced part a maximum-degree-two forest at the
cost of allowing maximum degree four on the other side
\cite{Matamala2007}.  Proposition \ref{prop:decomposition} has the desired
degree bounds, but its degree-two side may contain odd cycles.

\begin{proposition}[Necessary structure of an extremal counterexample]
If \eqref{eq:original} is false, there is a counterexample $G$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $G$ is $6$-vertex-critical and every vertex has degree five or six;
  \item every two degree-five vertices have distance at most two;
  \item the set $L$ of degree-five vertices satisfies $|L|\leq26$ when it is
        nonempty; and
  \item for each degree-five vertex $v$ and each $5$-colouring of $G-v$, all
        five colours occur on $N(v)$, and the neighbours receiving any chosen
        pair of colours lie in the same bichromatic component.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a counterexample with minimum order and, subject to that, maximum size.
Brooks' theorem gives $\chi(G)=6$.  Vertex minimality makes $G$
$6$-vertex-critical, hence its minimum degree is at least five, while the
hypothesis gives the upper bound six.  If two nonadjacent degree-five
vertices $x,y$ had no common neighbour, adding $xy$ would preserve
triangle-freeness, the degree cap, and non-five-colourability while increasing
the size.  This proves the distance-two assertion.

For $v\in L$, suppose that $t$ of its five neighbours lie in $L$.
Triangle-freeness and the degree cap give the deliberately overlap-ignoring
upper bound
\[
 |L|\leq1+t+4t+5(5-t)=26.
\]
Finally, a $5$-colouring of $G-v$ must use every colour on $N(v)$, or it
extends to $v$.  If two differently coloured neighbours lie in different
components of their bichromatic subgraph, swapping the two colours in one
component again frees a colour for $v$, which is impossible.
\end{proof}

The degree-five vertices in a spanning $6$-critical subgraph additionally
induce a Gallai forest \cite{Gallai1963}; triangle-freeness restricts its
nontrivial blocks to edges and odd cycles.  The handshake lemma makes the
number of degree-five vertices even.  Applying the Gao--Postle density bound
for $K_4$-free $6$-critical graphs \cite{GaoPostle2018} gives at least
\[
  \left\lceil\frac{316n-1890}{525}\right\rceil
\]
degree-six vertices in an $n$-vertex counterexample.  At $n=33$ this is at
least $17$.  These necessary conditions have not been combined into a
contradiction.

\begin{lemma}[A minimal obstruction to the partition has no leaf]
If the partition assertion in Conjecture \ref{conj:C001} is false, a
vertex-minimal counterexample has minimum degree at least two.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After deleting an isolated vertex, add it to either part.  After deleting a
leaf $v$, add $v$ to the part not containing its unique neighbour.  No old
induced degree changes and no cycle is created in the first part.
\end{proof}

\section{The robust candidate conjecture}

The sole candidate promoted by the independent communication-quality audit
is the following.  It remains unproved.

\begin{conjecture}[Registry C001]\label{conj:C001}
For every finite simple triangle-free graph \(G\) with \(\Delta(G)\le 6\), there is a partition \(V(G)=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B\) such that \(\Delta(G[A])\le2\), \(G[A]\) is bipartite, and \(\Delta(G[B])\le3\).
\par\smallskip
{\raggedright\noindent\emph{Plain-language statement.} Every finite simple triangle-free graph of \mbox{maximum} degree at most six has a vertex partition into a bipartite part of \mbox{induced} \mbox{maximum} degree at most two and a part of \mbox{induced} \mbox{maximum} degree at most three.\par}
\end{conjecture}

Conjecture \ref{conj:C001} is not a renamed colouring assertion.  It adds
parity control to Proposition \ref{prop:decomposition}: because $G[A]$ has
maximum degree two, requiring it to be bipartite is exactly the exclusion of
odd-cycle components.  If the conjecture is true, two colours suffice on
$A$, and Brooks' theorem gives three colours on the triangle-free subcubic
graph $G[B]$; disjoint palettes then prove \eqref{eq:original}.  No audited
example establishes that the converse implication fails, so no strictness
claim is made.

The statement is natural as a missing parity condition and is locally
certificate-friendly.  Its sharpness is less certain: the closest theorem
located in the recorded search, Matamala's result cited above, permits degree
four on the second side when the first side is acyclic, and the present record
contains no theorem showing that each of C001's numerical bounds is
individually best possible.

\section{Research routes and failure diagnosis}

\subsection{Odd-cycle elimination}
One proof route refines the minimization in Proposition
\ref{prop:decomposition} by secondarily minimizing the number and total length
of odd cycles in $G[A]$.  The missing implication is a finite alternating
exchange that removes a shortest such cycle while preserving both degree
bounds.  No such exchange has been proved.

A stronger fixed-partition induction is exactly false.  A serialized
eight-vertex tree comes with a valid C001 partition after deletion of a
degree-two vertex, yet none of the three one-vertex insertions extends that
fixed partition: one produces a same-side edge in the proposed bipartition of
$A$, one raises an old $A$-degree from two to three, and one raises an old
$B$-degree from three to four.  The full tree has a different C001 partition.
Thus the example obstructs the induction method, not Conjecture
\ref{conj:C001}; any repair must permit a multi-vertex recolouring.

\subsection{Independent two-domination}
Another route sought an independent set $I$ such that every vertex outside
$I$ has at least two neighbours in $I$.  In a graph of maximum degree six,
deleting such an $I$ would leave maximum degree at most four.  This universal
lemma is false even for triangle-free graphs with all degrees in
$\{5,6\}$.  The exact $16$-vertex graph encoded by
\verb|OqEK`UGDWiKsWIQe@QPhB| has degree multiset $5^8 6^8$, is
triangle-free and $3$-colourable, and has no independent two-dominating set.
An exhaustive verifier checks all $422$ independent sets.  This refutes only
the auxiliary lemma; it is not a counterexample to \eqref{eq:original}.

\subsection{Direct disproof search}
No triangle-free graph respecting the degree cap and failing five-colourability
was found.  The target-relevant exhaustive range starts at order $33$, and no
catalogue of $6$-vertex-critical degree-$\{5,6\}$ graphs at or above that
order was generated.  Negative results on smaller or sampled graphs therefore
have no direct universal consequence for \eqref{eq:original}.

\section{Exact evidence and reproducibility}

The principal positive dataset contains $2{,}298$ serialized graphs with
explicit C001 partitions: all $173$ eligible unlabelled graphs in the
NetworkX graph atlas through order seven, five named boundary graphs, and
$2{,}120$ deterministic greedy triangle-free graphs (forty at every order
from eight through sixty).  The named cases include Petersen, Chv\'atal,
Gr\"otzsch, Clebsch, and $K_{6,6}$; the generated sample includes a
$60$-vertex $6$-regular case.  A separate checker reconstructed each graph
and verified triangle-freeness, the degree cap, partition exhaustiveness,
bipartiteness, and both induced-degree bounds.

An explicit three-state SMT encoding searched all labelled graphs through
order ten by alternately choosing graph edges and blocking every potential
$A_0/A_1/B$ assignment.  With Z3 version 4.15.3 it returned unsatisfiable at
orders eight, nine, and ten after respectively $6{,}561$, $19{,}683$, and
$59{,}049$ assignment blockers.  These answers do not have exported DRAT,
LRAT, or QBF proof traces, so they are reported as bounded solver evidence,
not independently checkable universal certificates.

A lazy order-eleven CEGIS run returned unsatisfiable only in the imposed
minimum-degree-five stratum, after $462$ graph candidates and $125.294$
seconds.  That stratum is target-motivated but is not a proved reduction for
C001.  The unrestricted run timed out after $302.910$ seconds and $791$
blockers; the sound minimum-degree-two run timed out after $303.820$ seconds
and $808$ blockers; a degree-ordered variant returned unknown after $300.028$
seconds and $354$ blockers.  A minimum-degree-five order-twelve run also
timed out after $302.746$ seconds and $430$ blockers.  Every timeout and
unknown result is inconclusive.

The independent audit regenerated the $2{,}298$-record dataset byte for byte,
replayed both exact route-obstruction witnesses, repeated labelled synthesis
through order ten, tested $73$ canonical construction cases through order
$462$, checked $530$ fresh random graphs at orders eight through sixty, and
cross-checked three solver encodings at order six.  It found neither a C001
counterexample nor a certificate mismatch in these declared finite scopes.
This was an independent model context and evidence audit, not human peer
review and not a proof of the conjecture.

The reproduction commands, run from the project root, are:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 code/partition_probe.py \
  --seed 3405 --random-per-order 40 \
  --output data/partition-probe-seed3405.json
python3 code/verify_partition_data.py \
  data/partition-probe-seed3405.json
python3 code/synthesize_partition_counterexample.py 10 \
  --timeout-ms 300000
python3 code/verify_false_fixed_partition_extension.py \
  data/false-fixed-partition-extension.json
python3 code/verify_false_independent_2dom.py \
  data/false-independent-2dom.json
\end{verbatim}
The same synthesis command with orders eight and nine reproduces the smaller
bounded searches.  Solver output should not be called a certificate unless a
separately checkable proof trace is added.

\section{Adversarial falsification and literature boundary}

The counterexample-oriented tests included empty and disconnected graphs,
isolated vertices and leaves, trees, odd and even cycles, every
$K_{a,b}$ with $1\leq a,b\leq6$, odd-cycle blowups, Andr\'asfai graphs,
Kneser odd graphs of degrees three through six, standard cubic examples,
maximum-degree-six cases, and six $6$-regular cases.  The fixed-partition tree
was deliberately retained to prevent the refuted induction assumption from
entering the search.

A fresh literature search on 2026-09-04 used the exact statement, synonyms,
equivalent partition formulations, the constants two and three, and the
closest Lov\'asz and Matamala theorems.  It inspected publisher, journal, and
arXiv records, including recent partition work.  No equivalent C001 statement
was found in that finite search.  This is a bounded search report, not a claim
of priority, exhaustive novelty, or community recognition.

The fastest decisive falsifier is a concrete finite triangle-free graph
$G$ with $\Delta(G)\leq6$, together with a separately checkable proof that no
$A_0/A_1/B$ assignment satisfies the three partition conditions.  The most
immediate computational target is a proof-producing unrestricted order-eleven
SAT or QBF encoding.  A positive partition, a timeout, or an untraced
unsatisfiable solver response is not such a falsifier.

\section{Potential value and research agenda}

If C001 is true, it proves \eqref{eq:original} and identifies parity control
inside a standard degree decomposition as the endpoint mechanism.  Its
positive witnesses are also cheaper to verify than global non-five-colourability.
If C001 is false, the witness has two informative possibilities: a
$6$-chromatic witness simultaneously refutes \eqref{eq:original}, whereas a
$5$-colourable witness refutes only this partition route and isolates a real
parity-versus-degree obstruction.

The highest-value proof test is to classify blocked moves around a shortest
odd $A$-cycle and decide whether a bounded multi-vertex alternating exchange
lowers the secondary odd-cycle potential.  In parallel, a critical-graph
generator can impose the degree, distance-two, Gallai-forest, density, and
Kempe constraints above starting at order $33$, with proof-producing
five-colourability checks.  These two tests attack the conjectural partition
and the original statement from different directions.

\section{Limitations and open scope}

Both \eqref{eq:original} and Conjecture \ref{conj:C001} remain unresolved.
The evidence covers only finite graph sets and bounded solver instances.  In
particular, the record contains no completed exhaustive order-eleven search,
no proof trace for the solver exclusions through order ten, no unbounded
family theorem beyond the elementary decomposition, no general odd-cycle
exchange, and no exhaustive target-near graph generation from order $33$.
The literature search is finite and may have missed equivalent language or
unindexed work.  The independent audit certifies the precision,
reproducibility, falsification effort, disclosure, and bounded literature
wording of C001; it does not certify truth.  The conjecture may be refuted by
future exact computation or construction.
