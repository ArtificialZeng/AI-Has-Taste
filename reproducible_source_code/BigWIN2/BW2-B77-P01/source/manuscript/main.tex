\begin{abstract}
Baldacchino, Caro, and Mifsud ask which disconnected planar graphs are
eventually unavoidable under red--blue edge-colourings of maximal planar
graphs.  We settle one specialization negatively.  Let $S_{2,1,1}$ be the
five-vertex fork, and put $H=S_{2,1,1}\sqcup K_2$.  For every $m\geq 3$, the
bipyramid $\overline{K_2}+C_m$ has a colouring in which all apex--rim edges
are red and all rim edges are blue.  We prove that this is a maximal planar
graph and that it has no monochromatic, not necessarily induced, copy of
$H$.  Since these bipyramids realize every order at least five, no eventual
unavoidability threshold exists.  The broader classification of disconnected
graphs remains unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Context and statement}

Axenovich, Schade, Thomassen, and Ueckerdt call a graph planar unavoidable
when it has some planar Ramsey host~\cite{AxenovichEtAl2019}.  Baldacchino,
Caro, and Mifsud study a stronger eventual notion relative to a class
$\mathcal K$: every sufficiently large member of $\mathcal K$ must be a
Ramsey host~\cite{BaldacchinoCaroMifsud2026}.  We use precisely their latter
notion with $\mathcal K=\mathcal K_{\mathrm{MP}}$, the class of finite simple
maximal planar graphs (that is, edge-maximal planar graphs).

This edge-colouring notion should also be distinguished from small Ramsey
degrees for embeddings of ordered structures.  For example, the ordered edge
has small Ramsey degree three in the class of linearly ordered split graphs
with induced order-preserving embeddings~\cite{Zeng2026SmallRamsey}.  No such
ordered or induced-embedding parameter is used here: all target copies below
are ordinary, injective subgraph copies.

Let $F=S_{2,1,1}$ have distinct vertices $s,x,y,z,w$ and edge set
\[
 E(F)=\{sx,sy,sz,zw\}.
\]
Thus $s$ is the degree-three centre and $s$--$z$--$w$ is the arm of length
two.  Let $H=F\sqcup K_2$.  A monochromatic copy of $H$ therefore consists of
a monochromatic copy of $F$ and a same-colour edge whose two endpoints are
outside the five fork vertices; extra host edges are irrelevant.

The connected graph $F$ is eventually unavoidable in
$\mathcal K_{\mathrm{MP}}$: in fact,
$r_{\mathrm{MP}}(F,F)=9$~\cite[Theorem~12]{BaldacchinoCaroMifsud2026}.
The same paper asks for the disconnected diagonal classification
\cite[Problem~2]{BaldacchinoCaroMifsud2026}.  The following theorem shows that
adjoining just one disjoint edge reverses the conclusion for this fork.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every integer $m\geq 3$, let
\[
 B_m=\overline{K_2}+C_m,
\]
where the two vertices $a,b$ of $\overline{K_2}$ are called the apices.
Colour every apex--cycle edge red and every cycle edge blue.  Then $B_m$ is
maximal planar and contains neither a red nor a blue copy of
$S_{2,1,1}\sqcup K_2$.  Consequently, an avoiding maximal planar graph exists
at every order $n\geq 5$, and $H$ is not eventually unavoidable in
$\mathcal K_{\mathrm{MP}}$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Proof}

Write the rim cycle as $v_0v_1\dots v_{m-1}v_0$, with subscripts modulo $m$.
Embed this cycle as a Jordan curve on the sphere, put $a$ and $b$ in its two
complementary discs, and join each apex to every rim vertex within its own
disc.  The resulting embedding has the $2m$ triangular faces
\[
 av_iv_{i+1}\quad\text{and}\quad bv_iv_{i+1}
 \qquad (0\leq i<m).
\]
In particular, $B_m$ is planar and simple.  It has $m+2$ vertices and
$2m+m=3m=3(m+2)-6$ edges.  The planar edge bound shows that adding any missing
edge would destroy planarity, so $B_m$ is maximal planar.  This argument also
covers $m=3$, when $B_3$ is the triangular bipyramid $K_5-ab$.

The red spanning subgraph is exactly $K_{2,m}$, with parts
$\{a,b\}$ and $\{v_0,\ldots,v_{m-1}\}$.  Consider any injective red copy of
the fork $F$.  The image of its centre $s$ has at least three distinct red
neighbours.  Every rim vertex has red degree two, so $s$ must map to an apex.
Its three fork neighbours $x,y,z$ consequently map to distinct rim vertices.
The rim vertex receiving $z$ has only the two apices as red neighbours.  One
is already the image of $s$; injectivity therefore forces the distal vertex
$w$ to map to the other apex.  Hence every red fork uses both apices.  Every
red edge is incident with an apex, so no red edge has both endpoints outside
the vertices of a red fork.  Thus there is no red copy of $H$.

The blue spanning subgraph is exactly $C_m$ and has maximum degree two.  It
contains no copy of the fork, whose centre has three distinct neighbours, and
therefore contains no blue copy of $H$ either.  This proves the asserted
avoidance.

Finally, $|V(B_m)|=m+2$.  Given any proposed threshold $N$, take
$n=\max\{N,5\}$ and $m=n-2$.  The preceding construction gives an
$n$-vertex maximal planar graph with an avoiding colouring, which is exactly
the negation of eventual unavoidability.  This completes the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \qed

\section{Scope and assistance}

The theorem resolves only the graph $S_{2,1,1}\sqcup K_2$; it does not
classify disconnected planar targets.  The exact augmentation is not stated
separately in the inspected first version of
\cite{BaldacchinoCaroMifsud2026}.  Our comparison is limited to the cited
primary literature and bounded exact-case searches, and no exhaustive
priority claim is made.

A dependency-free program exhaustively checked labelled fork embeddings for
$3\leq m\leq18$, and a separate audit checked the construction through
$m=40$.  These computations were sanity checks for the encoding and the
boundary case; the universal conclusion rests only on the proof above.  An
AI system assisted with literature discovery, organization of proof audits,
and editorial work.  The mathematical argument was rederived from the stated
definitions and separately audited at the definition and quantifier level.
No proof assistant was used.
