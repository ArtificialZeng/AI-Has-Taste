# Corrected formal statement

Let \([7]=\{1,\ldots,7\}\), and let
\[
S_{3,7}=\{\sigma\subseteq[7]:|\sigma|\le 4\}.
\]
Thus the facets of this pure three-dimensional simplicial complex are exactly
the \(35\) four-subsets of \([7]\).

A sequence of distinct facets \(F_1,\ldots,F_m\) is a **partial shelling** of
\(S_{3,7}\) if it is a shelling order of the pure subcomplex generated by
these facets: for every \(2\le j\le m\),
\[
 \langle F_j\rangle\cap\langle F_1,\ldots,F_{j-1}\rangle
\]
is a nonempty pure two-dimensional simplicial complex.  Equivalently, every
face of that intersection is contained in a three-element face of the same
intersection.

The precise decision problem is:

> For every \(m\in\{1,\ldots,35\}\) and every partial shelling
> \(F_1,\ldots,F_m\), does there exist a permutation
> \(F_{m+1},\ldots,F_{35}\) of the remaining four-subsets such that
> \(F_1,\ldots,F_{35}\) is a shelling of \(S_{3,7}\)?

An affirmative answer proves that \(S_{3,7}\) is extendably shellable.  A
negative answer requires an explicit partial shelling for which no such
continuation exists.  Relabeling the seven vertices does not change either
property, so searches may quotient by the action of \(S_7\), provided orbit
coverage is certified.

## Terminology correction

The source phrase “seven vertices' three-dimensional simplex skeleton” is
read as the three-skeleton of the six-dimensional simplex on seven vertices,
not as the skeleton of a three-dimensional simplex.  The notation agrees
with Bolognini--Sentinelli: \(S_{d,n}\) has facets the \((d+1)\)-subsets of
\([n]\).

## Frontier as of 2026-09-02

The general Simon conjecture is false: a 2026 preprint constructs a
non-extendably-shellable \(S_{3,16}\), and cones give examples in every
dimension at least three.  That result does not decide \(S_{3,7}\).  Known
positive ranges \(d\le2\) and \(d\ge n-3\) leave \((n,d)=(7,3)\) as the
lexicographically/smallest-vertex unresolved parameter pair, subject to the
continuing literature audit recorded in `literature/claim_ledger.md`.
