## Title

Exact facial distance and minimizing face orbits of the \(3\times3\) Birkhoff polytope

## Original lead (preserved)

## Exact proposed statement

Let
\[
\mathcal B_3=\left\{X=(x_{ij})\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}:x_{ij}\ge0,
\ \sum_{j=1}^3x_{ij}=1\ (i\in[3]),\ \sum_{i=1}^3x_{ij}=1\ (j\in[3])\right\}
\]
with the Frobenius norm. For a nonempty face \(G\) of \(\mathcal B_3\), put
\[
\mathcal B_3[G]=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\operatorname{vert}(\mathcal B_3)\setminus G\bigr),
\qquad
d(G)=\operatorname{dist}\bigl(G,\mathcal B_3[G]\bigr).
\]
Determine exactly
\[
\Phi(\mathcal B_3)=
\min_{\substack{G\in\operatorname{faces}(\mathcal B_3)\\G\ne\mathcal B_3}}
d(G),
\]
and classify every minimizing face \(G\), up to the full natural symmetry group generated by independent row and column permutations and transposition,
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal B_3)\supseteq (S_3\times S_3)\rtimes C_2.
\]

The required output is both (i) the exact algebraic value of \(\Phi(\mathcal B_3)\) and (ii) the complete list of symmetry orbits of faces attaining it. A decimal approximation, one attaining face, or a search over only facets does not resolve the proposed statement.

## Quantifiers and conventions

- The minimum ranges over every nonempty proper face, including vertices and non-facet faces.
- Vertices are the six \(3\times3\) permutation matrices.
- Distance is Euclidean distance in \(\mathbb R^9\), equivalently the Frobenius norm on matrices, restricted to the four-dimensional affine hull of \(\mathcal B_3\).
- Orbit completeness is with respect to the stated row/column/transposition action; any claim that this is the entire affine-isometry group must be proved or avoided.

  \Phi(\mathcal B_n)\ge \frac1{\sqrt{n^2-1}},

## Precise delta and status caution

The delta is the complete exact \(n=3\) computation plus equality-orbit classification, not Conjecture 7.1 for arbitrary polytopes and not an asymptotic estimate for \(\mathcal B_n\). The source establishes that its own methods do not settle the Birkhoff case, but the exact \(\mathcal B_3\) refinement has not been exhaustively checked against all prior literature. Intake status is therefore **status-uncertain finite refinement**, not “verified open.”

## Decisive certificate

A resolution should contain a replayable finite certificate with:

1. the complete face list of \(\mathcal B_3\), for example via allowable support subgraphs of \(K_{3,3}\), together with a proof that no face is omitted;
2. canonical representatives and orbit sizes under row permutations, column permutations, and transposition;
3. for each face-orbit representative \(G\), an exact convex quadratic-program/KKT certificate for \(d(G)=\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{aff}G,\mathcal B_3[G])\), plus verification that the closest point in \(\operatorname{aff}G\) lies in \(G\) as guaranteed abstractly by Lemma 3.3 for the global minimizer;
4. exact comparisons among the resulting rational or algebraic squared distances; and
5. explicit equality witnesses for every minimizing orbit.

## Cheapest decisive route

Enumerate the six permutation vertices, identify all facial vertex subsets from the nine inequalities \(x_{ij}\ge0\), quotient the face list by the 72-element row/column/transposition action, and solve one exact small quadratic program per orbit. Squared distances should be kept rational whenever possible; otherwise use minimal polynomials and isolating intervals.

## Fast falsification

- Any omitted support-face orbit falsifies a claimed completeness certificate.
- Any face \(G\) with an exact value \(d(G)\) below the proposed global value falsifies that value.
- Two minimizing faces shown inequivalent under the stated group falsify an incomplete orbit list.
- Floating-point agreement alone is not a proof of either the minimum or orbit completeness.
