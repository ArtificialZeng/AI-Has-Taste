\begin{abstract}
For the Heilbronn problem in the unit square, the best known ten-point
construction is a three-parameter configuration introduced by Comellas and
Yebra.  We determine the maximum of the minimum triangle area over the entire
parameter cube, including unordered and degenerate parameter values.  If
$z_0$ is the root in $(3/10,1/3)$ of
$12z^3-27z^2+20z-4$, the exact maximum is
\[
 A_0=\frac{5z_0^2-4z_0^3}{8}
 =0.04653741958254177256\ldots.
\]
The proof combines a one-variable envelope on an ordered cell, an elementary
product inequality on a second cell, and an exact rational dyadic certificate
on the remaining cell.  The certificate has 2,893 nodes and is independently
checked using local Bernstein bounds.  We also classify the four parameter
triples attaining equality.  This is a partial result: it does not give an
upper bound for arbitrary ten-point configurations, so the unrestricted value
$\Delta_{10}$ remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For a ten-point configuration $P\subset[0,1]^2$, let
\[
 a(P)=\min_{\{p,q,r\}\subset P}\Area(pqr),\qquad
 \Delta_{10}=\max_{|P|=10}a(P).
\]
The boundary of the square is allowed, and collinear triples have area zero.
Compactness gives existence after the objective is extended to ordered
ten-tuples, since a tuple with a repeated point has value zero.

Comellas and Yebra~\cite{ComellasYebra2002} found the best known ten-point
construction by simulated annealing followed by exact local refinement.
Recent global-optimization work certifies the square problem through nine
points but leaves the unrestricted ten-point case open
\cite{MonjiModirKocuk2025,SudermannMerx2026}.  Sudermann--Merx also reproduces
the exact ten-point coordinates in its appendix
\cite[Appendix~A]{SudermannMerx2026}.

The source paper analyzes three active areas locally.  Its literal displayed
formulas for two of them on page~4 appear inconsistent with the point table;
the determinant formulas used below are derived directly from the coordinates
on page~6.  A later public research record gives an exact argument on the
ordered cell $0\le x\le y\le z\le1/2$ but does not cover the whole parameter
cube~\cite{TheoremDB2026}.  The contribution here is an exact theorem on all
$(x,y,z)\in[0,1]^3$, including the complementary order cells, together with an
independently checked finite certificate and an equality classification.

A dated search of arXiv, publisher records, and exact-formula web results on
August~30, 2026 found no earlier source stating the full-cube theorem.  This
novelty statement is limited to those searches.  Most importantly, the family
is a proper three-dimensional subset of the unrestricted twenty-coordinate
configuration space; no reduction from arbitrary configurations is claimed.

\section{The parameter family and the result}

For $x,y,z\in[0,1]$, define the ordered ten-tuple
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family}
\begin{split}
\CY(x,y,z)=(&(x,0),(1-y,0),(0,x),(1,y),(1-z,z),(z,1-z),\\
 &(0,1-y),(1,1-x),(y,1),(1-x,1)).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Let $\delta(x,y,z)$ be the least unsigned area among its $\binom{10}{3}=120$
triangles.  Repetitions are allowed in this parametrized statement and then
give $\delta=0$.

Let $z_0$ denote the root in $(3/10,1/3)$ of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic}
 f(z)=12z^3-27z^2+20z-4,
\end{equation}
and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 x_0=\frac{z_0}{2},\qquad
 y_0=(1-z_0)(1-2z_0),\qquad
 A_0=\frac{5z_0^2-4z_0^3}{8}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The full Comellas--Yebra parameter cube has exact optimum
\[
 \max_{(x,y,z)\in[0,1]^3}\delta(x,y,z)=A_0.
\]
The number $A_0$ is the unique real root of
\[
 3456A^3-1764A^2+268A-9=0.
\]
The equality triples are exactly
\[
 (x_0,y_0,z_0),\quad (1-y_0,1-x_0,z_0),\quad
 (x_0,y_0,1-z_0),\quad (1-y_0,1-x_0,1-z_0).
\]
\end{theorem}

The theorem gives a sharp result for an infinite family, not the unrestricted
identity $\Delta_{10}=A_0$.

\section{Exact preliminaries and normalization}

On $[0,1/2]$, the derivative
$f'(z)=36z^2-54z+20$ is positive.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-bracket}
 f(31/100)=-4651/125000<0,
 \qquad f(8/25)=444/15625>0.
\end{equation}
Thus $31/100<z_0<8/25$.  For the doubled target
\[
 t_0=2A_0=\frac{5z_0^2-4z_0^3}{4},
\]
monotonicity of $(5z^2-4z^3)/4$ on $[0,1/2]$ and evaluation at the endpoints
in~\eqref{eq:root-bracket} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t-bracket}
 \frac9{100}<t_0<\frac1{10}.
\end{equation}

Two elementary point-multiset identities normalize the entire cube:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:involutions}
 \CY(x,y,z)=\CY(1-y,1-x,z),\qquad
 \CY(x,y,z)=\CY(x,y,1-z).
\end{equation}
The first exchanges four boundary pairs; the second exchanges the two
nonboundary points.  Hence it suffices to consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized}
 0\le z\le\frac12,
 \qquad x+y\le1.
\end{equation}
We cover this normalized cell by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split}
 x\ge y;\qquad x\le y\le z;\qquad
 x\le y\ \text{and}\ z\le y.
\end{equation}

\section{The ordered cell}

Assume $0\le x\le y\le z\le1/2$.  Direct determinants of triangles
$012$, $145$, and $058$ give the nonnegative doubled areas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PQR}
 P=x(1-x-y),\qquad Q=y(1-2z),\qquad
 R=z(1-x+y)-y.
\end{equation}
Here \(R\ge z(1-x+z)-z=z(z-x)\ge0\); the other two signs are immediate.
Put $t=\min(P,Q,R)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ordered}
On the ordered cell, $t\le t_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $t\ge t_0$; then $0<z<1/2$.  Write
\[
 d=1-2z,
 \qquad L=\frac{t}{d}.
\]
The inequality $Q\ge t$ gives $L\le y$.  Lowering $y$ to $L$ increases both
$P$ and $R$, so $P(x,L)\ge t$ and $R(x,L,z)\ge t$.  The latter yields
\[
 x\le U=1-\frac{t+(1-z)L}{z}.
\]
The vertex of $P(x,L)=x(1-x-L)$ is $(1-L)/2$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertex}
 \frac{1-L}{2}-U=
 \frac{N}{2z(1-2z)},\qquad N=t(4-7z)+2z^2-z.
\end{equation}
Because $4-7z>0$ and $t\ge t_0$,
\[
 N\ge 2z^2-(1+7t_0)z+4t_0.
\]
The discriminant of the quadratic on the right satisfies, by
\eqref{eq:t-bracket},
\[
49t_0^2-18t_0+1
<49(1/10)^2-18(9/100)+1=-13/100<0.
\]
Thus $U$ lies strictly before the vertex.  From $x\le U$ and
$P(x,L)\ge t$, monotonicity up to the vertex gives $P(U,L)\ge t$.
Direct simplification gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 P(U,L)-t=\frac{tH}{z^2(1-2z)},\quad
 H=(6z-4)t+2z^3-5z^2+2z.
\end{equation}
Consequently $H\ge0$.  Since $6z-4<0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 t\le h(z):=\frac{2z^3-5z^2+2z}{4-6z}.
\end{equation}
Finally,
\[
 h'(z)=-\frac{f(z)}{2(3z-2)^2}.
\]
The polynomial $f$ is strictly increasing and changes sign once on
$[0,1/2]$, so $h$ has its unique maximum at $z_0$.  Reduction using
$f(z_0)=0$ gives $h(z_0)=t_0$.
\end{proof}

The equality chain in the proof is rigid.  Equality in the envelope forces
$z=z_0$; the strict vertex inequality in~\eqref{eq:vertex} then forces
$x=U$, and strict decrease under the $y$ substitution forces $y=L$.
Reducing by~\eqref{eq:cubic} gives $x=x_0$ and $y=y_0$.

\section{The cell \texorpdfstring{\(x\ge y\)}{x greater than or equal to y}}

Under~\eqref{eq:normalized} and $x\ge y$, both $y$ and $z$ lie in
$[0,1/2]$.  Triangles $145$, $014$, and $149$ have doubled areas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-witnesses}
 q=y(1-2z),\qquad w=z(1-x-y),\qquad |q-w|.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:reverse-order}
This cell contains a triangle of area at most $9/200<A_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $T=9/100$.  If all three quantities in
\eqref{eq:product-witnesses} exceeded $T$, then one of $q,w$ would exceed
$2T$, and hence $qw>2T^2$.  On the other hand,
\[
 qw=yz(1-2z)(1-x-y)
 \le y(1-2y)z(1-2z)\le\frac1{64},
\]
because $u(1-2u)\le1/8$ for $u\in[0,1/2]$.  This contradicts
$2T^2=81/5000>1/64$.  Divide the determinant threshold by two and use
\eqref{eq:t-bracket}.
\end{proof}

\section{The remaining cell and its exact certificate}

The last cell is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
D=\{(x,y,z):0\le x,y\le1,\ 0\le z\le1/2,
x\le y,\ z\le y,\ x+y\le1\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact dyadic cover]\label{prop:cover}
For every $(x,y,z)\in D$, one of the 120 triangle determinants of
$\CY(x,y,z)$ has absolute value at most $9/100$.  Hence
$\delta(x,y,z)\le9/200<A_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The serialized proof tree starts with
$[0,1]\times[0,1]\times[0,1/2]$ and repeatedly bisects a normalized widest
coordinate.  Its leaf data are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:cover}.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Exact finite-cover statistics.}\label{tab:cover}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Quantity & Count\\
\midrule
Binary-tree nodes & 2,893\\
Leaves & 1,447\\
Triangle-witness leaves & 1,169\\
Domain-infeasible leaves & 278\\
Maximum depth & 20\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

At a domain-infeasible leaf, one of
\[
 \underline x>\overline y,
 \qquad \underline z>\overline y,
 \qquad \underline x+\underline y>1
\]
holds.  Every other leaf names a literal triangle whose signed determinant is
bounded in $[-9/100,9/100]$ by exact rational arithmetic.  Completeness of the
binary prefix tree proves that these leaves cover the root box.

An independent checker reconstructs every box from its path, re-derives the
named determinant from the shoelace formula, changes to local box coordinates,
and bounds it by the minimum and maximum of its exact tensor-product Bernstein
coefficients.  Thus the checker uses neither the generator's determinant
decomposition nor its termwise interval evaluator.  The commands and files
are given in Section~\ref{sec:repro}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The involutions~\eqref{eq:involutions} reduce the cube to
\eqref{eq:normalized}.  The three cells in~\eqref{eq:split} are covered by
Lemma~\ref{lem:reverse-order}, Lemma~\ref{lem:ordered}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}.  Since $P,Q,R$ are actual doubled triangle
areas, Lemma~\ref{lem:ordered} gives $\delta\le A_0$ there, while the other
two cells have the strict bound $9/200<A_0$.

At $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$, exact enumeration of all 120 determinants gives minimum
$A_0$, attained by 16 triples.  Hence the bound is sharp.  Equality must
normalize to the unique ordered equality point; undoing the two involutions
gives precisely the four listed parameter triples.  Eliminating $z_0$ from
\eqref{eq:cubic} and~\eqref{eq:target} gives the displayed cubic for $A_0$.
Its discriminant is negative, so it has only one real root.
\end{proof}

\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}

The ancillary archive contains the exact input, proof tree, generator, two
independent incumbent verifiers, the analytic-identity audit, and the
independent cover checker.  From the archive root, run
\begin{verbatim}
python3 certificates/verify_incumbent_stdlib.py
python3 certificates/verify_incumbent_sympy.py
python3 certificates/verify_analytic_identities.py
python3 certificates/verify_family_cover.py
\end{verbatim}
All four commands print a compact record with status \texttt{PASS}.  The
cover can be regenerated deterministically by
\begin{verbatim}
python3 certificates/generate_family_cover.py \
  certificates/family_cover.json
\end{verbatim}
The standard-library incumbent checker works in the cubic number field using
rational root intervals.  The second incumbent checker uses SymPy and the
shoelace formula.  The cover generator and cover checker require only the
Python standard library.  The reported clean run used CPython~3.10.12 and
SymPy~1.14.0.

A separate 340-start floating-point search probed uniform, five-boundary,
eight-boundary, and scale-separated asymmetric starts.  It found no value
above the incumbent.  This experiment is a diagnostic and is not used in any
proof.

\section{Limitations and disclosure}

No theorem in this paper reduces an arbitrary ten-point configuration to
\eqref{eq:family}.  The exact certificate covers three parameters, whereas the
unrestricted problem has twenty coordinates before symmetries.  Consequently
this paper neither proves a matching unrestricted upper bound nor determines
$\Delta_{10}$.

Computer algebra and custom exact programs were used for symbolic
cross-checks and code development.  The mathematical claims are supported by
the displayed proof and independently executable exact checkers; the author
reviewed the sources, scope, code, and final manuscript and takes
responsibility for the result.
